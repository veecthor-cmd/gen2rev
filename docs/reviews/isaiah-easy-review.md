## 3. Contested-Territory Cross-Check

### 3.1 Deliberate exclusions (Isaiah 7:14, 9:6-7, Isaiah 53) — reconfirmed absent

Directly checked every reference cited in `docs/content/isaiah-easy.md`: all twelve items draw from
Isaiah 1, 2, 6, 12, 38, 40, 41, or 55. None draws from Isaiah 7, 9, or 53. This reviewer independently
re-derives, not merely re-accepts, the medium-tier finding that no narrowing technique closes the gap
for these three passages (see `docs/reviews/isaiah-review.md` §3.1-3.3 for the full sourced
reasoning — Jewish/Christian divergence on *almah* vs. *parthenos* at 7:14, on whether "Mighty God,
Everlasting Father" names a human king or asserts divine identity at 9:6-7, and on the identity of the
suffering servant at ch. 53). Nothing in this easy-tier authoring pass introduced any pressure to touch
this material — Isaiah's other 8 passages provide more than enough iconic, uncontested content for a
12-item easy tier. **Exclusion confirmed to hold.**

### 3.2 Isaiah 2:4 (item 3) — bounding confirmed to hold

Item 3 grades only the "swords into plowshares" narrative image, not the "latter days" timing or "the
mountain of Yahweh's house" referent question `docs/reviews/isaiah-review.md` §3.4 confirmed as a live
Jewish/Christian divergence. No challenge item in this brief asks about timing or referent. **Bounding
holds, no change needed.**

### 3.3 Isaiah 6:1, 6:3, 6:8, and the boss sequence (items 4, 5, 11) — bounding confirmed to hold

None of these items ask who or what Isaiah saw beyond the text's own words ("the Lord sitting on a
throne") — the confirmed 6:1 identity question (John 12:41's Christological reading vs. Jewish
tradition's unidentified theophany, `docs/reviews/isaiah-review.md` §3.5) isn't engaged by any item
here, which grade only the seraphim's cry, Isaiah's response, and the coal. The boss sequence's
narrative captions ("Isaiah sees the Lord seated high on a throne") describe only what the text
narrates, with no identity claim beyond it. **Bounding holds, no change needed.**

### 3.4 Isaiah 38:1 (item 7) — bounding confirmed to hold

Item 7 asks only who comes to Hezekiah and what he is told, staying inside Isaiah 38's own wording. No
comparison to or claim about 2 Kings 20 or 2 Chronicles 32:24-26 is made — same scope-only bounding
`docs/reviews/isaiah-review.md` §3.6 already confirmed for this passage. **Bounding holds.**

### 3.5 Isaiah 40-55 authorship (items 6, 8, 9, 10, 12) — bounding confirmed to hold

No item drawing from chapters 40, 41, or 55 makes or implies any authorship claim; every item cites its
passage simply as "Isaiah," book-chapter-verse. Same no-authorship-claim framing
`docs/reviews/isaiah-review.md` §3.7 confirmed for `medium`. **Bounding holds.**

### 3.6 Newly-isolated clauses (items 2, 6, 10, 12) — independently checked, no new contested ground found

Isaiah 1:17's "defend the fatherless," 38:1's narrative content taken as a whole, 55:6's "seek Yahweh
while he may be found," and 55:9's "my ways are higher than your ways" were each independently checked
for any cross-tradition disagreement not already catalogued for their parent passages. None found —
these are plain social-exhortation, narrative, and transcendence-affirming statements in near-universal
use across traditions, consistent with `docs/reviews/isaiah-review.md`'s existing characterization of
Isaiah 1, 38, and 55 as low-risk material. **No new restriction needed.**
