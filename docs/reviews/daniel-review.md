## 3. Contested-Territory Cross-Check

### Chapters 7-12 as a whole (the brief's central call)

This is the item the assignment specifically asked to be verified rather than taken on the brief's
word, and the one `GEN2REV_MASTER_PROMPT.md` §2.4 named by name before any book was built. Sources
consulted, spanning multiple traditions:

- **Jewish tradition:** [Jews for Judaism, "Daniel 9 - A True Biblical Interpretation"](https://jewsforjudaism.org/knowledge/articles/daniel-9-a-true-biblical-interpretation/)
  and [Aish, "The Seventy Weeks of Daniel 9"](https://aish.com/the-seventy-weeks-of-daniel-9/) both
  reject the Christian messianic identification of the "anointed one" in 9:24-27 with Jesus, reading
  the passage instead as referring to one or more historical figures from the Second Temple/Maccabean
  period (e.g. the high priest Onias), and dispute among themselves exactly how many "anointed ones"
  the text names and who they are.
- **Catholic tradition:** [Catholic Answers Encyclopedia, "Book of Daniel"](https://www.catholic.com/encyclopedia/book-of-daniel)
  and the [USCCB's own study notes on Daniel ch. 9](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/daniel/9) show
  Catholic scholarship itself split between a typological/messianic reading and a literal-historical
  reading tied to the Antiochus IV persecution — i.e., the disagreement isn't only cross-tradition,
  it runs inside Catholic scholarship too.
- **Orthodox and mainstream Protestant tradition:** read the seventy weeks messianically (citing
  patristic writers like Hippolytus) but fracture internally on chronology — dispensationalist,
  covenantal/futurist, and preterist frameworks each place the prophecy's fulfillment in different
  centuries, using different starting decrees and different identifications of the "prince to come."
  [City Reformed, "Four Interpretations of the '70 weeks' in Daniel 9:24-27"](https://cityreformed.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/views_of_the_70_weeks_of_daniel.pdf)
  documents four genuinely non-converging mainstream Christian readings side by side.
- **Chapter 7's four beasts / "little horn":** mainstream evangelical commentary (e.g.
  [enduringword.com, Daniel 7](https://enduringword.com/bible-commentary/daniel-7/)) identifies the
  fourth beast as Rome with an end-times Antichrist, but notes this is disputed even among
  conservative scholars (some read the fourth beast as Greece), and is rejected outright by
  historical-critical scholarship, which reads the "little horn" as Antiochus IV Epiphanes — a
  figure from the book's own past, not a future one.

**Finding: this is a genuine, live, unresolved disagreement, both across traditions and within
individual traditions** (Catholic and Protestant scholarship each internally split, not just
disagreeing with each other). This is a different — and in a sense clearer-cut — case than Genesis
22 or Genesis 3:15, where the narrative content was uncontested and only the *meaning* was disputed,
which let those items be safely bounded to narrative-only recall. Chapters 7-12 don't offer an
equivalent narrative floor: the content itself is symbolic vision material (a ram with two horns, a
composite beast, a figure "like a son of man") that has no independent "what happened" separable
from "what it means" — there's no verse to recall that isn't already an interpretive claim in
miniature once you ask what any image *is*. The brief's conclusion — exclude the whole span, don't
attempt narrow bounding — is the correct call, not an overcautious one. **Confirmed: no change
needed; the brief's reasoning holds up against independent sourcing and is, if anything,
under-stated rather than overstated.**

### Daniel 2, the dream's kingdom-identification

Confirmed as a genuine, narrower version of the same fault line found in chapter 7 (see above) —
which historical kingdom the dream's parts represent varies by tradition and even within
traditions. **The brief's bounding holds**: the structural rule restricting challenge items to "God
revealed the dream to Daniel" and "Daniel interpreted it," never "kingdom X is represented by part
Y," is sufficient and was already applied during drafting, matching the same technique that worked
for Genesis 1's creation-days item and the Flood's scope. No change needed.

### Daniel 4, Nebuchadnezzar's madness

Not a cross-tradition doctrinal dispute — reviewed instead against the content-integrity and

1:18-19. The brief already excludes the most graphic physical-description verse (4:33) from
challenge-ready text and frames the scene around the boast → pronouncement → restoration arc rather
than the middle of the ordeal itself. **Bounding holds; no change needed.**

Confirmed as uncontested narrative fact across the traditions checked — no source found treating

for what follows. **No change needed.**
