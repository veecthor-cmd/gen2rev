## 3. Contested-Territory Cross-Check

This tier introduces no new scenes. Six items reuse already-scoped verse text with new blanks;
three items (1:18, 28:1, 39:14) quote verses that sit inside already-approved scenes but were not
individually cited at medium. This review's job is to confirm the medium brief's Contested
Territory rulings (`docs/reviews/jeremiah-review.md` §3) still hold, and to independently check the
three newly-quoted verses for anything the medium review didn't have occasion to consider.

- **Jeremiah 1:5, the womb/personhood question.** Item 1 is recall/fill-in-blank, blank on "womb"
  instead of `easy`'s "nations" — still no multiple-choice framing. **Rule honored, confirmed.**
- **Jeremiah 1:18, newly quoted.** Read in full context: this is Yahweh's promise of Jeremiah's own
  resilience against opposition ("a fortified city... against the kings of Judah, against its
  princes, against its priests, and against the people of the land"), immediately preceding the
  already-approved 1:19. No cross-tradition dispute found over this verse's content — it is a
  call-narrative encouragement with no doctrinal claim beyond the narrative itself. **No new
  contested ground.**
- **Jeremiah 7, the Temple Sermon — no NT cross-reference.** Items 3 (7:9-11) and 4 (7:12) contain
  no reference to Matthew 21:13, Mark 11:17, or Luke 19:46. **Rule honored, confirmed.**
- **Jeremiah 28, testing true and false prophecy, and the newly-quoted 28:1.** Item 6 (28:1) is
  Hananiah's own scene-setting introduction — his name, patronymic, and hometown — confirmed via
  Bible Hub's aggregated commentary (the same source class the medium review already used for this
  chapter) that this verse is read uniformly as scene-setting narrative detail, with commentators'
  only disagreement (Gibeon's status as a Levitical/priestly town, discussed by the Pulpit
  Commentary) bearing on background color, not on anything this item grades. [Source: Bible Hub
  commentary aggregation on Jeremiah 28:1](https://biblehub.com/commentaries/jeremiah/28-1.htm).
  **No new contested ground**, and no item in this tier extends into whether prophecy continues
  today, matching the medium brief's bounding.
- **Jeremiah 37-38, the cistern imprisonment.** Item 7 (38:9) grades only the word "dungeon" —
  narrower than the medium brief's own approved 38:9-10 citation, which already includes the
  "likely to die... because of the famine" clause this item's un-blanked text still displays. No
  challenge-ready verse in this tier describes the mud (38:6, never cited in any tier of this
  book). **Rule honored, confirmed.**
- **Jeremiah 39:6-7, Zedekiah's sons killed, his eyes put out.** Verified by re-reading every item
  above against this exclusion range — items 8 (39:14) and 12 (39:17-18) both fall outside 39:6-7.
  **Exclusion confirmed, no overlap found.**
- **Jeremiah 39:14, newly quoted — Gedaliah.** Confirmed this verse is plain narrative
  continuation of Nebuchadnezzar's protective order (already approved at 39:11-12), naming who
  Jeremiah was released into the custody of. Gedaliah's subsequent governorship and assassination
  (Jeremiah 40-41) are outside this book's approved scene list in every tier and are not referenced
  here. **No new contested ground.**
- **Jeremiah 31:31-34, the New Covenant.** Item 9 (31:34) is recall/fill-in-blank, blank on
  "greatest" — a different clause than medium's already-quoted material but still purely reciting
  the verse's own words, never the addressee clause, never multiple-choice. **Rule honored,
  confirmed** — the deep Christian/Jewish divergence the medium review documented remains fully
  neutralized by the unchanged format restriction.
