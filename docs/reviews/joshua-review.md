## 3. Contested-Territory Cross-Check

### Ḥerem / the conquest as "genocide" vs. "just war"

This is the item the brief itself flags as its central concern, and the one the assignment
specifically asked to be checked for whether it can be neutrally bounded. Sources consulted,
representing different traditions:

- **Jewish tradition/scholarship:** [My Jewish Learning, "Conquering Canaan"](https://www.myjewishlearning.com/article/conquering-canaan/)
  states plainly that the book of Joshua "does not make a moral case for the conquest" — the only
  recorded sins in the land are Israel's own — and that the stated rationale for destroying the
  Canaanites is religious (their worship of other gods), not a response to any wrong the text
  attributes to them. It reports that terms including "war crime" and "ethnic cleansing" appear in
  serious discussion of the ḥerem law, not just hostile external criticism.
- **Critical/historical-critical Jewish-adjacent scholarship:** [TheTorah.com, "Deuteronomy's Herem
  Law: Protecting Israel at the Cost of its Humanity"](https://www.thetorah.com/article/deuteronomys-herem-law-protecting-israel-at-the-cost-of-its-humanity)
  — title alone signals the framing: even scholarship sympathetic to the text's own internal logic
  concludes the law comes at a real ethical cost.
  - **Historicity note relevant to the same debate:** [WebSearch summary citing scholarly consensus](https://www.thetorah.com/article/deuteronomys-herem-law-protecting-israel-at-the-cost-of-its-humanity)
    also surfaced that "most scholars who have studied the history of that region conclude that the
    biblical accounts of extermination are exaggerated, fictional, or metaphorical" — relevant to
    the historicity item, not the ethics item, but shows the two debates are entangled in the
    literature.
- **Evangelical apologetics (mainstream, not fringe):** [The Gospel Coalition/Themelios, "Helpful
  Distinction or Quarrel over Words? The Conquest as 'Genocide' in Evangelical Apologetics"](https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/themelios/article/helpful-distinction-or-quarrel-over-words-the-conquest-as-genocide-in-evangelical-apologetics/)
  documents that evangelical scholars themselves are not unified — positions range from treating
  "genocide" as an accurate and troubling description requiring a theological answer, to arguing
  the destruction language is ancient Near Eastern hyperbolic war rhetoric not meant literally
  ("kill all that breathes" as stock phrasing, not a body count), to treating the ḥerem command as
  a unique, non-repeatable act tied to that specific land and moment. [NAMB Apologetics, "Joshua's
  Conquest: Was It Justified?"](https://www.namb.net/apologetics/resource/joshua-s-conquest-was-it-justified/)
  frames the question in exactly the just-war terms the assignment flagged, confirming this framing
  is active in mainstream (not fringe) Christian apologetics, not a strawman.
- **Academic/mixed:** [Yale Bible Study, "Joshua and Judges: The Moral Problem of Conquest"](https://yalebiblestudy.org/courses/joshua-and-judges/lessons/the-moral-problem-of-conquest-study-guide/)
  treats "the moral problem of conquest" as a standing, named subject of study, not a fringe
  concern.

**Finding: this is a genuine, live, unresolved disagreement**, both across traditions (Jewish
scholarship naming it a real moral problem; Christian apologetics internally split between several
non-converging positions) and within traditions (evangelical scholars disagreeing with each other).
It is not a case like Genesis 22 or Genesis 3:15, where the underlying narrative-only bounding was
judged sufficient because the *interpretive* disagreement (why/what it means) was cleanly separable
from the *narrative* content (what happened). Here, several of the "what happened" framings
themselves are contested — e.g., whether "utterly destroyed all that breathed" describes something
that occurred as narrated, or is hyperbolic war rhetoric not meant as a literal report, is itself
part of the live scholarly disagreement, not a settled fact the challenge content can safely rest
on. Excluding the destruction-language verses from challenge-ready content (as the brief already
does) removes the *graded-item* risk, but does not fully resolve whether Scene 4 belongs in the
brief's core rotation at all, given the target audience includes children.

**This reviewer is not confident the brief's exclusion-based bounding is sufficient on its own**,
for two reasons distinct from the graded-content question: (1) the scene description itself, even
without a challenge item, narrates that cities' populations were destroyed as divine command,
which is different in kind from Genesis 22's "a test was given and stopped" — there is no narrative
off-ramp here comparable to the ram substituted for Isaac; (2) this is exactly the scenario
`.claude/agents/theological-reviewer.md` step 7(b) names: content intersecting the child-safety
guardrail in a way this reviewer isn't fully confident adjudicating alone.

### Historicity of the conquest
Confirmed as a genuine, live scholarly debate (rapid-conquest vs. gradual-settlement models),

## 4. Verdict

**`escalate-to-human`** — specifically and only for Scene 4 (The Conquest Campaigns, Joshua 10-11)
and the excluded-but-discussed destruction language in Scene 3 (6:17, 6:21). This is not a rejection
of the brief as a whole.

**Reasoning:** The ḥerem/conquest-ethics question is a genuine, unresolved, cross-tradition and
even intra-tradition disagreement, directly bearing on content whose target audience includes
children, and touching the child-safety guardrail named in `GEN2REV_MASTER_PROMPT.md` Section 5.
Per `.claude/agents/theological-reviewer.md` step 7, this combination — live disagreement the
brief's own narrative-only/exclusion-based bounding may not fully neutralize, plus a child-safety
dimension — is exactly the pairing that should trigger escalation rather than a forced approval.
This reviewer attempted the same bounding technique that worked for Genesis 22 and Genesis 3:15
(exclude or restrict the sensitive verse to a narrow format) and does not have the same confidence
it fully closes the gap here, because part of what's contested is whether the narrative content
itself ("what happened") can be presented as settled fact at all.

**Recommended interim path (not a substitute for the escalation):**
- Scenes 1, 2, 3 (using only the non-excluded verses: 3:3-5, 15-17, 20, 25), and 5 are **not**
  blocked by this escalation — their bounding is sound by the same reasoning that passed Genesis's
  comparable items, and they can proceed toward `approved` status independently.
- Scene 4 (The Conquest Campaigns) and the excluded-verse framing in Scene 3 should remain out of
  the boss-battle rotation and out of ingestion until a human reviewer resolves the escalation, as
  the brief's own Acceptance Criteria already state.
- If no named human theological reviewer becomes available (see Known Limitation below), the
  product-owner-level fallback should be an explicit decision to either (a) drop Scene 4 from the
  MVP brief entirely and note that as a scope decision in `docs/CONTENT_REVIEW_LOG.md`, or (b) accept
  the brief's exclusion-based bounding as sufficient and formally override this escalation — either
  is a legitimate outcome, but it should be a deliberate decision, not a default.

**Known limitation, stated per the agent definition's own instruction:** as of this review, there is
no named human theological reviewer in `GEN2REV_MASTER_PROMPT.md` Open Decision #7. This escalation
has nowhere to land yet. The calling session should surface this to Kachi explicitly rather than
treat the brief as blocked-and-forgotten.
