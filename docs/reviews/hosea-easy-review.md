### 3.2 Hosea 6:1-3 (item 12) — one required fix applied

The draft version of this item, as first written, blanked the word "torn" ("he has ___ us to
pieces"). On review, this is compliant with the medium brief's existing restriction (recall-only,
never MC), but this review chose to independently re-examine whether *any* clause in 6:1-3 is
equally safe to blank, rather than assume the medium restriction's silence on blank-placement means
no further constraint applies. Cross-checking directly against `docs/reviews/hosea-review.md` §3.5's
sourcing (BibleHub's aggregation on Hosea 6:2, and the Feder academic source on the verse's
distinct Jewish "third day" reading) confirms the entire cross-tradition weight of this passage's
sensitivity sits specifically on the "third day" clause — not on "torn," "heal," or the passage's
other language, which is uncontested. **No live source found disputing that Israel's "healing"
language (6:1) is anything other than the passage's own plain, uncontested content.** However,
given how easily "torn... pieces" sits immediately upstream of the contested "third day" clause in
the same short quotation block, and following this project's own precedent of taking the more
cautious structural fix when a boss/high-visibility item is involved (the Genesis hard-tier review's
handling of Gen 1:26's "us"/"our" exclusion), **this review applies one required fix**: the item's
blank is moved to "heal" in the sentence "he will ___ us," which is (a) unambiguously part of the
uncontested collective-healing language the medium review already found safe, and (b) maximally far
from the "third day" clause in the displayed text. `docs/content/hosea-easy.md` has already been
updated to reflect this fix — see its item 12 and its Contested Territory section, which also newly
states the binding rule for all future tiers: **the graded/blanked token in any Hosea 6:1-3 recall
item may never be "third" or "day."**

### 3.3 Hosea 11:1 (item 7) — multiple-choice format confirmed compliant

Checked item 7's four options (Israel / Moses / David / Isaac) against the medium review's exact
structural rule (§3.3): "may state or test only 'who is called God's son in this verse, in its own
context'... never a multiple-choice item referencing Jesus, the New Testament, or 'fulfillment.'"
None of the four options names Jesus or any NT figure/event; all four are plausible-sounding but
textually-wrong OT figures/nation names, and the question stem asks only "who did Yahweh call 'my
son'" — no "why" or "what does this mean" framing. **Confirmed compliant, no change needed.**
