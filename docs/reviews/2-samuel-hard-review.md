## 4. Contested-Territory Cross-Check

Each item the brief flagged was checked against live sources representing genuinely different
traditions. I did not accept any proposed bounding at face value.

### 4.1 — 2 Sam 6:17, burnt offerings and peace offerings. **Rule CONFIRMED, and needed for a stronger reason than the brief gives.**

The brief's rationale is that the item doesn't require affirming what the offerings *mean*. That is
correct as far as it goes, but the cross-tradition check surfaced a second, more concrete reason the
rule is necessary — **the offering terms are themselves a live translation divergence:**

- **WEB / KJV tradition** — "burnt offerings and **peace offerings**"
  ([ebible.org 2SA06](https://ebible.org/web/2SA06.htm))
- **Jewish (Revised JPS 2023, via Sefaria)** — "burnt offerings and **offerings of well-being**"
  ([Sefaria, II Samuel 6:17](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/II_Samuel.6.17?context=0))
- Contemporary renderings elsewhere in the same family diverge further still ("fellowship
  offerings," "communion sacrifices"), reflecting genuine disagreement about how to render Hebrew
  *shelamim*.

So an item blanking "peace offerings" would not merely brush against atonement theology — it would
have a defensible alternative answer drawn from a mainstream translation, i.e. an answer-key defect
of exactly the `genesis-easy-review.md` §6.1 (Gen 8:11) type. **The proposed rule holds and should
be kept, with this rationale added.**

On interpretation, classic commentators are largely convergent — thanksgiving, consecration,
communal feasting — with Matthew Henry alone extending Christologically ("all our joy in God must be
connected with repentance and faith in the Redeemer's atoning blood")
([BibleHub commentaries, 2 Sam 6:17](https://biblehub.com/commentaries/2_samuel/6-17.htm)). The
graded token is "Yahweh," which every tradition renders as the recipient of the offerings. **Bounding
holds.**

**One item the brief did not flag:** the same commentary aggregation notes that commentators address
how David — who held no priestly office — can be said to have "offered" the sacrifices, harmonizing
it as *commissioning* rather than personally performing them. That is a live interpretive question
about the priestly/royal boundary. It does not touch item 3 (blank on "Yahweh"), but a future item
of the shape "who offered the burnt offerings?" → "David" would land directly on it. Folded into
Required Change 1's rule extension below.

### 4.2 — 2 Sam 18:9, "head" vs "hair". **Rule CONFIRMED as necessary — but the brief's stated rationale is factually overstated and must be corrected.**

I verified the WEB text myself, as requested. WEB reads "**his head** caught hold of the oak"
([ebible.org 2SA18](https://ebible.org/web/2SA18.htm), confirmed against
[BibleGateway WEB](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2+Samuel+5%3A10%3B+2+Samuel+5%3A12%3B+2+Samuel+6%3A17%3B+2+Samuel+7%3A9%3B+2+Samuel+15%3A30%3B+2+Samuel+18%3A9&version=WEB)).
The word "hair" does not appear anywhere in WEB's 2 Samuel 18. The Hebrew is רֹאשׁוֹ (*rosho*), and
Strong's H7218 lists **no** sense meaning "hair"
([Blue Letter Bible H7218](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/h7218/kjv/wlc/0-1/)).

**So the drafter is right that the distinction is real and worth enforcing. But the brief describes
the "hair" reading as coming from "popular retellings and artistic depictions (following Josephus,
not the biblical text)" — and that is not accurate.** The hair rendering is carried by mainstream
Bible translations across at least three traditions:

- **Jewish** — Revised JPS 2023: "**his hair** got caught in the terebinth"
  ([Sefaria, II Samuel 18:9](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/II_Samuel.18.9?context=0))
- **Catholic** — NABRE: "**his hair** caught fast in the tree"
  ([BibleGateway NABRE](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2+Samuel+18%3A9&version=NABRE))
- **Evangelical** — NIV and NLT both read "hair"
  ([BibleHub parallel, 2 Sam 18:9](https://biblehub.com/2_samuel/18-9.htm))

Against: WEB, ESV, NASB, CSB, BSB, **Douay-Rheims** ("his head stuck in the oak") and **Brenton's
Septuagint** ("his head was entangled in the oak") — so the older Catholic and the Orthodox-relevant
Greek textual traditions both read "head," making this a divergence *within* traditions as well as
across them.

Classic commentators split correspondingly
([BibleHub commentaries, 2 Sam 18:9](https://biblehub.com/commentaries/2_samuel/18-9.htm)):
Ellicott rejects the hair reading and names Josephus as its origin ("There is nothing said to
support the common idea (which seems to have originated with Josephus), that he hung by his long
hair"); Benson favours hair; Barnes treats the hair as contributory; the Cambridge Bible hedges;
Keil and Delitzsch say the text doesn't settle it; the Pulpit Commentary says "apparently it was his
neck which became fixed."

**Consequence for the rule.** The brief's proposed rule bars only *one direction* — asserting hair.
That is insufficient. Because "hair" is the reading of NABRE, NIV, NLT and RJPS, an item whose
answer key is "head" with "hair" as a distractor would mark wrong a player reasoning from a
mainstream Bible — the same defect class as Gen 8:11. **The rule must bar grading head-vs-hair in
either direction.** See Required Change 1.

**Item 12 as written is unaffected:** the graded token is "earth," and every rendering consulted
(WEB "between the sky and earth"; RJPS and NABRE "between heaven and earth") ends on "earth."
**Bounding holds for the current item.**

### 4.3 — 2 Sam 5:12, "Yahweh had established him king". **Drafter's "no rule needed" CONFIRMED.**

Checked across traditions and found genuinely convergent:

- **Jewish (Revised JPS 2023)** — "Thus David knew that GOD had **established** him as king over
  Israel and had exalted his kingship for the sake of Israel—God's people"
  ([Sefaria, II Samuel 5:12](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/II_Samuel.5.12?context=0)). The
  graded token "established" is the same word, in the same clause, in the Jewish rendering.
- **Classic Protestant commentary** — unanimous, with no interpretive dispute; the consistent
  emphasis is that David recognized the kingship was given *for Israel's sake* rather than his own
  (Ellicott: "David's prosperity had not blinded him to the fact that his blessings came to him as
  the head of the theocracy, and for the sake of God's chosen people")
  ([BibleHub commentaries, 2 Sam 5:12](https://biblehub.com/commentaries/2_samuel/5-12.htm)).

No source consulted turns this verse into a "divine right of kings" proposition; commentators read
it as David's perception, which is how the text frames it and how the brief bounds it. **The
drafter's judgment that no structural rule is needed holds, and I confirm rather than challenge it.**
The brief was still right to flag it for visibility. One optional prudential note at §5.5.

### 4.4 — 2 Sam 7:9 and the 7:13/7:16 messianic restriction. **Item is safe, but the drafter's stated basis does NOT hold. Rule extension recommended.**

The brief claims 7:9 has "no cross-tradition fulfillment dispute attached to it in any source
consulted." That claim does not survive checking:

- The **Pulpit Commentary** applies a typological reading directly to this verse's content: David's
  conquests "were not for the sake of mere earthly dominion. It was, first of all, a **type of
  Messiah's reign**, to whom God has promised the heathen for his inheritance."
- **Matthew Henry's Concise** frames the whole oracle block 7:4–17 — which contains v9 — as relating
  to Christ, "who is often called David and the Son of David."
  ([BibleHub commentaries, 2 Sam 7:9](https://biblehub.com/commentaries/2_samuel/7-9.htm))

So a messianic/typological layer *does* attach to 7:9, contrary to the brief. The Jewish rendering
carries none of it: RJPS reads "I will give you **great renown** like that of the greatest men on
earth" ([Sefaria, II Samuel 7:9](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/II_Samuel.7.9?context=0)) — a
plain promise of fame, with no throne/dynasty language.

**Does this break item 4? No.** The disagreement concerns what David's greatness *typifies*, not
what the clause *says*; both traditions agree the verse promises David a great name/renown, and the
graded token is "name." The item is in-bounds under §2 as direct verse recall.

**But the brief's reasoning should not stand as written**, because it tells a future authoring pass
that 7:9 is clear ground when it is adjacent ground. The precedent is already set: at the easy tier,
2 Sam 7:12 was handled by *extending* the restriction rather than declaring the verse clear
(`docs/content/2-samuel-easy.md`, Contested Territory). The consistent and cheap move is the same
here. See Required Change 2.

### 4.5 — 2 Sam 15:30, David's flight up the Mount of Olives. **Item is safe, but "no new rule needed" does NOT hold. This one protects the human-escalated bounding.**

The brief says this sits inside existing Absalom bounding with nothing new. Checking the commentary
tradition shows the opposite of "uncontested" — the dominant reading of this exact verse is
**causal**, tying David's flight directly to the Bathsheba/Uriah episode
([BibleHub commentaries, 2 Sam 15:30](https://biblehub.com/commentaries/2_samuel/15-30.htm)):

- **Benson** — David is "reflecting on his own conduct in the matter of Uriah, as the cause of this
  calamity"; "Sin alone has wrought all this!"
- **Matthew Poole** — David went barefoot "in testimony of his deep sorrow, and humiliation and
  shame for his sins, whereby he had procured this evil to himself."
- **Matthew Henry's Concise** — "David's sin was ever before him."
- **Gill** adds a Christ-typology on the Mount of Olives ("where our Lord Jesus Christ, the antitype
  of David, often was, in his state of humiliation").

This matters more here than it would in another book. The medium bounding explicitly excludes "a
causal/moral claim that the rebellion was divine punishment"
(`docs/content/2-samuel.md`, Contested Territory), and an item asking *why* David wept, covered his
head, or went barefoot would not only cross that line — it would **pull chapter 11's content back
into challenge scope through the back door**, which is precisely what Kachi's 2026-08-08 sign-off
forecloses. The verse selection is compliant; the *question framing* is where the exclusion could
erode.

**Item 8 as written is safe** — it grades "barefoot," a narrated physical detail, and asks nothing
about cause. But a forward-looking rule is warranted. See Required Change 3.

## 5. Required and Recommended Changes

### 5.1 Required Change 1 — 2 Sam 18:9: correct the rationale and make the rule bidirectional

The Contested Territory bullet currently attributes the "hair" reading to Josephus and popular
retelling "not the biblical text." Replace with an accurate statement: the WEB text and the Hebrew
*rosh* (H7218) read "head," **and** the "hair" rendering is carried by mainstream translations across
traditions — NABRE (Catholic), NIV and NLT (evangelical), Revised JPS 2023 (Jewish) — while WEB, ESV,
NASB, CSB, Douay-Rheims and Brenton's LXX read "head," and classic commentators are split.

Then widen the rule to bar grading in **either** direction:

> No challenge built from 2 Samuel 18:9 may grade, assert, or imply whether it was Absalom's *head*
> or his *hair* that caught in the oak — in either direction. Specifically: no item may state that
> his hair was caught, and no item may use "his hair" as a distractor against a "head" answer key,
> since "hair" is the reading of several mainstream translations and a player relying on one would
> have textual warrant. WEB's own wording is displayed verbatim; the graded token must fall
> elsewhere in the verse (as item 12's "earth" does).

### 5.2 Required Change 2 — 2 Sam 7:9: correct the claim, and extend the recall-only rule

Strike the assertion that 7:9 carries "no cross-tradition fulfillment dispute… in any source
consulted" — the Pulpit Commentary reads David's greatness here as "a type of Messiah's reign," and
Matthew Henry's Concise frames 7:4–17 as a whole Christologically. Replace with the accurate
position: the disputed layer is what the promise *typifies*, not what the clause *says*; the Jewish
rendering ("great renown," RJPS) and the Christian renderings agree on the narrative content, which
is why grading "name" is safe.

Then extend the existing structural rule to cover 7:9, matching how 7:12 was handled at the easy
tier:

> 2 Samuel 7:9 is challenge-ready in recall/fill-in-blank format only, never multiple choice, and no
> item may ask what David's "great name" ultimately refers to or points toward.

This is a rule addition, not a loosening, and it does not affect item 4 as written.

### 5.3 Required Change 3 — 2 Sam 15:30: add the forward-looking rule the brief says isn't needed

The claim that no new rule is needed does not hold: the dominant commentary reading of this verse is
explicitly causal, tying the flight to the Uriah/Bathsheba episode (Benson, Poole, Matthew Henry).
Add:

> No challenge built from 2 Samuel 15:30 may grade *why* David wept, covered his head, or went
> barefoot, and no item may connect the flight to the events of 2 Samuel 11–12 or frame the
> rebellion as consequence or punishment. Only the narrated physical detail of the flight is
> challenge-ready. This rule also protects the human-escalated chapter-11 bounding, which a
> causal-framing item would otherwise reopen indirectly.

### 5.4 Required Change 4 — record the 2 Sam 7:9 WEB edition variance for the QA pass

Add to the brief's verbatim-text caveat: ebible.org (both `/web/` and `/engwebp/`) reads "the great
ones who are **on** the earth," while BibleGateway `version=WEB` and BibleHub's WEB read "**in** the
earth." **ebible.org is authoritative for this project**; the brief matches it and is correct. State
this explicitly so `QA_REQUIREMENTS.md` §3 does not report a false mismatch or "correct" item 4 to a
different edition. Add: no item may grade the preposition in this clause.

### 5.5 Recommended (non-blocking)

- **6:17 rule extension.** Add the translation-divergence rationale (WEB "peace offerings" vs RJPS
  "offerings of well-being" vs "fellowship"/"communion" elsewhere) as a second reason the offering
  terms may never be graded, and extend the rule to bar an item asking *who* offered the sacrifices
  — commentators divide over how David, a non-priest, "offered" them.
- **5:12 prudential note.** Optionally record that no item may ask *why* Yahweh established or
  exalted David's kingdom, or generalize to kingship-by-divine-appointment. Cross-tradition sources
  are convergent on the verse itself, so this is future-proofing, not a live problem.
- **Item 1 (5:10), answer-key robustness.** The graded token is "Armies" — WEB's distinctive
  rendering of *tseva'ot*, where nearly every other English Bible reads "hosts" (RJPS: "Hosts"). A
  player who knows the verse well from another translation may type "hosts" and be marked wrong.
  Defensible for a hard tier under a fixed-translation policy, and no change is required, but the
  grader's accepted-answer handling should be a deliberate decision rather than an accident. Same
  family as the "lamb" distractor note in `genesis-easy-review.md` §5.2.
- **Item 5 (7:16), answer-key robustness.** The blank on "sure" invites "secure" or "established"
  from players drawing on other renderings. Same category as above; noting, not blocking.
