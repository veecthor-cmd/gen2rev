## 3. Contested-Territory Cross-Check

### 3.1 The Fulfillment-Quotation Formula generally — this review's central task, per this
assignment's explicit framing

This is the genuinely new category this assignment specifically asked to be thought through
carefully, using Matthew 1:22-23 and 2:15 as the anchor cases. The brief's proposed principle is:
report that Matthew's text makes a fulfillment claim (in-bounds narrative fact about what the text
says) without grading whether the underlying OT passage "truly" refers to Jesus (out-of-bounds,
since this project has already excluded/bounded the same OT passages on their own books' briefs for
exactly this reason). This review checked that principle against the specific cases:

- **Matthew 1:22-23 / Isaiah 7:14.** Confirmed via `docs/CANON_STRUCTURE.md` §6's own explicit
  citation of this exact case ("Isaiah 7:14 excluded because of Matthew 1:23's use of it, not
  because of anything contested within Isaiah itself") that this project already made a considered
  decision on the OT side. The brief's recall-only bounding on the Matthew side is the natural,
  consistent completion of that same decision — reporting Matthew's claim as Matthew's claim,
  neither re-litigating nor silently overriding the Isaiah-side exclusion. **Finding: necessary and
  correctly scoped.**
- **Matthew 2:15 / Hosea 11:1.** Checked directly against `docs/content/hosea.md`'s own Contested
  Territory section (already reviewed and approved-with-changes in `docs/reviews/hosea-review.md`
  §3.3), which explicitly anticipated this exact cross-book question and stated its own bounding
  principle: Hosea 11:1 tests only the plain OT referent (Israel, called out of Egypt at the exodus)
  and "never makes or implies any claim about NT fulfillment." The Matthew brief's 2:15 bounding is
  the mirror-image completion — Matthew's side may state that Matthew's text applies this line to
  Jesus, but may not grade the OT passage's "true" meaning either. Independently confirmed via
  [The Gospel Coalition, "What's Christ Got to Do with Hosea 11:1? Getting a Grip on
  Typology"](https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/article/whats-christ-got-to-do-with-hosea-111-getting-a-grip-on-typology/)
  that even mainstream Christian scholarship treats Matthew's use here as typological pattern
  recognition rather than direct verbal prediction — a genuinely subtle distinction, exactly the kind
  a graded multiple-choice item could not responsibly test. **Finding: necessary, correctly scoped,
  and consistent with the already-approved Hosea brief.**
- **Matthew 2:17-18 (Jeremiah/Rachel weeping), 4:14-16 (Isaiah/Galilee of the Gentiles), 8:17
  (Isaiah 53:4 applied to physical healing), 21:4-5 (Zechariah 9:9).** Spot-checked each of these
  against their OT source material's own plain sense: 2:17-18 (Jeremiah 31:15) is about the Babylonian
  exile's grief in its own context; 4:14-16 (Isaiah 9:1-2) is about God's people restored from
  Assyrian-era darkness; 8:17 (Isaiah 53:4) is applied by Matthew to physical illness specifically,
  distinct from how 1 Peter 2:24 applies neighboring Isaiah 53 language to the atonement — itself a
  minor internal-NT variation in how the same OT chapter gets used, further supporting that these are
  each the NT text's own applications rather than the OT passages' single settled meaning; 21:4-5
  (Zechariah 9:9) is about a coming king in its own context and is the least disputed of the seven
  (near-universal agreement, across traditions, that it plausibly anticipates a coming king, though
  Jewish tradition and Christian tradition differ on whether that king is Jesus specifically). None of
  these four carry 1:22-23's or 2:15's specific doctrinal weight, but applying the same recall-only
  rule uniformly (rather than trying to rank each one's exact contestedness) is the more defensible,
  consistent policy. **Finding: the brief's uniform bounding across all seven fulfillment verses is
  reasonable and avoids an arbitrary case-by-case distinction a future challenge-authoring pass might
  not replicate correctly.**
- **Matthew 2:23, "he will be called a Nazarene."** This is a distinct sub-case worth flagging
  separately: unlike the other six, this fulfillment quotation has **no identifiable single OT source
  verse at all**. Confirmed via [Catholic Answers Q&A, "'He Shall Be Called a
  Nazarene:' Where in Old
  Testament?"](https://www.catholic.com/qa/he-shall-be-called-a-nazarene-where-in-old-testament) and
  the [Wikipedia summary, "Matthew
  2:23"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_2:23) that no such exact phrase appears anywhere in
  the Old Testament, Septuagint, Apocrypha, or Dead Sea Scrolls, and that mainstream scholarship's
  leading explanation is a Hebrew wordplay between "Nazareth" and *netser* ("branch," a messianic term
  in Isaiah 11:1) — itself explicitly described by sources as "a stretch" even among scholars
  sympathetic to it, alongside a competing "despised Messiah theme" explanation that treats it as a
  summary of a scattered prophetic motif rather than a single citation. **This is a genuine,
  additional textual/scholarly puzzle the brief's blanket "Fulfillment-Quotation Formula" framing
  does not fully capture** — it isn't primarily a doctrinal dispute about the OT passage's referent
  (there's no clear referent to dispute) but an open scholarly question about what Matthew is even
  citing. The brief's existing text does gesture at this ("an unattributed 'Nazarene' prophecy whose
  exact OT source is itself a matter of scholarly debate") but treats it as a footnote inside item 1
  rather than its own line. **Given the recall-only bounding already applied is the correct
  structural fix regardless of exactly which uncertainty is in play, this review does not require a
  brief change here** — the existing bounding already prevents any challenge item from asking "what
  OT verse is this quoting" (which sourced material confirms has no clean answer) or "was this really
  fulfilled" in either direction.

### 3.2 Soteriology and the Law (Matthew 5:17-20) — confirmed genuine, bounding holds

Confirmed via [The Gospel Coalition, "Jesus and the law: an exegesis on Matthew
5:17-20"](https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/themelios/article/jesus-and-the-law-an-exegesis-on-matthew-517-20/)
that Catholic and Protestant traditions, while sharing a common historical starting point (the
moral/civil/ceremonial division of the Law traced to Augustine), diverge on how the "surpassing
righteousness" of 5:20 relates to justification — Catholic teaching treating it as the fruit of
infused sanctifying grace, Protestant traditions generally treating righteousness before God as
forensically imputed rather than infused, with good works flowing from rather than contributing to
justification. **Finding: the brief's bounding (5:17 recall-only; 5:20 itself not selected for
challenge content) is necessary and correctly scoped.** No change needed.

### 3.3 Ecclesiology: "The Keys of the Kingdom" and "On This Rock" (Matthew 16:18-19) — confirmed
as the single most contested item in this brief, bounding holds

Confirmed via [The Gospel Coalition, "What Does 'This Rock' Refer to in Matthew
16:18?"](https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/article/what-does-this-rock-refer-to-matthew-1618/) and
[Catholic Answers, "Matthew 16 and a Parallel for
Peter"](https://www.catholic.com/magazine/online-edition/matthew-16-and-a-parallel-for-peter) that
this is a genuine three-way split with real institutional stakes (the historical basis claimed for
the papacy itself): Catholic tradition reads Peter himself as the rock, founding apostolic
succession and papal primacy; Protestant tradition splits between reading Christ himself as the rock
(via the *petros*/*petra* wordplay) or Peter's confession as the rock, explicitly rejecting an
institutional-succession reading; Orthodox tradition accepts a historical "first among equals" status
for Rome/Peter while rejecting Catholic papal supremacy specifically. This is not a settled question
with one denomination simply being idiosyncratic — it is close to evenly contested across the three
major traditions this project is instructed to weight. **Finding: the brief's bounding (recall-only,
no "what does this rock refer to" or "who holds the keys" item) is necessary and correctly scoped —
if anything, this is the item in the entire brief where a multiple-choice format would be most likely
to produce a genuinely wrong (denominationally-siding) "correct answer," so the recall-only
restriction is doing real work here.** No change needed.

### 3.4 General Church-Order Material (Matthew 18:15-20) — confirmed lower-stakes than 16:18-19,
narrative-fact bounding sufficient

Checked whether the same sharp three-way split from 16:18-19 recurs here: it does not to the same
degree. 18:15-20 describes a disciplinary *process* (private confrontation, witnesses, "tell it to
the assembly") rather than asserting a specific office or successor holds unique authority, and the
"binding and loosing" language recurs from 16:19 but is now addressed to the gathered community
generally rather than to Peter individually — itself a datapoint some Protestant commentators use to
argue 16:19's authority is not exclusively Petrine, which is precisely the kind of comparative
argument this brief correctly does not ask a player to adjudicate. **Finding: the brief's
narrative-fact bounding (steps, Peter's question, numerical answer) is sufficient; the additional
recall-only restriction used for 16:18-19 is not strictly required here but doesn't hurt — the brief
does not use 18:18 (the bind/loose repetition) as challenge content at all, which this review
confirms is the more conservative and defensible choice given its echo of 16:19.** No change needed.

### 3.5 Sacramental Theology: The Last Supper (Matthew 26:26-28) — confirmed as a five-way split,
bounding holds

Confirmed via [Catholic Answers' Bible Navigator on Matthew
26:26-28](https://www.catholic.com/bible-navigator/real-presence/matthew2626-28), the
[OPC's "Meaning of 'Real Presence' in the Lord's
Supper"](https://www.opc.org/qa.html?question_id=332), and the [Wikipedia summary of Lord's Supper
views in Reformed theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord%27s_Supper_in_Reformed_theology) that
this verse anchors five distinguishable positions across Catholic (transubstantiation), Lutheran
("in, with, and under"), Reformed/Calvinist ("spiritual real presence" received by faith), Zwinglian/
most Baptist-Evangelical (memorial/symbolic), and Orthodox (real presence, undefined
philosophically) traditions — a wider spread than almost any other item in this brief. **Finding: the
brief's bounding (institution narrative narrated and quoted verbatim; no item asks what the elements
"become" or accomplish) is necessary and correctly scoped.** No change needed.

### 3.6 Sacramental Theology: The Baptismal Formula (Matthew 28:19) — confirmed genuine, bounding
holds

Confirmed via [GotQuestions, "What does the Bible say about infant
baptism?"](https://www.gotquestions.org/infant-baptism.html) and
[OrthodoxWiki, "Infant baptism"](https://orthodoxwiki.org/Infant_baptism) that this verse is the
central anchor text for both paedobaptist (Catholic, Orthodox, Lutheran, Anglican, Presbyterian,
Reformed, Methodist) and credobaptist (Baptist, most non-denominational Evangelical) positions, plus
separate live disputes over mode (immersion vs. affusion/aspersion) and baptismal regeneration.
**Finding: the brief's bounding (recall-only for the baptismal-formula clause; no item asks what
baptism accomplishes, who may receive it, or what mode is correct) is necessary and correctly
scoped.** No change needed.

### 3.7 Atonement Theory (Matthew 26:28 and the crucifixion narrative) — confirmed genuine, bounding
holds

Checked "poured out for many for the remission of sins" against the four atonement-theory framings
`docs/CANON_STRUCTURE.md` §7 names (penal substitution, Christus Victor, moral-influence, ransom) —
confirmed this is exactly the kind of clause each theory claims as supporting text, without the verse
itself adjudicating between them. **Finding: the brief's bounding (narrated and quoted; no item asks
how or why the crucifixion accomplishes forgiveness) is necessary and correctly scoped.** No change
needed.

### 3.8 Textual-Criticism: The Doxology of the Lord's Prayer (Matthew 6:13) — a genuine additional
case this review independently surfaced with full weight, matching the Hosea review's precedent for
surfacing 6:2 beyond the draft's own framing

The draft brief already flagged this correctly and with appropriate weight (unlike the Hosea
precedent at 6:2, where the draft under-weighted a real issue this review had to strengthen — no
strengthening was needed here, the draft's framing already matched what this review found).
Confirmed via [Wikipedia, "Matthew
6:13"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_6:13) and
[KJV Today, "Is the Doxology of the Lord's Prayer in Matthew 6:13 a late
addition?"](https://sites.google.com/site/kjvtoday/home/is-the-doxology-to-the-lords-prayer-in-matthew-613-a-late-addition)
that the doxology is absent from Codex Sinaiticus and Codex Vaticanus (both 4th century, among the
earliest and most reliable witnesses), appears in the Didache (c. 130 AD) in a different, shorter
form, and circulated in at least ten variant forms before standardizing — while still appearing in
over 98% of later (Byzantine-tradition) manuscripts. Confirmed WEB actually prints the doxology (per
§1 above, matching two independent live sources), consistent with WEB's general use of the
majority/Byzantine textual tradition at contested points, as opposed to modern critical-text
translations (NIV, ESV, NRSV, NLT) which omit or footnote it. **Finding: this is a real, independent
textual-criticism case of the same kind as Mark 16:9-20/John 7:53-8:11/1 John 5:7-8, correctly
identified and correctly left in the brief as WEB actually prints it (per Open Decision #1's fixed
translation choice), with the textual note on record rather than silently resolved.** No change
needed — this is documentation, not a bounding gap, since the brief does not build any doctrinal
item around the doxology specifically (6:9-13 is used as a single recall block, testing the prayer's
text as a whole, not isolating the doxology's authenticity question).

### 3.9 Excluded Entirely: Matthew 27:24-25 — confirmed as the single highest-stakes exclusion
decision in this brief, and confirmed correct

This is the most consequential judgment call in the brief, so this review checked it with particular
care, including reading the full surrounding narrative context (27:15-26, fetched separately — see
§1) to confirm the brief's scene narration (Barabbas offered, crowd incited by chief priests, Pilate
washes his hands) is accurate without needing to quote 27:24-25 itself. Confirmed via
[CSBV Bristol, "'On us and on our children': Repenting of the harm caused by Matthew
27:25"](https://www.csbvbristol.org.uk/2023/03/20/repenting-of-the-harm/) and the [Washington Times,
"Antisemitism in the Bible? Study of Matthew
27"](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2024/jan/25/antisemitism-in-bible-study-of-matthew-27/)
that this verse's anti-Jewish reading — Jewish people collectively and perpetually guilty of Christ's
death — dates to the early church fathers, was "universally accepted by the Middle Ages," and has a
documented causal role in centuries of persecution, and that mainstream churches (e.g. the Church of
England's "God's Unfailing Word") have formally repented of this history. This is categorically
different from every other item in this review: it is not a live disagreement between mainstream
traditions about correct doctrine (all mainstream traditions checked here now reject the anti-Jewish
reading), but a verse with a demonstrated, severe, one-directional history of real-world harm from
misreading. **Finding: full exclusion (not narrow bounding) is the correct call, exceeding what a
recall-only or narrative-fact restriction would achieve, since even narrating the line risks
normalizing exposure to it without the historical-harm context this brief is not positioned to
deliver responsibly to players.** This is the item in this brief this review is most confident about
excluding rather than bounding.

### 3.10 Excluded Entirely: Matthew 8:11-12 — confirmed genuine, lower severity than 27:25, exclusion
still the right call

Confirmed via [BibleHub's commentary aggregation on Matthew
8:12](https://biblehub.com/commentaries/matthew/8-12.htm) that Augustine and other patristic/later
commentators read "the children of the kingdom" as ethnic Israel being wholesale replaced by Gentile
believers — a supersessionist reading — while other commentators (also documented in the same
aggregation) read it non-ethnically, as a warning to anyone (Jewish or Christian) who presumes on
inherited status without genuine faith. This carries real interpretive weight but with a less
severe, less doctrinally uniform, and less historically documented harm profile than 27:25 (no
source found describing 8:11-12 as having driven documented historical violence the way 27:25 has).
**Finding: the brief's decision not to select this verse for challenge content, rather than narrowly
bound it, is a defensible and appropriately cautious call** — a recall-only restriction alone would
not prevent a player from encountering the verse's most severe reading unexplained, since recall
format still displays the full verse text. Full exclusion is the more conservative and correct choice
here, even though the underlying dispute is less severe than 27:25's. No change needed.

### 3.11 Excluded Entirely: The Olivet Discourse (Matthew 24-25) — confirmed consistent with the
Revelation/Daniel precedent, exclusion appropriately conservative

Checked this exclusion against `docs/CANON_STRUCTURE.md` §7's explicit instruction that Revelation
should default to "exclude rather than narrowly bound" on eschatological material, and against the
Daniel 7-12 precedent cited there for apocalyptic material generally. Matthew 24-25 contains
comparably fragmented territory (the "abomination of desolation," "this generation," the ten
virgins, sheep and goats) sitting inside the same pre-/mid-/post-tribulation and
premillennial/amillennial/postmillennial disputes this project has already decided not to adjudicate
elsewhere. **Finding: excluding this material entirely, rather than attempting a narrow bounding the
way 16:18-19 or 26:26-28 were bounded, is the right call and consistent with existing project
precedent — this material's contestedness is broader and less textually localized than a single
verse, making narrow bounding harder to do reliably.** No change needed. Note for future
