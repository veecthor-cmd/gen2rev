### 1.1 Item 6 (8:10-11) is *not* a repeat of the Genesis-hard splice defect

The brief states (line 13) that verses already quoted in `docs/content/1-kings.md` are "reused

## 2. Finding A — three partial verses presented as whole verses. **Required fix.**

`CONTENT_STYLE_GUIDE.md` §3 (final bullet) and the `CLAUDE.md` content-integrity guardrail exist to
stop a player being shown text as "the verse" when it isn't the verse. The `medium` brief's own
"(WEB, trimmed)" convention is the established remedy, and this brief applies it correctly three
times (items 3, 5, 10) and omits it three times:

**(i) Item 8 — 1 Kings 8:23, tagged "(WEB, new)".** The verse begins with narrative framing that the
brief drops. Confirmed on three independent sources, including the Masoretic Hebrew, which opens
`וַיֹּאמַר` ("and he said") — so this is a verse-division fact, not a formatting artifact of one
site. BibleGateway WEB prints: *"and he said, 'Yahweh, the God of Israel, there is no God like
you…'"*. Fix: re-tag "(WEB, trimmed)", or restore the opening clause.

**(ii) Item 12 — 1 Kings 18:22, tagged "(WEB, new)".** Full verse: *"Then Elijah said to the people,
'I, even I only, am left as a prophet of Yahweh; but Baal's prophets are four hundred fifty men.'"*
The brief drops "Then Elijah said to the people,". Fix: re-tag "(WEB, trimmed)", or restore.

**(iii) Item 11 — 1 Kings 18:21, tagged "(WEB, already-approved verse)".** Trimmed at *both* ends.
Full verse: *"Elijah came near to all the people, and said, 'How long will you waver between the two
sides? If Yahweh is God, follow him; but if Baal, then follow him.' The people didn't say a word."*
The dropped tail is narratively significant — the people's silence is the beat the contest hangs on.
Fix: re-tag "(WEB, trimmed)", or restore the full verse. **Also fix the same line in
`docs/content/1-kings.md`**, where 18:21 carries no trim label either while the adjacent 18:24 does.

None of these is paraphrase — every displayed word is verbatim WEB. They are labelling defects, and
they are the exact defect `docs/reviews/genesis-easy-review.md` §6.2 required fixed on Gen 8:11. The
pilot's lesson has been applied to stem-anchoring in this brief but not to trim-labelling.

## 3. Finding B — invented text inside quotation marks. **Required fix.**

`CONTENT_STYLE_GUIDE.md` §3, final bullet, is unusually literal: *"if it's not verbatim WEB text, it
isn't presented in quotation marks or as 'the verse.'"* Two items put strings that are not WEB text
of 1 Kings into quotation marks, in the same option list as a string that is:

**Item 1** — distractors `"Build me a house"` / `"Choose a wife"` / `"Fight my enemies"` sit beside
the verbatim `"Ask for what I should give you"` (3:5).

**Item 11** — distractors `"Who built this altar?"` / `"Where is your God?"` / `"Why have you
forsaken Yahweh?"` sit beside the verbatim `"How long will you waver between the two sides?"`
(18:21). These read like scripture, and one is a near-echo of very familiar Psalm wording. (I did
not verify whether similar phrasing occurs anywhere else in WEB — it does not matter to the finding,
since none of them is 1 Kings 18 text and all four options are framed as things Elijah asked.)

This matters more at the easy tier than elsewhere: this is the tier for players with no prior Bible
knowledge, who have no basis for distinguishing a real quotation from a plausible invented one, and
the game's whole premise is that quoted text is bankable.

**Fix (pick one, apply to both items):**
1. Strip the quotation marks from *all four* options and render them as descriptions
   ("that Solomon ask for whatever he wanted" / "that Solomon build him a house" / …), keeping the
   verbatim verse in the displayed stem where it belongs; **or**
2. Keep quotation marks only where the string is verbatim WEB text, and draw distractors from real
   WEB text elsewhere in the book, clearly out of context.

Option 1 is preferred and is what items 5 and 9 already do — their options are unquoted paraphrase,
which is correct.

**Forward-looking rule to record:** item 15 reuses the `medium` level-5 example and no distractor
set has been authored for it yet. When one is, the same rule applies — its correct answer
("What portion have we in David?") is verbatim 12:16 text, so any invented distractor beside it must
not be quoted.

## 5. Contested-Territory Cross-Check

### 5.1 1 Kings 6:7 (item 7) — the brief's "no known cross-tradition dispute" claim is **wrong**, though the item survives it

The brief asserts this detail has "no known cross-tradition dispute." I checked rather than accepted
it, and there is a real and quite old divergence — it just doesn't touch the answer key.

- **Jewish tradition** — [Rashi on I Kings 6:7 (Sefaria)](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Rashi_on_I_Kings.6.7?context=0):
  the stones were removed from the mountain "through the worm [=a שמיר]" and set straight into the
  wall, "and they did not chisel it with any metal tools." On this reading **no metal was used at
  all, anywhere**, including at the quarry.
- **Classic Christian commentary** — [Bible Hub, 1 Kings 6:7](https://biblehub.com/commentaries/1_kings/6-7.htm):
  Keil-Delitzsch have the stones "so perfectly tooled and prepared when first broken in the quarry"
  that no further work was needed on site; JFB point to a subterranean quarry near Jerusalem "where
  the temple stones are supposed to have been hewn." On this reading **iron tools were used — at the
  quarry.** Commentators then divide again on *why*: Ellicott reads it narrowly as "reverence" tied
  to the Old Testament altar prohibitions on iron tools, with Cambridge and Pulpit likewise rooting
  it in Exodus/Deuteronomy law, while Matthew Henry, MacLaren and Gill read it as symbolic
  (Henry: "all things be transacted there with perfect peace and quietness"; MacLaren: "Destructive
  work is noisy, constructive work is silent").

**The bounding holds, on the narrow fact.** Every source consulted agrees no hammer, ax or iron tool
was heard *in the house*; that is what item 7 grades. But the disagreement sits precisely on
*where* the tools were used, which the item's current stem accidentally reaches toward.

**Required fix, §5.1:** the stem says "at the building site," while the verse says "**in the
house**." Under the Christian quarry reading, tools *were* heard at a work site — just not at the
temple. Change the stem to "…what sound was never heard **in the house** while the temple was under
construction?" That is the verse's own wording and puts the item back inside text nobody disputes.

**Required fix, §5.2:** replace the brief's "no known cross-tradition dispute" bullet with the
finding above, plus the structural rule: **no challenge in any tier may grade *how* the stones were
prepared, *where* the tools were used, or *why* iron was excluded.** Grading *what was not heard in
the house* is fine and is what item 7 does.

I also verified the brief's supporting claim that no other verse in 1 Kings 6 describes construction
sounds ([ebible 1KI06](https://ebible.org/web/1KI06.htm), chapter runs to v38 — v7 is the only such
verse). **The anchored stem is therefore genuinely a precaution, not a patch over a live ambiguity,
exactly as the brief says.** The Genesis 8:11 lesson was applied correctly here.

### 5.2 1 Kings 8:23 (item 8) — bounding holds

The brief flags this as sitting in the same temple-prayer material as 8:27 and claims it grades only
what Solomon says. Checked against two traditions:

- **Classic Christian commentary** — [Bible Hub, 1 Kings 8:23](https://biblehub.com/commentaries/1_kings/8-23.htm).
  Commentators are unanimous that Solomon asserts monotheism rather than mere comparative
  superiority; the Pulpit Commentary states the words "do not at all imply the existence of other
  gods," and Ellicott frames Israel's movement "from the polytheism of their forefathers to the
  knowledge of the One only God." One minor textual note: Cambridge records an LXX **singular**
  "thy servant" against the Hebrew plural "thy servants."
- **Jewish tradition** — [Sefaria, I Kings 8:23 (Hebrew + Revised JPS 2023)](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/I_Kings.8.23?context=0):
  "in the heavens above and on the earth below there is **no god like You**, who keep Your gracious
  covenant with Your servants when they walk before You in wholehearted devotion."

The one rendering divergence worth recording: JPS reads lowercase "**no god** like You," WEB reads
"there is **no God** like you." That is a live translation choice about whether the line is
comparative or absolute. **It does not reach the answer key** — item 8 asks what Solomon says there
is none like, and both renderings answer with the deity. The graded token is the one word the
traditions render identically in substance.

**Recommended, §6.3:** record two structural rules alongside the existing 8:27 bounding — (a) no
challenge may grade *whether other gods exist* or how many gods there are; (b) the plural/singular
"servants" and the capital/lowercase "God/god" renderings are display-only and never a graded token
or a distractor axis.

### 5.3 1 Kings 18:22 (item 12) — the "450" claim confirmed

Verified across three independent traditions of transmission: WEB
([ebible 1KI18](https://ebible.org/web/1KI18.htm), [biblehub WEB](https://biblehub.com/web/1_kings/18.htm)),
the Masoretic Hebrew `אַרְבַּע־מֵאוֹת וַחֲמִשִּׁים אִישׁ`, and Revised JPS "four hundred and fifty"
([Sefaria, I Kings 18:22](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/I_Kings.18.22?context=0)). **No
divergence.** The brief's assessment stands.

**Recommended, §6.3:** add the guard the brief didn't think to add. 1 Kings 18:19 has Ahab summon
450 prophets of Baal **and 400 prophets of the Asherah**. Item 12's stem is safely anchored *and*
Baal-specific, so it is unambiguous. But a future unanchored item of the form "how many prophets did
Elijah face on Mount Carmel?" has a defensible answer of 850. Note that the `medium` brief's
difficulty-ladder level-2 example is worded loosely in this direction ("How many prophets of Baal
did Elijah face…" — safe as written only because it says *of Baal*). Record: **any item on this
number must name Baal explicitly and cite 18:22.** This is the Genesis 8:11 defect one authoring
pass away from recurring.

## 6. The two sensitivity exclusions — explicitly confirmed

Both were checked directly against the brief's full text, not inferred.

**1 Kings 18:40 — confirmed absent.** The reference appears exactly twice in the brief (the scene
list and the Contested Territory section), both times as an explicit statement of exclusion.
**No challenge item cites it, quotes it, or narrates its content.** Scene 5 is bounded to 18:20-39
and the two verses actually used (18:21, 18:22) both precede the contest's resolution. For the
record, so the exclusion is auditable, the excluded verse reads: *"Elijah said to them, 'Seize the
prophets of Baal! Don't let one of them escape!' They seized them; and Elijah brought them down to
the brook Kishon, and killed them there."* ([ebible 1KI18](https://ebible.org/web/1KI18.htm)).

One wording recommendation (§6.2): item 16's beat reads "Elijah **defeats** the prophets of Baal on
Mount Carmel." Defensible as a description of the contest, but "defeats" is the one word in this
brief that could be read as gesturing at what happens in v40. Prefer the text's own resolution —
e.g. "**Yahweh answers Elijah by fire on Mount Carmel**" — which is more precise, keeps the
sequencing task identical, and puts no violence in a beginner player's frame.

**The 3:16 "prostitutes" detail — confirmed not leaked.** The word appears twice in the brief, both
times in reviewer-facing prose explaining why 3:16 is *not* selected. It appears in **no** challenge
item, stem, option, answer key, or boss beat. Scene 2 is titled "The Two Women"; item 4's stem says
"the two women"; item 5's says "the child's true mother"; item 13's first beat says "The women are
brought before the king." The `medium` brief's framing is preserved exactly. **Acceptance criterion
5 in the brief is accurate as checked.**

Structural rule to record (§6.4): item 13's opening beat corresponds to 3:16 — the one verse in this
scene deliberately kept off the challenge-ready list. It is currently a narrative summary with no
verse text, which is the right handling. Mark it explicitly as **narrative-summary only, never
displays 3:16 text**, so a later pass adding citations to the boss beats (see §6.5) doesn't pull the
verse in mechanically.
