### Finding A — item 12 (1 Kgs 18:26): trimmed verse presented as complete. **Required fix.**

The brief renders item 12's source text as:

> "They took the bull which was given them, and they dressed it, and called on the name of Baal from
> morning even until noon, saying, 'Baal, hear us!' But there was no voice, and nobody answered."

tagged **"(WEB, new)"** — the brief's marker for a complete, untrimmed verse. All three sources agree
the verse continues with a further sentence:

> "…But there was no voice, and nobody answered. **They leaped about the altar which was made.**"

The words quoted are accurate; the completeness label is wrong. This is exactly the defect
`docs/qa/1-kings-verbatim-qa.md` corrected twice in the medium brief (3:12, 12:14) and that
`docs/reviews/genesis-easy-review.md` §6.2 required for Gen 8:11 — presenting part of a verse to a
player as *the* verse. **Fix:** re-tag as **"(WEB, trimmed)"**, or restore the final sentence to the
displayed text. The recall blank itself ("But there was no ___, and nobody answered" → *voice*) is
verbatim, unambiguous, and needs no change.

*Verification note worth recording for the QA pass:* my first fetch of this chapter (via the
`engwebp` path) returned verse 26 ending at "nobody answered", which would have confirmed the
brief's quotation as complete. Two further independent fetches (ebible.org `/web/`, Bible Hub WEB,
and BibleGateway WEB) all carry the trailing sentence. A single fetch would have missed this.

### Finding B — item 9 (1 Kgs 8:29): altered punctuation inside a quotation. **Required fix (minor).**

The brief renders the blank as:

> "…even toward the place of which you have said, 'My name shall be ___.'" → **there**

Both sources give the verse as:

> "that your eyes may be open toward this house night and day, even toward the place of which you
> have said, 'My name shall be **there;**' to listen to the prayer which your servant prays toward
> this place."

The semicolon has been converted to a full stop and the quotation closed, making a mid-verse cut
read as a sentence end. The leading ellipsis correctly signals the front trim; there is no trailing
ellipsis signalling the back trim. **Fix:** restore `there;` and add a trailing ellipsis, i.e.
`"…even toward the place of which you have said, 'My name shall be ___;'…"`, or tag the item
"(WEB, trimmed)" with an explicit note that display shows the clause only.

This defect is **inherited from the approved medium brief**, which renders 8:29 the same way
("That your eyes may be open…'My name shall be there.'" — also capitalising the verse's initial
lowercase "that"). `docs/qa/1-kings-verbatim-qa.md` recorded 8:29 as "Match, character-for-character"
and did not catch it. Flagging for the medium brief's record as well; it is not this tier's fault,
but this tier is propagating it.

### Finding C — source-path caveat for the verbatim-QA pass. **Required (process, not content).**

The brief states its text was "fetched fresh from ebible.org". **ebible.org serves more than one WEB
edition, and they differ on the divine name.** The `engwebp` path renders 1 Kgs 6:1 as "he began to
build **the LORD's** house", 18:38 as "**the LORD's** fire", 18:39 as "**The LORD**, he is God!",
and 18:21 as "If **the LORD** is God". The `/web/` path, BibleGateway `version=WEB`, and Bible Hub's
WEB all render these as **Yahweh**, which is what `docs/content/1-kings.md` uses under an approved
review and Open Decision #1.

**No error resulted in this brief** — item 7 correctly reads "Yahweh's house", so the drafter clearly
used a Yahweh edition. But the ambiguity is live and undocumented, and note that the Genesis pilot
reviews cite `ebible.org/engwebp/…` URLs (harmlessly — the Genesis verses checked there contain no
divine name). **Fix:** pin the canonical fetch path in the brief's translation note and in
`docs/qa/1-kings-hard-verbatim-qa.md` — `https://ebible.org/web/…` or BibleGateway `version=WEB`,
**not** `/engwebp/`.

## 5. Contested-Territory Cross-Check

### 5.1 — 1 Kings 3:14, the conditional promise (brief's item 3). **Bounding holds — more strongly than the brief argues.**

The brief flags this as "adjacent to broader retribution-theology questions (does righteousness
reliably produce a long life? Job and Ecclesiastes are frequently read in tension…)" and proposes
recall-only bounding. I did not accept that on the brief's word. Checked across traditions:

- **Jewish — Rashi on I Kings 3:14 (Sefaria).** Rashi draws the distinction explicitly: wealth and
  honour God will give Solomon "whether deserving or not," while longevity and dynastic continuity
  are contingent on obedience — citing the Sifrei and R. Chanina ben Gamliel.
  [sefaria.org/api/texts/Rashi_on_I_Kings.3.14](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Rashi_on_I_Kings.3.14?context=0)
- **Jewish translation — Revised JPS (2023) via Sefaria.** "And I will further grant you long life,
  if you will walk in My ways and observe My laws and commandments, as did your father David."
  Hebrew: `וְאִם תֵּלֵךְ בִּדְרָכַי… וְהַאֲרַכְתִּי אֶת־יָמֶיךָ`.
  [sefaria.org/api/texts/I_Kings.3.14](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/I_Kings.3.14?context=0)
- **Classic Christian commentators (Bible Hub aggregation).** Unanimous, and on the *same* point
  Rashi makes: Gill — "the other promises of riches and honour are absolute, but this of long life
  conditional"; Poole — the condition was attached "lest his great wisdom should make him proud";
  Barnes — the condition "was not observed… the right to the promise was forfeited"; Ellicott,
  Cambridge and Barnes converge on Solomon dying around 59–60, Gill citing a Jewish tradition of 52.
  [biblehub.com/commentaries/1_kings/3-14.htm](https://biblehub.com/commentaries/1_kings/3-14.htm)
- **Lexical — Blue Letter Bible H748 `אָרַךְ` ('āraḵ)**, "to be (causative, make) long," glossed
  "lengthen, prolong, defer, draw out." No tradition-specific loading; WEB's "lengthen" is the plain
  rendering of the verb the Hebrew actually uses.
  [blueletterbible.org/lexicon/h748](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/h748/kjv/wlc/0-1/)

**Result: the disagreement the brief feared is not actually live on this verse.** Jewish and
Christian commentary converge on reading 3:14 as a *specific, personal, conditional covenant promise
to Solomon* — not as a general statement of the retribution principle that Job and Ecclesiastes are
read against. The graded token "lengthen" is the direct rendering of H748 and is disputed by no one.
A player typing "lengthen" affirms nothing a Jewish, Catholic, Orthodox or Protestant reader would
contest. **The recall-only rule is sufficient and the acceptance-criteria checkbox is justified.**

**But the brief's rule is one clause too narrow — see Recommendation 6.3.** The commentary consensus
above is reached by *inference from 1 Kings 11*, not from anything 1 Kings 3 states. So a future item
of the shape "Did Solomon receive the long life God promised?" or "Why did Solomon not live long?"
would require the player to affirm a judgment the text of this scene does not make — and would lean
on 1 Kings 11, which `docs/content/1-kings.md` already flags as a deliberate, unincluded gap. That
belongs in the structural rule.

### 5.2 — 1 Kings 6:1, the "four hundred and eightieth year" (brief's item 7). **Bounding holds, but the brief has the risk half-wrong and the rule needs strengthening.**

The brief describes the risk as an early-date (c. 1446 BC) vs. late-date (c. 1250 BC) *interpretive*
dispute over what the number implies. That much is real and confirmed. What the brief misses is that
**the number itself sits on a live manuscript variant**, which matters more because the graded token
*is* the number.

- **Textual variant.** The majority of Septuagint manuscripts read **440**, not 480; the Cambridge
  Bible (via Bible Hub) explains the LXX figure as a sum of the judges' years omitting other events.
  Independent confirmation of the MT/LXX split and its effect on the dating argument:
  [Armstrong Institute, "The '480 Years' of 1 Kings 6:1"](https://armstronginstitute.org/762-the-480-years-of-1-kings-6-1-just-a-symbolic-number),
  [Associates for Biblical Research, "The Biblical Date For The Exodus Is 1446 BC"](https://biblearchaeology.org/research/exodus-from-egypt/2954-the-biblical-date-for-the-exodus-is-1446-bc-a-response-to-james-hoffmeier),
  [Bible and Interpretation, "The Mysterious Number 480 in 1 Kings 6:1"](https://bibleinterp.arizona.edu/articles/2016/04/laa408018).
- **Mainstream Christian commentators disagree with each other about the phrase's authenticity.**
  The Pulpit Commentary calls the figure "manifestly erroneous" and floats it as "an interpolation
  into the sacred text"; Barnes argues similarly; Keil & Delitzsch defend 480 as correct and say its
  correctness "is now pretty generally admitted." Several note the figure conflicts with the summed
  Hebrew data (totals of roughly 498–600 years) and with Josephus's 592.
  [biblehub.com/commentaries/1_kings/6-1.htm](https://biblehub.com/commentaries/1_kings/6-1.htm)
- **Jewish tradition reads 480 and builds no dating argument on it.** Revised JPS (2023): "In the
  four hundred and eightieth year after the Israelites left the land of Egypt…"
  [sefaria.org/api/texts/I_Kings.6.1](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/I_Kings.6.1?context=0).
  Radak on the verse comments only on the month of Ziv (= Iyar), on "the fourth year of his reign"
  as confirmed by the Chronicles parallel, and on "he built" meaning "he began to build" — no
  chronological reckoning at all.
  [sefaria.org/api/texts/Radak_on_I_Kings.6.1](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Radak_on_I_Kings.6.1?context=0)
- **Late-date proponents read the number as symbolic** rather than literal (a *Distanzangaben*),
  since neither 480 nor 440 fits a 13th-century Exodus — confirmed in the sources above.

**Why the bounding nonetheless holds.** The item displays the verbatim WEB verse, anchored to the
reference, with the blank *inside* the displayed clause: "In the four hundred and ___ year after the
children of Israel had come out of the land of Egypt… he began to build Yahweh's house." A player
answering "eightieth" is asserting only *what the WEB text of 1 Kings 6:1 prints* — a fact no
tradition disputes, since MT, WEB and JPS all read 480 and WEB is fixed as the MVP translation by
Open Decision #1. This is §2 direct verse recall. The player is not asked when the Exodus happened,
which chronology is right, or whether the number is original. **The item as written is in-bounds and
the acceptance-criteria checkbox is justified.**

**The brief's proposed rule is not sufficient as phrased — see Recommendation 6.4.** It says the
challenge "grades only the number the text itself states." That does not close the unanchored-stem
hole: a future item reading "How many years passed between the Exodus and the building of the
temple?" would still technically grade "the number the text states" while having at least three
answers in live circulation (480 MT/WEB, 440 LXX, and "not a literal figure"). That is precisely the
answer-key ambiguity class the Genesis 8:11 finding established, and this tier's easy sibling already
applied the anchoring lesson at 6:7 and 18:22. The rule must require the reference-anchor and
displayed verbatim clause, not just restrict the subject matter.

### 5.3 — 1 Kings 18:37, "turned their heart back again" — contested item the brief did NOT flag. **Recommendation 6.5.**

Item 16 is a boss item that displays the full text of 18:37, including a clause whose English
rendering is genuinely disputed and which WEB resolves in one direction:

- **WEB** — "and that you have turned their heart back **again**" — i.e. God restoring the people
  toward himself.
- **Revised JPS (2023), via Sefaria** — "for You have turned their hearts **backward**," with the
  note that the meaning of the Hebrew `הֲסִבֹּתָ אֶת־לִבָּם אֲחֹרַנִּית` **is uncertain**.
  [sefaria.org/api/texts/I_Kings.18.37](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/I_Kings.18.37?context=0)
- **Classic Christian commentators (Bible Hub)** — near-unanimous for the restorative reading (Poole:
  "back again unto thee, from whom they have revolted"), with Gill recording and rejecting the
  opposite reading: "though some understand this of God's giving them up to a spirit of error, and
  suffering them to fall into idolatry, and hardening their hearts, as he did Pharaoh's."
  [biblehub.com/commentaries/1_kings/18-37.htm](https://biblehub.com/commentaries/1_kings/18-37.htm)

So the two readings — God turning hearts *back to himself* vs. God turning hearts *away* — are both
attested, they land on opposite sides of a divine-agency/hardening question, and the Jewish
translation tradition takes the reading WEB does not while flagging the Hebrew as uncertain.

**The item as written is safe.** The graded token is "God," in the clause "that you, Yahweh, are
___", which every tradition renders identically and which is the plainest possible narrative fact.
Nothing in the answer key depends on the disputed clause. **But this is exactly the situation
`CONTENT_STYLE_GUIDE.md` §4 says must be flagged deliberately rather than surface after ingestion,
and it is directly parallel to the *ruach elohim* finding at Gen 1:2 in the Genesis hard-tier
review.** The brief's Contested Territory section currently characterises 18:26/18:37 as carrying
"no new contested ground," which is not accurate for 18:37.

## 7. Verdict

**`approved-with-changes`.**

Every verse text the brief asserts is verbatim-correct against two independent live WEB sources, and
every one of the 16 items is in-bounds under `CONTENT_STYLE_GUIDE.md` §2 and clear of all five §3
prohibitions. No item requires a player to affirm a doctrinal or denominational position. Both
standing medium-tier exclusions — 1 Kings 18:40, and the 3:16 "prostitutes" identification — are
fully honoured, with the "two women" framing kept throughout.

The seven required changes are text-completeness labelling (6.1, 6.2), structural-rule tightening
(6.3, 6.4, 6.5), provenance accuracy (6.6) and source-path hygiene (6.7). **None requires removing or
rewriting a challenge item**, and none reflects a doctrinal problem with the content as drafted.

**On the two items the review request asked me to verify rather than accept:**

- **1 Kings 3:14** — the motivating concern is retribution theology (whether obedience reliably
  produces long life). The bounding **holds, and more securely than the brief argues**: Rashi and the
  classic Christian commentators independently converge on the *same* structural reading — riches and
  honour unconditional, long life conditional on Solomon personally — so this verse is not in fact a
  site of cross-tradition disagreement, and the graded word "lengthen" is the plain rendering of
  Hebrew H748 `אָרַךְ`. Recall-only is sufficient; 6.3 closes the one remaining opening.
- **1 Kings 6:1** — the motivating concern is Exodus dating. The bounding **holds, but for a
  different reason than the brief gives, and the brief understates the risk**: beyond the early/late
  date dispute, the graded number itself sits on an MT-480 / LXX-440 manuscript variant, and
  mainstream commentators disagree over whether the phrase is even original. What saves the item is
  that it displays the verbatim WEB verse with the blank inside it and anchors to the reference, so
  the player asserts only what WEB prints — which MT, WEB and JPS all agree on. 6.4 is required to
  make the rule actually do the work the brief credits it with.

**Not escalated.** Both flagged items were checked against live sources representing genuinely
different traditions (Jewish: Sefaria/Revised JPS, Rashi, Radak; classic Christian: Bible Hub
aggregation across Henry, Barnes, Gill, Poole, Ellicott, Cambridge, Pulpit, Keil & Delitzsch;
contemporary scholarship on both sides of the Exodus-date question; Blue Letter Bible for the
lexical check). In each case the graded token turned out to be text the disagreeing traditions render
identically, which is what makes the narrative-only bounding hold rather than merely sound plausible.
The one unflagged contested item I found (18:37) is display-only with an uncontested graded token,
and 6.5 records it properly. Nothing here rose to the level of an unresolved live disagreement
bearing on a graded answer key, and nothing touched the child-safety guardrails beyond the 18:40
exclusion, which is intact.

**Two caveats I could not close, stated plainly rather than glossed:**

1. **I could not reach a Catholic reference source for either flagged item.** `bible.usccb.org`

   sourcing.
2. **This review was performed by an AI agent standing in for the named human theological reviewer**

   Decision #7 still has no named human reviewer, so an escalation would currently have nowhere to
