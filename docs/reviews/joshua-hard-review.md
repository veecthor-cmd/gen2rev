## 2. Citation Precision (§5) — required fix

Six items cite a multi-verse range while the graded blank sits in one specific verse (see the third
column of §1). This is not a splice and not a text-integrity violation — the displayed context is
honest and ellipsis-marked — but `CONTENT_STYLE_GUIDE.md` §5 requires the item to carry *the* verse
reference, and `BACKEND_REQUIREMENTS.md` §4 enforces citation technically at ingestion. A stored
item whose `reference` is `Joshua 3:15-17` while its answer token lives in 3:16 will either fail that
check or, worse, pass it while being imprecise in what a player sees. See §6, change **R4**.

This is the mild form of the Genesis hard-tier lesson: re-cutting blanks out of an already-approved
multi-verse block without re-checking verse boundaries. Here the boundaries turned out fine; the
citations did not follow them.

## 5. Contested-Territory Cross-Check

The brief asserts "Nothing new is introduced by this brief: no new verse, no new scene." That is
true at the level of *verses and scenes*. It is **not** true at the level of *exposure*, because a
hard tier changes which clause a player is graded on. Four findings follow, three of them new
ground the brief did not flag.

### 5.1 Item 10 — the blank moves into the divine-warfare/justification clause. **Required change.**

This is the substantive finding of the review.

The easy tier's Scene 4 item blanks **"Gaza"** — pure geography, inside 10:41. The hard tier's item
10 keeps the same two approved verses but blanks **"Israel"** inside 10:42: *"Joshua took all these
kings and their land at one time because Yahweh, the God of Israel, fought for ___."* Same approved
text; a different clause is now the graded object.

**What the cross-tradition check found:**

- **Textually, the clause is uncontested.** Sefaria's Revised JPS (2023) renders 10:42 "All those
  kings and their lands were conquered by Joshua at a single stroke, for the E<small>TERNAL</small>,
  the God of Israel, fought for Israel"
  ([Sefaria, Joshua 10:42](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Joshua.10.42?context=0)) — materially
  identical to WEB on the graded word. By the Genesis-hard test ("does the graded token read the
  same across the traditions that disagree?"), the token itself passes.
- **Interpretively, this exact clause is the one mainstream commentators use to carry the
  conquest's moral justification.** The Bible Hub aggregation for 10:42
  ([biblehub.com/commentaries/joshua/10-42.htm](https://biblehub.com/commentaries/joshua/10-42.htm))
  shows the commentators agreeing on what the clause *says* while explicitly building the conquest's
  legitimacy on it: Matthew Henry — "God here showed his hatred of the idolatries and other
  abominations of which the Canaanites had been guilty"; the Pulpit Commentary — "Wars of
  independence, wars undertaken to chastise wickedness and oppression, seldom fail in their
  object… where the Lord God fights for Israel… there is a moral strength which triumphs";
  Keil-Delitzsch — "He was able to accomplish this, because Jehovah the God of Israel fought for
  Israel." This is the justification argument, resting on this verse.

So the clause a hard-tier player would be asked to complete from memory is the textual hinge of the
just-war-versus-ḥerem debate that `docs/reviews/joshua-review.md` §3 documented as genuinely live and
unresolved, and that Kachi personally adjudicated on 2026-08-08. The medium brief's structural rule
for Scene 4 says no item drawing on it "may ask *why* the campaigns happened, *whether they were
commanded/justified*, or require the player to affirm or evaluate the ḥerem practice in any way,"
and Kachi's sign-off scopes Scene 4 to its "**geographic/summary** verses… in recall format only."

Item 10 satisfies the *letter* of the format rule (it is fill-in-blank, not multiple choice) while
moving the graded token off geography and onto the stated divine rationale. I do not think that is
what the human sign-off scoped, and the brief's own self-check — "grades only the word 'Israel,' and
makes no claim about motive or justification" — understates it: the *stem the player must complete*
is the motive clause, even if the token is a proper noun.

Aggravating, and independently sufficient on its own: the stem contains "the God of **Israel**"
four words before the blank. The answer is cued by the stem, so the item has near-zero recall
difficulty in a tier explicitly designed for "very deep scripture knowledge" — it buys nothing
pedagogically in exchange for the exposure.

**Required fix (R1):** move the blank into 10:41's geography, matching the easy tier's precedent and
Kachi's "geographic/summary" scoping — e.g. *"Joshua struck them from Kadesh Barnea even to Gaza,
and all the country of ___, even to Gibeon."* → **Goshen** (a genuinely secondary, less-memorized
place name, which serves this tier's stated design intent better than a cued proper noun), or
*"…even to ___."* → **Gibeon**. Re-cite the item to **Joshua 10:41**. Verse 42 may remain as
displayed context.

### 5.2 Item 2 — "Red Sea" is itself a translation choice. Not flagged by the brief. **Recommended.**

The graded token is **"Red Sea"**, which is WEB's rendering of Hebrew *yam suph*. That rendering is
not shared across traditions: Sefaria's Revised JPS reads "the **Sea of Reeds**"
([Sefaria, Joshua 2:10](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Joshua.2.10?context=0)), and Blue Letter
Bible's lexicon for *suph* (H5488) glosses it "a reed, especially the papyrus," noting *yam suph*
literally means "sea of rushes or reeds" and that "Red Sea" reaches English via the Greek θάλασσα
Ἐρυθρά rather than from the Hebrew
([blueletterbible.org H5488](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/h5488/kjv/wlc/0-1/)).

This is philological, not denominational — no tradition's doctrine hangs on it — so it does **not**
make the item out-of-bounds under §3. But this is the first graded token in the book where the
*answer key itself* is a translation decision rather than text all traditions render alike, and a
player from a Jewish background typing "Sea of Reeds" on a hard tier would be marked wrong for
knowing more, not less. Same handling pattern as the *ruach elohim* finding in
`docs/reviews/genesis-hard-review.md` §3.3. See §6, **Rec-1**.

### 5.3 Item 2 — Joshua 2:10 contains ḥerem vocabulary in the half the brief trims. **Required change.**

Newly identified; neither the medium brief nor the easy-tier brief flags it.

The brief's item 2 quotation ends with an ellipsis after "when you came out of Egypt." The full WEB
verse continues: "…and what you did to the two kings of the Amorites, who were beyond the Jordan, to
Sihon and to Og, **whom you utterly destroyed**"
([ebible.org WEB, Joshua 2](https://ebible.org/web/JOS02.htm)). That the underlying Hebrew here is
ḥerem vocabulary is confirmed cross-tradition: Revised JPS renders the same clause "whom you
**doomed**" ([Sefaria, Joshua 2:10](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Joshua.2.10?context=0)) — the
standard JPS rendering of the ḥerem verb.

**As written, item 2 is compliant** — the destruction clause is trimmed out and is not quoted,
referenced, or graded. The risk is structural and forward-looking, and it is exactly the failure
shape the Genesis pilot caught twice: item 2's blank now sits **inside 2:10**, so any authoring or
ingestion pass that resolves "the verse for this item" to the full verse for display context will
pull destruction language into a child-facing challenge screen — inside the most sensitive book in
the MVP, under a permanent exclusion regime. The medium brief's exclusion list names 6:17, 6:21 and
10:40 by reference, so a pipeline checking against that list would not catch 2:10.

**Required fix (R3):** add an explicit Contested Territory bullet stating that Joshua 2:10 contains
ḥerem vocabulary in its second half, that item 2's display text is locked to the trimmed form shown
in the brief, and that the clause "and what you did to the two kings of the Amorites… whom you
utterly destroyed" is never display text, never a graded token, and never a challenge subject at any
tier. Carry the same note to `docs/content/joshua.md` and `docs/content/joshua-easy.md` so the
exclusion list is complete rather than tier-local.

### 5.4 Item 1 — "at Adam" rests on a ketiv/qere variant. **Recommended forward guard.**

The graded token "Adam" is stable: Revised JPS also reads "at Adam, the town next to Zarethan"
([Sefaria, Joshua 3:16](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Joshua.3.16?context=0)). But the same
source shows the Masoretic text carries a ketiv/qere at exactly this point — written באדם ("at/in
Adam"), read מאדם ("from Adam") — so translations legitimately differ on the *preposition*, not the
place name.

No effect on the current item, which grades the name. Recommend recording the guard now, in the
style of the Gen 22:13 and Gen 12:1 guards added by `docs/reviews/genesis-easy-review.md` §6.4: **no
challenge at any tier may grade "at" versus "from" Adam.** See §6, **Rec-2**.

  WEB is fixed by Open Decision #1 and the medium brief already displays it under an approved

## 6. Required and Recommended Changes

**R1 — Item 10: move the blank out of the justification clause. [required]**
Per §5.1. Blank a geographic token in **Joshua 10:41** (preferred: "all the country of ___" →
**Goshen**) and re-cite the item to 10:41. **If the drafter prefers to retain the "fought for ___"
→ Israel blank, that variant may not be self-approved: it must go to Kachi Okere as a human
decision**, because it changes the graded exposure of the exact clause his 2026-08-08 sign-off
bounded. This reviewer approves the geographic fix, not the current form.

**R2 — Item 3: fix the answer-key ambiguity. [required]**
The stem "Israel came over this Jordan on dry ___" → **land** is textually correct for 4:22, but the
same scene's 3:17 says "on dry **ground**" twice, and **this brief's own item 8 sequence card reads
"Israel crosses the Jordan on dry ground."** In a typed-recall tier with no multiple-choice
scaffolding, "ground" is a defensible answer a well-read player will type. This is the same defect
class as `docs/reviews/genesis-easy-review.md` §6.1 (unanchored stem with a second correct answer).
Fix: anchor the stem to the reference visibly ("According to Joshua 4:22…"), and record "ground" in
the answer key as an explicitly-rejected near-miss so the ingestion pipeline handles it
deliberately rather than by accident.

**R3 — Item 2: lock the display text and add the 2:10 ḥerem-vocabulary note. [required]**
Per §5.3. Propagate the note to `docs/content/joshua.md` and `docs/content/joshua-easy.md`.

**R4 — Items 1–6: re-cite to the verse containing the blank. [required]**
Per §2. Item 1 → **3:16**; item 2 → **2:10**; item 3 → **4:22**; item 4 → **14:11**; item 5 →
**4:7**; item 6 → **6:4**. The wider range may be retained as a labelled display-context field, but
the item's citation must name the verse it grades.

**Rec-1 — Item 2: handle the *yam suph* rendering. [recommended]**
Either accept "Sea of Reeds"/"Reed Sea" as alternate correct answers, or move the blank to an
uncontested token in the same clause ("when you came out of ___" → **Egypt**). Add a Contested
Territory bullet recording the WEB/JPS divergence with the two sources in §5.2.

**Rec-2 — Item 1: add the ketiv/qere forward guard. [recommended]**
No challenge at any tier grades "at" versus "from" Adam. Per §5.4.

**Rec-3 — Item 7: restate the Rahab UI/voice-over handling note explicitly. [recommended]**
Per §5.5; the easy brief does this and the hard brief should not rely on inheritance while
displaying the full verse.

**Rec-4 — Duplicate answer token. [recommended, design nit]**
Items 3 and 9 both grade **land**. If R2's fix is applied by anchoring rather than re-blanking, no
correctness problem remains, but two identical answers in a 10-item tier is worth a deliberate look.

**Rec-5 — For the verbatim-QA pass, not a review defect.**
WEB prints inner quotation marks the brief drops: 4:6-7 ("…saying, 'What do you mean by these
stones?' then you shall tell them, 'Because the waters…'"), 4:22-24, and 2:1. Same pattern as
`docs/reviews/genesis-easy-review.md` §5.1 — ingestion must store WEB's inner quotes, not the
brief's flattened form. Item 5 should also carry the "(WEB, trimmed)" label, per the medium brief's
convention, since it quotes only the final sentence of 4:7.

## 7. Verdict

**`approved-with-changes`.**

**Reason:** the ḥerem exclusion holds absolutely, every quotation is verbatim-correct against live
WEB sources with no splices, and all ten items are in-bounds under §2 — but item 10 relocates the
graded blank into the conquest's stated-justification clause, which changes the exposure Kachi's
sign-off bounded even though the verse text is old, and item 3 has a second defensible answer.

**Blocking before ingestion:** R1, R2, R3, R4.

**Not escalated — and why, stated explicitly.** Item 10 sits on the one genuinely live, unresolved
cross-tradition disagreement in this book, which is normally escalation territory. I am not
escalating because a precedented fix (R1) exists that returns the item to indisputably geographic
ground — exactly the scope Kachi's 2026-08-08 sign-off named — and with that fix applied there is no
residual contested exposure for a human to adjudicate. I am confident the fixed form is safe and
confident the current form should not ship as-is; neither leaves me uncertain in the way step 7(a)
describes. **The escalation path is preserved as a condition, not discarded:** if the current
"fought for ___" blank is retained instead of fixed, that is a human decision for Kachi, not
something this review authorizes.

**Residual risk stated plainly:** this review was performed by an AI agent standing in for the named
human theological reviewer scoped in `GEN2REV_MASTER_PROMPT.md` (substitution logged in `MEMORY.md`,
2026-08-08). Open Decision #7 was resolved for this book specifically when Kachi took the named-
reviewer role on 2026-08-08, so unlike Genesis there *is* a human this book's escalations can reach.
A human specialist may still weigh the item 10 clause more strictly than I have — my judgment is
that the geographic fix resolves it, but the fact that this book required a human decision once
already is reason for the calling session to surface R1 to Kachi as information even if the fix is
simply applied.

**Outstanding, not this reviewer's job:** independent verbatim-text QA per `QA_REQUIREMENTS.md` §3
(`docs/qa/joshua-hard-verbatim-qa.md`), which must re-check the corrected items 1–6 citations, the
