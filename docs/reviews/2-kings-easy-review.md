## 4. Contested-Territory Cross-Check

### 4.1 2 Kings 19:35 — mass-casualty sensitivity (inherited)

Confirmed handled correctly in §2 above. As the `medium` review recorded, this is a
sensitivity/graphic-content question rather than a doctrinal-neutrality one; the graded fact (the

### 4.2 2 Kings 18:13 (item 7) — the "fourteenth year" crux. **Bounding holds, but tighten the rule.**

The brief flags this as a "one campaign or two" historical-critical discussion. That understates
where the live disagreement actually sits, so this needed checking rather than accepting.

- **Classic Protestant commentary** — [Bible Hub, 2 Kings 18:13](https://biblehub.com/commentaries/2_kings/18-13.htm)
  (Barnes, Ellicott, Pulpit Commentary, Gill, Keil-Delitzsch, Cambridge). The dispute is sharper
  than the brief suggests, and it is about the **date, not the campaign count**. Barnes: this note
  of time "is hopelessly at variance with the Assyrian dates for the two events," and he suggests
  it "crept in from the margin." The Pulpit Commentary: "It is impossible to accept this note of
  time as genuine without rejecting altogether the authority of the Assyrian inscriptions," and
  proposes a later redactor inserted it. The arithmetic problem is real and uncontested: the
  biblical framing implies ~8 years between Samaria's fall (722/721 BC) and this invasion, while
  the Assyrian record places Sennacherib's Judean campaign in 701 BC, ~21 years later. On the
  campaign-count question the same commentators are effectively agreed on one main campaign.
- **Jewish translation tradition** — [Sefaria, II Kings 18:13, Revised JPS 2023](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/II_Kings.18.13?context=0):
  "In the fourteenth year of King Hezekiah, King Sennacherib of Assyria marched against all the
  fortified towns of Judah and seized them." Same actor, same regnal-year framing, rendered without
  hedging.
- Rashi's commentary on this verse returns **empty** on Sefaria
  ([API](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Rashi_on_II_Kings.18.13?context=0)) — stating that
  plainly rather than substituting my own synthesis. I could not confirm a specific classical
  Jewish commentary position on the chronology from a live source.
- I attempted the Catholic NABRE notes at [bible.usccb.org/bible/2kings/18](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/2kings/18)
  and received **HTTP 403**; no Catholic reference source was successfully consulted for this verse.
  Recorded as a gap, not papered over. It does not change the outcome below, because the graded
  token is confirmed identical across the Protestant and Jewish sources that *were* reached.

**Why the bounding holds:** the graded answer is "Sennacherib king of Assyria," and every source
reached — WEB, Revised JPS, and the full Bible Hub commentary set — agrees Sennacherib is the
invader this verse names. The disputed material (the year's reliability against Assyrian records)
is not the graded token; the stem is anchored "According to 2 Kings 18:13," which puts the question
squarely on what the text says. Displaying contested text while grading uncontested text is the
same instrument already approved for Genesis 1:5's "first day"
(`docs/reviews/genesis-easy-review.md` §4.3).

**But the brief's own bounding sentence is wrong and must be fixed.** It claims the item "grades
only what the text itself states (**who invaded, and when**, per the text's own regnal-year
framing)." Grading *when* is exactly what would cross into §3's historicity prohibition. The item
as written does not do this — but the brief's stated rule licenses a future authoring pass to write
"In the ___ year of King Hezekiah" → "fourteenth", which would grade a date that mainstream
commentators call textually suspect. See §6.3.

### 4.3 2 Kings 2:11 / 2:14 (items 2, 16) — the Elijah succession scene. **Bounding holds; confirmed the exclusion is guarding something real.**

The brief inherits `medium`'s "no NT typology" bounding and asserts item 16 adds "no new ground." I
checked both halves of that.

- **Orthodox** — [OrthodoxWiki, Elijah](https://orthodoxwiki.org/Elijah). The tradition attaches a
  substantial typological layer to this scene: John the Baptist as an Elijah-type, Elijah's
  appearance at the Transfiguration, and a distinctively Orthodox eschatological expectation that
  "God will send Elijah and Enoch before coming of the Antichrist." The Enoch pairing in particular
  is not a mainstream Protestant position. So the exclusion is guarding a **genuine, live
  cross-tradition divergence**, not a hypothetical one.
- **Jewish translation tradition** — [Sefaria, II Kings 2:11, Revised JPS 2023](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/II_Kings.2.11?context=0):
  "a fiery chariot with fiery horses suddenly appeared and separated one from the other; and Elijah
  went up to heaven in a whirlwind." Narratively identical to WEB on the point item 2 grades.
- I attempted [enduringword.com/bible-commentary/2-kings-2/](https://enduringword.com/bible-commentary/2-kings-2/)
  for a contemporary evangelical reading and received **HTTP 403**. Noted as a gap; the Bible Hub
  and Sefaria checks above carry the finding.

**Why it holds:** items 2 and 16 grade *what appeared and separated them* and *what Elisha did with
the mantle*. The Revised JPS and WEB narrate both facts the same way. Nothing in the Orthodox
typological layer, or its Protestant counterpart, bears on either answer key — the traditions
diverge on what the ascension *prefigures*, while agreeing completely on what happens. Item 16's
answer ("struck the water with it, and it parted so he could cross") is stated outright in 2:14.
**No new ground; the brief's assessment is accurate.**

One forward-looking guard is worth adding, though: the mantle's transfer is read across traditions
as the transmission of prophetic authority (OrthodoxWiki states this explicitly), so a future item
of the shape "what did the mantle *mean*?" or "what did the double portion *confer*?" would leave
in-bounds territory. See §6.6.

### 4.4 2 Kings 22:13 (item 10) — divine wrath and national calamity. **Bounding holds.**

The brief flags this as causation-adjacent to 17:7. Checked:

- **Classic Protestant commentary** — [Bible Hub, 2 Kings 22:13](https://biblehub.com/commentaries/2_kings/22-13.htm)
  (Matthew Henry, Poole, Gill, Keil-Delitzsch, Pulpit Commentary). Unanimous on **what Josiah's
  stated reason was**: the fathers' failure to obey the book's words. Gill has Josiah fearing "what
  was threatened would fall upon him" because the ancestors were "very deficient in observing the
  laws." Where they differ is on the *immediacy and remediability* of the wrath — Poole asks
  whether "the curses here threatened must come upon us without remedy"; the Pulpit Commentary
  frames the live question as whether "the measure of sin is already full, or whether there is yet
  hope of grace?" That is a theodicy question, and it is downstream of the graded content.
- **Jewish translation tradition** — [Sefaria, II Kings 22:13, Revised JPS 2023](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/II_Kings.22.13?context=0):
  "For great indeed must be GOD's wrath that has been kindled against us, because our ancestors did
  not obey the words of this scroll to do all that has been prescribed for us." Same stated
  causal chain, in a translation from a different tradition, with no interpretive softening.

**Why it holds:** the graded answer is Josiah's own in-text reason, and the two traditions render
that reason identically. The item asks *what Josiah said and why he said he was sending* — §2's
"direct quotations attributed in-text." It does not ask whether the wrath was deserved, whether it
fell, or whether national calamity generally indicates divine judgment; the commentators' actual
disagreement is on precisely those questions and none of them is graded. **The brief's claim that
this "does not extend to a general claim about divine wrath and national calamity" is accurate.**

## 5. Additional Observations (non-blocking)

**5.1 WEB edition divergence on the divine name — flag for the verbatim-QA pass.** The brief states
its text was "fetched fresh from ebible.org." In verification I found that ebible.org serves more
than one WEB edition at different paths, and my fetches returned **different divine-name renderings
from them**: `ebible.org/engwebp/` consistently returned "the LORD" (e.g. 2 Kings 22:13 "Go inquire
of **the LORD**…"), while `ebible.org/web/` and BibleGateway `version=WEB` consistently returned
"Yahweh" (2 Kings 22:13 "Go inquire of **Yahweh**…"). The wording is otherwise identical. **The
brief's readings match the "Yahweh" edition, which is the one the approved `medium` brief and
`docs/reviews/2-kings-review.md` used, so the brief is correct as written** — but the QA pass
should pin the exact edition and URL it treats as canonical, because a future fetch against the
wrong path would produce spurious "mismatches" on every verse containing the divine name. I am
stating this as an observed difference between fetches rather than a settled bibliographic claim
about the two editions.

**5.2 Quote nesting.** WEB prints inner speech with double quotes (2:9, 2:15, 22:8, 2:14); the
brief nests them as single quotes because each verse sits inside a quoted block. Correct brief
formatting, but the ingestion pipeline must store WEB's double quotes. Same note as
`docs/reviews/genesis-easy-review.md` §5.1 — for `docs/qa/2-kings-easy-verbatim-qa.md`, not a
review defect.

**5.3 Item 16's placement.** A level-2-shaped MC item sits in the boss set where `medium`'s ladder
specifies level 3–5 weighting. Appropriate for an easy tier and outside my remit; noting only so it
reads as deliberate.

**5.4 Distractor quality.** Item 5's "Babylon" and item 7's "Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon" are
strong distractors precisely because this book contains both empires. That is good pedagogy, not a
defect — recorded so it is not "fixed" later by someone reading it as a confusion risk.

own scope. Fix: reduce the bullet to "**2 Kings 2:14 (item 16)**" and drop the "hard tier"
parenthetical, or — if those verses are intended for a later hard tier — move the note there.

**6.2 — Items 14 and 15: inline the verbatim text. [required]**
`CONTENT_STYLE_GUIDE.md` §5 requires every challenge item, "with no exceptions," to carry
reference + translation + verbatim text where the challenge displays or requires recall of text.
Item 14 displays three quotations (2:9, 19:19, 22:8) and item 15 turns on the wording of 22:11, but
both cite by cross-reference to `docs/content/2-kings.md` rather than reproducing the text. §5 says
this is enforced technically at ingestion (`BACKEND_REQUIREMENTS.md` §4), so a cross-reference will
not satisfy the pipeline, and it leaves the verbatim-QA pass without a self-contained artifact.
Paste the WEB text inline. (Item 13 is pure scene-sequencing with no verse text and is exempt.)
Same finding as `genesis-easy-review.md` §6.6 — worth noting the pattern is recurring across
difficulty-tier briefs and should probably become a drafting checklist item.

**6.3 — Item 7 (18:13): correct the bounding sentence and add a structural rule. [required]**
The brief currently says the item "grades only what the text itself states (who invaded, **and
when**, per the text's own regnal-year framing)." Delete "and when." Per §4.2, the "fourteenth
year" is the *actual* disputed element here — Barnes calls it "hopelessly at variance with the
Assyrian dates" and suggests it "crept in from the margin"; the Pulpit Commentary says accepting it
as genuine requires "rejecting altogether the authority of the Assyrian inscriptions." Replace with
an explicit structural rule in the style of the Genesis 3:15 / 1:27 restrictions: **the "fourteenth
year" may be displayed as verse text but must never be the graded token or a multiple-choice
subject in any tier.** The current item is safe; this keeps a future pass from writing the
fill-in-the-blank that wouldn't be.

**6.4 — Items 6, 8 and 11: anchor the question stems. [recommended]**
The Genesis-easy pilot's only substantive defect was an unanchored stem with a second defensible
answer (`genesis-easy-review.md` §6.1). I found no live ambiguity here — in each case the
alternative reading is not among the offered distractors — but three stems are unanchored where the
brief's own better items (5, 7, 10, 15) are anchored:
- **Item 6** "What did Hezekiah ask Yahweh to do?" — Hezekiah prays again in 2 Kings 20:2-3, for
  his own life. No distractor offers healing, so the key is safe today. Anchor as "According to
  2 Kings 19:19, …".
- **Item 8** "What happened to the Assyrian army that night?" — "that night" is only meaningful
  with the verse in view. Anchor to 19:35-36.
- **Item 11** "What did Nebuzaradan do to Jerusalem?" — the sharper case. **Nebuzaradan is named in
  25:8, not in the displayed 25:9 text**, so the stem supplies a subject the player cannot see; and
  25:10 attributes breaking down the walls to the army "who were with the captain of the guard,"
  i.e. also Nebuzaradan, which is a second true answer to the stem as written (not currently
  offered as a distractor, and item 12 grades it separately). Anchor as "According to 2 Kings 25:9,
  what did Nebuzaradan burn in Jerusalem?" — this also removes the last trace of the `medium`
  brief's 25:8-9 block dependency.

**6.5 — Trim marking: apply the ellipsis convention consistently. [recommended]**
