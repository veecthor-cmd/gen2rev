## 3. Contested-Territory Cross-Check

### Item 8 — Song of Solomon 8:6's "a very flame of Yah," graded for the first time in this book's content
This is the item this review scrutinized most closely, since `medium`'s review (§3.4) explicitly
flagged this clause as a live scholarly dispute — whether Hebrew *shalhebetyah* contains a
truncated form of the divine name YHWH (which would make this the book's only reference to God) or
is simply an intensifying superlative — and every prior item in this book's content (`medium`,
`easy`) only ever displayed this clause without grading it. This brief's item 8 is the first to make
"Yah" the graded answer.

Re-checked the underlying dispute directly: [TheTorah.com, "YHWH's Flame: A Love Metaphor in the
Song of Songs"](https://www.thetorah.com/article/yhwhs-flame-a-love-metaphor-in-the-song-of-songs)
(already cited in the `medium` review, re-confirmed live) lays out both positions and confirms this
remains unresolved among scholars — not a settled question either direction. Given that, the
question is not whether the dispute is real (it is, and `medium` already established this) but
whether *grading recall of the bare word* crosses a line the display-only treatment didn't. Applying
the same reasoning validated in this same review pass for Ecclesiastes' analogous "eternity"/*olam*
case (`docs/reviews/ecclesiastes-hard-review.md` §3): grading verbatim recall of a contested word,
never via multiple-choice, never asking what it means or whether it's correctly translated, is
in-bounds per `docs/CONTENT_STYLE_GUIDE.md` §2 and does not require the player to take a side.
**Checked item 8's actual construction against this bar: it is fill-in-the-blank recall only ("Its
flashes are flashes of fire, a very flame of ___" → Yah), no distractor options exist (this tier has
no MC items), and no surrounding text in the item or its display asks what "Yah" refers to or
whether the verse names God. Complies with the structural rule the brief itself states.** No change
needed.
