## 3. Contested-Territory Cross-Check

### Korah's rebellion — fate of his household (Num 16:31-32 vs. 26:11)
Checked against Jewish tradition sources on why Korah's sons survived:
[Sefaria — commentary on Numbers 26:11](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Numbers.26.11) (Rashi,
Targum Jonathan, Or HaChaim, Rabbeinu Bahya, retrieved via API). All commentaries agree the sons
survived because they did not actively participate / repented before the ground opened, and that
Or HaChaim reads the verse as establishing that "punishment is limited to actual offenders, not the
entire family." Rabbeinu Bahya connects their survival to their later role as Temple musicians
(the Korahite psalms). **This is not a live cross-tradition dispute** — Jewish commentary is
essentially unanimous on this point, and it isn't contested in Christian tradition either since the
same text (26:11) is uncontroversial. **The brief's bounding holds and its inclusion of 26:11
alongside 16:31-32 is correct and worth keeping** — it prevents a challenge item from asserting
something (that Korah's sons died with him) the text itself later corrects. No change needed beyond
what's already in the brief.

### The bronze serpent's theological status (Num 21:8-9)
Checked Jewish and Christian readings, which diverge genuinely:
- **Jewish tradition:** [Mishnah Rosh Hashanah 3:8](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Mishnah_Rosh_Hashanah.3.8)
  (retrieved via Sefaria API) — the serpent itself "had no inherent power"; healing came from Israel
  "turning their eyes upward and subjecting their hearts to their Father in Heaven." The object is
  explicitly de-emphasized in favor of devotion.
- **Christian tradition:** [Bible Hub commentary aggregation on Num 21:9](https://biblehub.com/commentaries/numbers/21-9.htm)
  — near-unanimous typological reading connecting the bronze serpent to Christ's crucifixion (citing
  John 3:14-15, NT, out of MVP scope), with commentators (Ellicott, Keil and Delitzsch, citing
  Luther) drawing detailed form/elevation/faith-response parallels.

**Genuine, confirmed divergence.** The brief's bounding — challenge items scoped strictly to Numbers
21's own narrative (what Moses made, what it did), with no claim about the object's typological or
later status — is the correct scope given how differently these two traditions read the object's
*meaning*. No change needed to challenge content; the brief already flags this correctly.

### Balaam's overall characterization (Num 22-24 vs. the rest of his story)
Checked both traditions:
- **Jewish tradition:** genuinely ambivalent.
  [Pirkei Avot 5:19](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Pirkei_Avot.5.19) contrasts "disciples of
  Balaam" (evil eye, haughty spirit) unfavorably with "disciples of Abraham," while Balaam's
  blessing ("How fair are your tents, O Jacob") is recited in Jewish liturgy — a documented "mixture
  of positive and negative press" ([My Jewish Learning](https://www.myjewishlearning.com/article/balaam-the-prophet/)).
- **Christian tradition:** predominantly negative, citing 2 Peter 2:15-16 and Jude 1:11 (NT, out of
  MVP scope) which explicitly condemn Balaam ([GotQuestions](https://www.gotquestions.org/Balaam-donkey.html)).

**Genuine divergence in overall characterization, though not really a "graded item" risk** since the
brief's chosen scenes (the donkey speaking, the angel, the 23:19 oracle) are narrated favorably by
the text itself in Numbers 22-24 regardless of how later tradition or later chapters (Num 31:16,
outside this brief) treat him. The brief correctly scopes to only the Numbers 22-24 material and
explicitly notes it makes no claim about his overall character. No change needed.

### Numbers 23:19 — "God is not a man, that he should lie... repent" (newly identified this pass)
Not originally flagged with this specificity when the brief was first drafted; identified during
this review as analogous to the Genesis review's discovery of the Genesis 1:27 issue. This verse
(and its near-twin in 1 Samuel 15:29, outside MVP scope) is the classic OT prooftext for divine
immutability/impassibility, and sits in real tension with OT passages describing God "repenting" or
relenting (Genesis 6:6, Exodus 32:14 — outside this book). Checked
[GotQuestions on open theism](https://www.gotquestions.org/open-theism.html), which confirms this
exact tension (open theism vs. classical theism) is a live, current debate among mainstream
Evangelicals, treating the apparent-repentance verses as anthropomorphic language against verses
affirming complete foreknowledge. **Confirmed as a genuine live theological debate the verse sits
within**, even though the source consulted didn't cite Num 23:19 by chapter/verse specifically.

**Recommendation:** same structural treatment as Genesis 3:15/1:27 — restrict this verse to
recall/fill-in-blank format only, never multiple-choice "what does this verse mean/imply about
God's nature." Applied directly to `docs/content/numbers.md` as part of this review.

whether the judgment was justified. Applied directly to `docs/content/numbers.md`.
