## 3. Contested-Territory Cross-Check

### 3.1 Re-confirming the two full exclusions (1:2-3, 2:10-16)

Every item's citation (1:6, 1:7, 3:1, 3:3, 3:9, 3:17, 4:3, 3:6, 4:5, the reused 3-quote sequence, and
4:6) falls within the 6 already-approved passages, none of which touch 1:2-3 or 2:10-16. **Mechanical
scope check — passes.**

### 3.2 Malachi 3:1 (item 3) — re-confirming the identity-neutral bounding, new clause checked

`Medium` blanked "way" (what the messenger will prepare). This item blanks "desire" — the people's
own stated longing for "the messenger of the covenant." Confirmed this is still identity-neutral: the
graded word describes the people's attitude, not any claim about who the messenger, the Lord, or the
messenger of the covenant are. Sits inside the exact bounding already established in
`docs/reviews/malachi-review.md` §3.3. **Bounding holds.**

### 3.3 Malachi 3:3 (item 4) — re-confirming the identity-neutral bounding

Grades only the stated material outcome ("gold and silver") of the refining process, not any claim
about who performs it. Same bounding as §3.2. **Holds.**

### 3.4 Malachi 3:6 (item 8) and 3:9 (item 5) — re-confirming the recall-only bounding, and the new no-divine-name-blank rule

Item 8 grades "change" (Yahweh's stated unchangingness) rather than "consumed" (`medium`'s blank) or
"Yahweh" (never used as a blank anywhere in this brief, per the structural rule stated in §2 above and
confirmed applied). Item 5 grades "nation" — the stated scope of the charge ("this whole nation"),
not a present-day application claim. Both sit inside the exact recall-only/no-modern-application
bounding already established in `docs/reviews/malachi-review.md` §3.4. **Bounding holds for both,
and the new divine-name-blanking rule is independently confirmed sound: blanking "Yahweh" itself in a
verse about Yahweh's own unchanging nature would risk reading as though the challenge is testing or
implying something about the divine name/identity rather than simple verse recall — avoiding that is
a reasonable, conservative choice, consistent with this project's general practice of not blanking
names/pronouns that could carry more theological weight than the surrounding narrative fact.**

### 3.5 Malachi 4:5 (item 9) and 4:6 (item 11) — re-confirming the no-fulfillment-claim bounding, boss-capstone placement checked

Item 9 blanks "prophet" (not "Elijah," which `medium` already used) — the name "Elijah" remains
present as plain text in the item's displayed verse, exactly as the text states it, with the graded
token being the title "prophet." Item 11 blanks "curse" — the verse's own stated consequence
("...lest I come and strike the earth with a curse"), the literal final word of Malachi and, in this
product's canonical order, of the Old Testament. Neither item asserts, implies, or asks about
fulfillment, timing, or identity beyond what the text states. Both sit inside the exact bounding
already established in `docs/reviews/malachi-review.md` §3.6, including that review's explicit
endorsement of 4:4-6 as "both the most textually natural choice and... safely bounded" for a closing
capstone. **Bounding holds for both items, and their use as this brief's boss/capstone material is
confirmed to carry forward the medium tier's own reviewed rationale for that choice, not a new
editorial decision requiring fresh justification.**

### 3.6 Remaining new items (2, 6, 7) — independently checked

- **Malachi 1:7 (item 2)** — "Yahweh's table is contemptible," the priests' own dismissive
  characterization, quoted directly. Plain narrative fact (what the priests said), part of the
  already-cleared 1:6-11 passage. **No concern.**
- **Malachi 3:17 (item 6)** — "my own possession," Yahweh's stated claim on those who feared him.
  Covenantal language, not doctrinally loaded beyond what the plain text states; part of the
  already-cleared 3:13-18 passage. **No concern.**
- **Malachi 4:3 (item 7)** — "ashes under the soles of your feet," part of the already-cleared 4:1-3
  passage, purely descriptive judgment imagery with the same no-identity-claim bounding already
  applied to that passage in `docs/reviews/malachi-review.md` §3.5. **No concern.**
