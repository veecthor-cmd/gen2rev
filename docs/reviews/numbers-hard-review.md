### Finding A — item 2 (Num 1:47): non-verbatim text **and** an answer key absent from the verse. **BLOCKING.**

The brief renders item 2 as:

> "But the Levites after the tribe of their fathers were not **numbered** among them." — recall:
> "…were not ___ among them." → **numbered**

**Four independent live WEB retrievals all read "counted", not "numbered":**

- ebible.org `/web/NUM01.htm` — "But the Levites after the tribe of their fathers were not **counted**
  among them."
- ebible.org `/engwebp/NUM01.htm` — same, "counted".
- BibleGateway `version=WEB` — "47 But the Levites after the tribe of their fathers were not
  **counted** among them."
- BibleHub's WEB text for Numbers 1 — explicitly "counted", not "numbered".

"Numbered" is the **KJV/ASV** reading of this verse
([KJV, Num 1:47](https://biblehub.com/kjv/numbers/1-47.htm)), which is almost certainly where the
wording came from. (A general web search for this verse returns overwhelmingly KJV hits and its
auto-summary confidently but wrongly reports "numbered" as the WEB reading — a good illustration of
why the per-source retrievals above, not a search summary, are the evidence here.)

This fails two style-guide rules at once:

1. **§3, final bullet / `CLAUDE.md` content-integrity guardrail** — text presented in quotation marks
   as WEB verse text that is not WEB verse text.
2. **§5** — the verbatim text carried by the item is wrong, and worse, **the graded answer key
   ("numbered") is a word that does not occur anywhere in WEB Numbers 1:47.** A player who correctly
   recalls the displayed WEB verse would be marked wrong.

**Concrete fix (preferred):** change both the quoted text and the answer key to "counted":
"But the Levites after the tribe of their fathers were not ___ among them." → **counted**. The item
is otherwise sound and stays in-bounds; this is a text-integrity fix, not a content one.

Two process notes worth recording, because they are the reusable lesson:

- Item 2's own parenthetical claims it is safer than a multi-verse cut because it "quotes exactly one
  complete, self-contained verse." That is true structurally — and it is exactly the item whose text
  is wrong. Avoiding the *previous* pilot's failure mode does not confer immunity from a different
  one.
- The brief's header caveat states that "every newly-introduced verse below was retrieved via a live
  WebFetch call against ebible.org (WEB) on 2026-08-19." **That claim does not hold for 1:47.**
  Whatever produced this string was not the live ebible.org text. The caveat should not be treated as
  evidence of retrieval for any verse in future briefs, and the verbatim-QA pass
  (`QA_REQUIREMENTS.md` §3) should re-check *all twelve* items rather than only the ones flagged
  here.

The full WEB verse ([ebible.org NUM16](https://ebible.org/web/NUM16.htm)) continues:

The quoted words are verbatim, so this is not paraphrase — but presenting part of a verse as *the*

the cleaner option here, or move the blank to "who is ___" → **holy**… except that collides with
`medium`'s boss blank. **Recommendation: keep the current blank, add the "(WEB, trimmed)" label.**

### 4.1 Numbers 24:17 — "a star out of Jacob". **Dispute confirmed as real and live. Bounding HOLDS. One claim needs correcting; one new guard needed.**

The drafter's claim that this is a genuine cross-tradition dispute is **correct, and if anything
understated** — the disagreement is live *within* the Christian commentary tradition as well as
between traditions.

**Classic Christian commentators are divided among themselves**
([Bible Hub, Numbers 24:17 commentaries](https://biblehub.com/commentaries/numbers/24-17.htm)):

- **Messianic reading** — Ellicott ("A star is a fitting image of an illustrious king"), Matthew
  Henry, Gill, and the Cambridge Bible.
- **David as the primary referent, Christ as ultimate** — Barnes and Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; the
  Pulpit Commentary holds the prophecy was "partially, or typically, fulfilled in the time of David."
- **Both, without adjudicating** — Keil & Delitzsch, who read it of a ruler who would "destroy all
  the enemies of Israel," applied both historically and eschatologically.
- Gill also documents the **Bar Kokhba** connection the brief cites, noting that "a false Messiah took
  the name of Bar Cochab."

**Jewish tradition** ([Rashi on Numbers 24:17, via Sefaria](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Rashi_on_Numbers.24.17?context=0)):
Rashi reads "a star comes out of Jacob" as **"a king will arise from Jacob"** and ties it explicitly
to **David**, citing 2 Samuel 8:2 (David striking Moab). He glosses *shevet* as "a king, who chastises
with the rod and has sovereign power," and stresses that the fulfilment "is not now… but will be
after some time." **Rashi does not mention the Messiah, and does not mention Bar Kokhba.**

**So a multiple-choice item of the shape "who is the star?" would be flatly out-of-bounds** under
§3 (denominational position) — the brief is right to pre-empt it, and the recall-only rule is the
correct instrument.

**Why the bounding actually holds, not merely plausibly holds:** the graded token is "star," and
that word is rendered identically across the traditions that disagree about its referent. WEB: "A
star will come out of Jacob." The Revised JPS (2023), via
[Sefaria, Numbers 24:17](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Numbers.24.17?context=0): "**A star** rises
from Jacob, / A scepter comes forth from Israel." The Hebrew is כּוֹכָב (*kokab*, H3556), whose base
gloss is simply "star," with a secondary figurative sense "prince/ruler"
([Blue Letter Bible H3556](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/h3556/kjv/wlc/0-1/)). A player
typing "star" affirms nothing a Jewish, Catholic, Orthodox or Protestant reader would dispute.
**Bounding confirmed sufficient; item 8 needs no change.**

**Correction required to the brief's Contested Territory text.** The brief asserts that "broader
rabbinic tradition also read[s] it messianically of a future Davidic king." The single most-consulted
rabbinic commentator on this verse reads it of **David historically**, not messianically. The
Akiva/Bar Kokhba application is real (Gill attests it, and it is why Bar Koseba was called *Bar
Kokhba*, "son of a star") but **I did not confirm the Akiva attribution against a primary rabbinic
source in this pass.** The brief should either (a) soften to "some strands of Jewish tradition applied
it messianically, most famously in the Bar Kokhba episode; Rashi reads it of David," or (b) cite a
primary source for the Akiva claim. Stating this plainly rather than letting the brief's synthesis
stand is the point.

**New guard required — the verse's second half, which the brief did not notice.** The clause the
brief displays but does not analyse is textually contested in a way directly parallel to the Gen
22:13 אַחַר/אֶחָד finding at `docs/reviews/genesis-easy-review.md` §4.1:

- WEB: "…and shall **strike through the corners of Moab**, and **crush all the sons of Sheth**."
- Revised JPS: "It **smashes the brow of Moab**, / **The foundation of all children of Seth**."
- Bible Hub's commentators split on "sons of Sheth": the traditional reading takes Sheth as Adam's
  son, making the phrase mean *all humanity* (which commentators themselves concede "seems harsh"),
  while **Cambridge and Keil & Delitzsch prefer emending to "sons of tumult/confusion,"** i.e. the
  Moabite warriors specifically, citing the parallel in Jeremiah 48:45.

Nothing currently grades this — the blank is "star," in the first line. But this is a live emendation
dispute sitting inside displayed text. **Add to Contested Territory:** no challenge in any tier may
blank or grade "Moab," "corners," or "Sheth," or ask who or what the sons of Sheth are; the second
half of 24:17 is display-only.

### 4.2 Numbers 16:46-48 — Aaron's atonement. **Drafter's claim CONFIRMED. Two things the brief missed.**

The drafter's core claim — that this extends rather than introduces divine-judgment ground, and that
the inherited Korah rule covers it without a new rule — **is correct.** The item grades "dead," a
narrative fact; no item asks why the plague began, whether it was deserved, or what the atonement
accomplished. The narrative framing in the brief also checks out against the text: WEB 16:41 does
read "But on the next day all the congregation of the children of Israel complained against Moses and
against Aaron, saying, 'You have killed Yahweh's people!'"
([ebible.org NUM16](https://ebible.org/web/NUM16.htm)), so "a second rebellion the day after Korah's"
is accurate, not an inference.

**And the graded token survives the cross-tradition test the same way "star" does.** The Revised JPS
renders the clause "**he stood between the dead and the living** until the plague was checked"
([Sefaria, Numbers 17:11-13](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Numbers.17.11-13?context=0)) — the
graded word "dead" is identical to WEB's. That is what makes the bounding hold.

**Missed item (a) — atonement language. Recommended new rule.** WEB reads "made **atonement** for the
people"; the Revised JPS reads "made **expiation** for the people." Style guide §3 names "specific
soteriological framing of sacrifice/atonement language" as an explicit out-of-bounds category — this
is the first time in the Numbers content set that atonement vocabulary appears inside a graded item's
displayed text. The current item is safe (the blank is "dead," two clauses away), but per §4's
"flag it deliberately rather than let it surface after ingestion," the brief should record:
**"atonement"/"made atonement" is display-only in Num 16:46-48 — never the graded token, never a
multiple-choice subject, and no item may ask what Aaron's atonement accomplished or how it worked.**
Same pattern as the Gen 1:2 *ruach elohim* recommendation.

**Missed item (b) — versification. Recommended note, not a defect.** In the Hebrew (Masoretic) and
Jewish versification, this passage is **Numbers 17:11-13, not 16:46-48**: Sefaria returns exactly the
censer/plague text at
[Numbers 17:11-13](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Numbers.17.11-13?context=0), because the Hebrew
chapter 16 ends at v35 and what English Bibles number 16:36-50 is Hebrew 17:1-15. (Corroborated by
reference material on Hebrew/English chapter-and-verse divergence, e.g.
[JewishEncyclopedia, "Verse-Division"](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/14682-verse-division).)
The WEB citation "Numbers 16:46-48" is **correct for WEB** and nothing needs to change in the item.
But the brief justifies item 12 as "extending the Korah scene to its immediate, related aftermath
**later in the same chapter**" — which is true in WEB and false in the Hebrew, where it is a
different chapter. Reword that justification, and record the versification divergence so a future
authoring pass doesn't build a cross-reference item that silently assumes one numbering system.

### 4.3 Numbers 14:9 — "they are bread for us". **Drafter's flag CONFIRMED; the item is in-bounds, but this is more contested than the brief realised.**

The drafter flagged this "for awareness only… not because a moral-judgment item is built from it."
**I confirm no moral-judgment item is built from it:** item 4 is fill-in-the-blank on a single word,
and no item anywhere in this brief asks the player to characterise the land's inhabitants, evaluate
Caleb and Joshua's rhetoric, or take any position on the conquest. §3's moral-judgment prohibition is
clear.

**But the brief under-describes the issue.** It calls "bread" a "somewhat vivid idiom." In fact the
divergence lands **directly on the graded token**:

- WEB: "for they are **bread** for us."
- Revised JPS (2023), via [Sefaria, Numbers 14:9](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Numbers.14.9?context=0):
  "for they are **our prey**; their protection has departed from them, but God is with us."
- Hebrew: כִּי לַחְמֵנוּ הֵם (*ki lachmenu hem*) — the word is the ordinary noun for bread/food, which
  is why translations split between a literal "bread" and an idiomatic "prey"/"food"/"we will devour
  them."

This is unlike §4.1 and §4.2, where the graded word was identical across traditions. Here the graded
word is translation-specific.

**The item is nonetheless in-bounds, for a specific reason worth stating:** it is fill-in-the-blank
against *displayed WEB text*, so the player is completing a sentence in front of them, not asserting
that "bread" is the right rendering of *lachmenu*. WEB is fixed as the MVP translation by Open
Decision #1, and §5 requires exactly this — reference, translation, verbatim text. **No change to
item 4.**

**New guard recommended:** record that **"bread" in Num 14:9 may only ever be graded as a
fill-in-the-blank against displayed WEB text — never as a multiple-choice answer** (e.g. "what did
Caleb and Joshua call the people of the land?" with options bread / prey / food / grasshoppers),
because that format would grade a translation choice rather than displayed text. Also record that
the clause "their defense is removed from over them" and any characterisation of the land's
inhabitants are not challenge-ready in any tier.

## 7. Verdict

**`approved-with-changes`.**

The brief is structurally sound: it introduces no new scenes, honours every inherited format
restriction, contains no multiple choice at all (so the "no MC" rules cannot be breached), keeps
Numbers 25 fully excluded, duplicates no blank from the `easy` or `medium` tiers, and — the specific
thing this review was asked to check hardest — **commits no verse-boundary splice in any of its four
multi-verse cuts.** The Genesis pilot's Finding A defect class was searched for directly and is
absent.

All three contested-territory items the drafter flagged were checked against real, live sources
representing genuinely different traditions, and **all three boundings hold.** In two cases (24:17,
16:46-48) they hold for a reason the brief did not state and that is worth recording: the graded
token turns out to be a word that the disagreeing traditions render *identically* — "star" is "star"
in both WEB and the Revised JPS despite total disagreement about its referent; "the dead" is "the
dead" in both despite the divergence between "atonement" and "expiation" one clause earlier. In the
third case (14:9) the graded word *does* diverge across translations, and the item survives for a
different reason — it is a fill-in-the-blank against displayed WEB text, not a choice between
renderings — which is exactly why the new MC guard in §6.6 matters.

The blocking defect is textual, not doctrinal: **Numbers 1:47 in the WEB reads "counted", not
"numbered"**, confirmed against four independent live retrievals, and the item's answer key is
currently a word absent from the verse. That is a straightforward fix, but it is a real one, and it
would have shipped a permanently-unanswerable challenge.

**Not escalated.** The disagreements found are genuine and live, but none of them bears on a graded
answer key once the guards in §6 are recorded.

**Source gap, stated plainly rather than papered over:** I could not reach a Catholic (USCCB returned
403), Orthodox, or Enduring Word source on this pass; those sites refused the request. The

is more than two genuinely distinct traditions and is sufficient for the conclusions drawn — in each

strengthen but not overturn. A Catholic/Orthodox check on Num 24:17's messianic reading would be
worth adding whenever those sources are reachable; I would expect it to confirm rather than change
the §4.1 conclusion, but I have not verified that and am not asserting it.
