## 3. Contested-Territory Cross-Check

### 3.1 Whole-book interpretive framing: allegorical vs. literal reading

This is the item the assignment specifically asked to be checked against sources spanning both
readings, and it is the brief's own headline Contested Territory item. Sources consulted,
representing genuinely different traditions and eras:

- **Jewish tradition (allegorical, historic):** [My Jewish Learning, "Song of Songs as
  Allegory"](https://www.myjewishlearning.com/article/song-of-songs-as-allegory/) and
  [jewishideas.org, "The Love of Song of Songs"](https://www.jewishideas.org/article/love-song-songs)
  both confirm Rabbi Akiva's Mishnaic-era declaration that "the Song of Songs is the Holy of
  Holies," and that for many centuries the predominant tendency in Jewish exegesis read the book
  allegorically, as depicting the history of God's love for Israel. Medieval Jewish tradition
  layered pshat (plain sense: two lovers in dialogue), drash (allegory of God/Israel, Akiva's
  preferred reading), and remez (philosophical/moral teaching) as coexisting, not competing, levels
  — i.e. even within Jewish tradition the literal and allegorical readings were held together, not
  resolved in favor of one.
- **Christian tradition (allegorical/typological, historic):** [Logos/Fellipe do Vale, "Is the Song
  Allegorical?"](https://www.logos.com/grow/witw-song-of-solomon/) and
  [The Gospel Coalition, "Is the Song of Songs About Sex or Jesus?"](https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/article/song-songs-sex-or-jesus/)
  confirm the allegorical approach (Origen in the early church, Bernard of Clairvaux in the
  medieval period, reading the book as Christ's love for the Church or the soul) prevailed as the
  dominant Christian reading until the 19th century.
- **Modern/literal reading (cross-tradition, contemporary):** The same Gospel Coalition source and
  [Logos](https://www.logos.com/grow/witw-song-of-solomon/) confirm that 19th-century
  historical-critical scholarship shifted emphasis toward the Song's parallels with other ancient
  Near Eastern love poetry and its literal, human-romantic-love content — a reading now widely held
  across traditions, not confined to secular scholarship.
- **A genuine third position, not just two poles:** Both sources describe a contemporary
  "figural" or "typological" reading that treats the human-love content as real and primary while
  also holding it as inherently pointing toward covenantal/divine love — explicitly framed as a
  deliberate alternative to choosing allegory *or* literal reading. [The Gospel Coalition](https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/article/song-songs-sex-or-jesus/)
  states plainly: "After more than twenty centuries, there is still no consensus."

**Finding: this is a genuine, live, unresolved disagreement**, cross-tradition (Jewish
allegorical/multi-level vs. historic Christian allegorical/typological vs. modern
literal/historical-critical) and enduring across two millennia, not a settled question modern
scholarship has closed. Unlike Genesis 22 or Genesis 3:15 — where the *narrative content* ("what
happened," "what the text says") was cleanly separable from the *interpretive* disagreement about
what it means — here the disagreement is about what the book fundamentally *is*, and that
question cannot be avoided by narrowing to "safe" verses. The brief's neutral Setting/Era language
("this brief takes no position... coherent under either reading") is the right instinct and the
best available narrative-only-style move, but this reviewer notes a limitation the brief itself
can't fully resolve: **describing the book at all, in any language a general-audience game can use,
requires some framing choice**, and no framing is fully neutral between "this is fundamentally a
human love poem" and "this is fundamentally about divine love expressed through human-love
imagery." The brief's current language leans toward presenting both without picking, which is the
best available mitigation, but this reviewer is not fully confident it closes the gap the way the
recall-only structural rule closed the Genesis 1:26/3:15 gap — because there the *graded token*
was interpretation-free even though the *surrounding word* was contested, whereas here the
book's *identity itself*, not a single pronoun or clause, is what's contested.

### 3.2 The refrain's context boundary (2:6/3:4/8:3 vs. 2:7/3:5/8:4)

Confirmed via both live sources (§1 above) that the embrace description ("His left hand is under
my head...") sits in the verse immediately preceding each refrain occurrence, and that the brief's
challenge-ready text starts cleanly at the refrain verse itself. **The brief's boundary holds** —
no challenge-ready text touches the preceding verse. No change needed.

### 3.3 Song of Solomon 2:16's excluded second clause

Confirmed via mainstream commentary that "he browses among the lilies" (2:16, repeated in 6:2-3)
carries a widely-attested euphemistic reading for physical intimacy:
[Enduring Word on Song of Solomon 2](https://enduringword.com/bible-commentary/song-of-solomon-2/)
and [Together in Christ](https://www.heartlight.org/togetherinchrist/0407.html) both discuss this
reading directly, with Enduring Word noting the parallel to lips being called "lilies" elsewhere in
the book (5:13). **The brief's trim (to the verse's first sentence only) is correctly scoped** and
matches the sentence boundary exactly — no mid-sentence splice. No change needed.

### 3.4 Song of Solomon 8:6's "a very flame of Yah"

Confirmed as a genuine scholarly (textual/philological, not denominational) dispute:
[TheTorah.com, "YHWH's Flame: A Love Metaphor in the Song of Songs"](https://www.thetorah.com/article/yhwhs-flame-a-love-metaphor-in-the-song-of-songs)
lays out both the reading that *shalhebetyah* contains a truncated divine name (making this the
book's only reference to God) and the reading that "-yah" is an intensifying superlative with no
theological content. **This does not affect the brief's bounding** — the challenge item grades
recall of the verbatim WEB clause only, never asks what "Yah" refers to, and the brief's Contested
Territory section already documents this so a future authoring pass doesn't build an interpretive
item on top of it. No change needed.

### 3.5 Excluded material: chapters 1:2, 4, 6, 7

Spot-checked (not re-verified verse-by-verse, since none of this text appears in the brief) that
these are in fact the book's body-description poems and opening physical-intimacy line — the
assignment's own framing of what to exclude matches how this material is broadly discussed in
commentary (see the euphemism sources in §3.3, which reference 4:5, 5:13, and 6:2-3 as part of the
same descriptive-poem pattern). **Confirmed the brief's exclusion is complete** — no verse from
these chapters appears anywhere in `docs/content/song-of-solomon.md`. No change needed.

### 3.6 General-audience / child-safety distribution risk

This is the item this reviewer is least confident resolving alone, and the primary driver of the
verdict below. [Ministry-to-Children's Song of Solomon curriculum listing](https://ministry-to-children.com/curriculum/bible/song-of-solomon/)
confirms that children's-ministry resources for this book exist, but — consistent with what its
own description says — they work by redirecting to broad, one-step-removed themes ("God's love is
strong, joyful, kind, and faithful") rather than by presenting the book's own verses as
recall/quiz content the way this product's challenge format requires for every other book. That is
a meaningfully different content strategy than this brief's (verbatim-verse recall and sequencing),
and this reviewer could not find a directly comparable precedent — a general-audience,
verbatim-verse-recall game treatment of Song of Solomon — to check the brief's approach against.
Combined with `PRODUCT_REQUIREMENTS.md` §2's confirmed distribution model (guest play, no signup,
no age gate, links shared informally including plausibly into family group chats), this reviewer
is not confident that narrow verse-level bounding — however clean on the style guide's own
graded-item terms (see §2 above, which does pass) — fully answers whether a "world" thematically
organized around romantic love belongs in this product's general-audience MVP at all, independent
of any single verse's content.

## 4. Verdict

**`escalate-to-human`** — for the brief as a whole, not one isolated passage. This is not a
rejection: on every graded-item, style-guide-compliance, and verse-text-accuracy check performed
above, the brief passes cleanly. The escalation is specifically about §3.1 (whole-book
interpretive framing, which no amount of verse-level bounding fully resolves) and §3.6
(general-audience distribution risk given the book's inherent romantic-love subject matter, even
under strict, careful scene/passage selection).

**Reasoning:** Per `.claude/agents/theological-reviewer.md` step 7(b) and (d) — the content
intersects the child-safety/general-audience guardrail in a way this reviewer isn't fully
confident adjudicating alone, and the whole-book framing question doesn't sit right as a purely
self-resolved matter given how squarely `docs/CANON_STRUCTURE.md` itself flagged this book by name
as needing real attention rather than a rubber stamp. This mirrors the Joshua escalation's logic
(`docs/reviews/joshua-review.md`) — there, the reviewer found narrow verse-level exclusion
insufficient to fully resolve a whole-scene ethical question; here, narrow verse-level bounding is
judged insufficient to fully resolve a whole-book identity/audience question, for a structurally
similar reason: part of what's contested (what kind of book this is; whether its subject matter
fits this distribution model) isn't reducible to any single graded item's wording.

**Recommended interim paths (not a substitute for the escalation, offered so Kachi has concrete
options rather than an open-ended question):**

1. **Ship the brief as scoped** (the 4 passages above, with the neutral both-readings framing in
   Setting/Era) — recommended if Kachi judges the book's inclusion, handled this carefully, a
   worthwhile and appropriate part of a full-canon-literacy product. This is this reviewer's
   preferred option if the escalation resolves toward inclusion, since the four passages already
   represent close to the safest, least-contested material the book contains.
2. **Ship a narrower version** — Passage 4 (Love Strong as Death, 8:6-7) only, generically framed
   as "a poem about love's power" with the book's specific romantic-love identity de-emphasized in
   the world's flavor text. Reduces the general-audience concern further at the cost of a
   noticeably thinner world (parallel to how 1 Chronicles' brief was deliberately left thin, per
   `docs/CANON_STRUCTURE.md` §6).
3. **Drop Song of Solomon from the MVP-expansion's playable-world list entirely**, logged as a
   scope decision in `docs/CONTENT_REVIEW_LOG.md` rather than treated as a silently-abandoned
   brief. None of the other 24 books in this expansion carry this book's specific combination of
   whole-book interpretive contest and inherent romantic-love subject matter, so this would not set
   a precedent requiring re-litigation elsewhere.

**Known limitation, stated per the agent definition's own instruction:** this review was performed
by an AI agent standing in for the named human theological reviewer role (Kachi Okere, per Open
Decision #7's resolution recorded in `docs/reviews/joshua-review.md`'s addendum). Nothing here is
asserted as certain — a human reviewer, and Kachi specifically as someone who has already weighed
comparable calls on Joshua and 2 Samuel, may reasonably resolve this differently than any one of
the three paths above, including by proposing a fourth option this reviewer didn't consider.

**Outstanding, not this reviewer's job:** the independent verbatim-text QA pass required by

`SKILL.md` step 3. `docs/CONTENT_REVIEW_LOG.md` is to be updated by the calling/orchestrating
