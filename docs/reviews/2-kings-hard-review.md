## 2. Finding — 2 Kings 19:15 sits on a live WEB edition conflict. **Blocking.**

Item 6 renders the verse "Yahweh, the God of Israel, **enthroned above the cherubim**, you are the
God, even you alone, of all the kingdoms of the earth," and labels it "(WEB, already-approved
verse)". The approved `medium` brief renders the same verse "Yahweh, the God of Israel, **who are
enthroned above the cherubim**…". Those are not the same string, so the "already-approved verse"
label is not accurate as written.

I did not assume one of them was a typo. Four live WEB sources return **three different readings**:

| Source | Reading |
|---|---|
| [ebible.org/web 2KI19](https://ebible.org/web/2KI19.htm) | "Yahweh, the God of Israel, **enthroned above** the cherubim" |
| [ebible.org/engwebp 2KI19](https://ebible.org/engwebp/2KI19.htm) | "**LORD**, the God of Israel, **enthroned above** the cherubim" |
| [BibleGateway WEB](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2+Kings+17%3A7%2C18%3B+2+Kings+19%3A1%2C14-15&version=WEB) and [BibleHub WEB](https://biblehub.com/web/2_kings/19.htm) | "Yahweh, the God of Israel, **who are enthroned above** the cherubim" |
| [BibleStudyTools WEB](https://www.biblestudytools.com/web/2-kings/19-15.html) | "Yahweh, the God of Israel, **who sit [above]** the cherubim" |

So the drafter's text is defensible — it matches ebible.org's *current* WEB, which is what the brief
says it fetched. The `medium` brief matches an older printing that BibleGateway and BibleHub still
serve. This is the same class of edition drift already recorded at `docs/reviews/genesis-easy-review.md`
§1 for Gen 12:1 ("Get out of your country" → "Leave your country"), but here it has bitten: two
project documents now carry two different verbatim strings for one verse, and both are headed for
the same `challenge` table.

**A second, sharper edge on the same problem:** ebible.org's `engwebp` edition renders the divine
name **"the LORD"** where its `web` edition renders **"Yahweh"** — confirmed independently at
[2:14](https://ebible.org/engwebp/2KI02.htm) ("Where is the LORD, the God of Elijah?" vs
[ebible.org/web](https://ebible.org/web/2KI02.htm) "Where is Yahweh…") and at 19:14 ("the LORD's
house" vs "Yahweh's house"). The brief cites its source only as "ebible.org", which is not precise
enough to identify which of these it used. Every item in this brief renders "Yahweh", so it
evidently used `/web` — but the citation doesn't say so, and the Genesis reviews cite `engwebp`.
The divine-name rendering is not cosmetic: `docs/reviews/genesis-easy-review.md` §4.5 already
records it as genuinely cross-tradition sensitive (Jewish practice on vocalizing the Tetragrammaton;
the Catholic Church's 2008 liturgical directive). Picking an edition is therefore a product-owner
decision, not a copy-edit, and it is currently unpinned: Open Decision #1 names the *translation*
but not the *edition or printing date*.

**Doctrinally, item 6 is fine either way** — the graded token is "cherubim", which is identical in
all three printings above. This is a text-integrity and consistency defect, not a content one.

## 4. Contested-Territory Cross-Check

### 4.1 Items 4–5 (17:18, 17:7) — the fall of Israel's stated cause. **Bounding holds.**

The brief inherits `medium`'s bounding: recall-only, grading the stated word, never extending to
whether national calamity generally signals divine judgment. Checked across two traditions:

- **Jewish translation tradition (Sefaria, Revised JPS 2023)** renders 17:7 "This happened because
  the Israelites sinned against the ETERNAL their God, who had freed them from the land of Egypt,
  from the hand of Pharaoh king of Egypt. They worshiped other gods."
  [Sefaria API, II Kings 17:7](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/II_Kings.17.7?context=0)
- **Classic Protestant commentators (BibleHub aggregation on 17:18)** — Matthew Henry, Barnes, Gill,
  Pulpit, Ellicott, Keil-Delitzsch, Cambridge, JFB. Complete agreement that the stated cause is
  idolatry and covenant violation; Gill: idolatry was "nothing being more provoking to him".
  [biblehub.com/commentaries/2_kings/17-18.htm](https://biblehub.com/commentaries/2_kings/17-18.htm)

**Why the bounding actually holds rather than merely sounds plausible:** the causation statement is
the *narrator's own*, and the two traditions render it identically — the Revised JPS and the WEB
both say the fall happened *because* Israel sinned. A player typing "sinned" (item 5) or "Judah"
(item 4) affirms only what the text says about itself. Nothing in either item generalizes.

One divergence worth recording, which does not touch the graded tokens: **Keil-Delitzsch** stands
apart in pressing that "Judah also did not keep the commandments of the Lord and walked in the
statutes of Israel, and therefore had deserved rejection" — i.e. Judah's survival was not a verdict
on Judah's righteousness, a nuance the other commentators downplay. Since no item grades *why Judah
was spared*, this is out of scope; recording it so a future authoring pass doesn't wander in.

**Forward guard (recommended, §6.5):** all eight commentators found it necessary to gloss "there was
none left but the tribe of Judah only" as meaning *the kingdom* of Judah, not one literal tribe —
Matthew Henry notes "the greatest part of the tribe of Benjamin, with those of the tribes of Simeon
and Levi, who adhered to them"; the Cambridge Bible: "By the 'tribe of Judah' is meant the kingdom".
Item 4's typed recall of the text's own word is safe. But an MC item of the shape "which tribe was
left?" would have a factually contested key, and must not be built in any tier.

### 4.2 Items 1–3 (2:10, 2:12, 2:13) — Elijah/Elisha succession. **Bounding holds; one new guard.**

The brief asserts "no NT typology / no new ground". That holds, and a Jewish cross-check strengthens
rather than weakens it — but it also surfaces a genuine interpretive divergence the brief didn't
name.

- **Rashi on II Kings 2:12** reads "the chariots of Israel and its horsemen" as referring to
  **Elijah himself** — the prophet "who is better for Yisroel with his prayers than chariots and
  horsemen", i.e. his intercession is worth more to the nation than an army. Not the fiery chariot
  of v. 11 at all.
  [Sefaria API, Rashi on II Kings 2:12](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Rashi_on_II_Kings.2.12?context=0)

That is a substantively different referent from the plain-reading assumption most players would
make, and it is a live reading in a mainstream tradition. **The graded token is unaffected** — item
2 grades the word "Israel", which sits in the phrase identically under every reading, and item 3
grades "mantle", a physical object no tradition disputes. So the recall-only bounding does neutralize
it, which is exactly what the brief needed to be true.

**Forward guard (recommended, §6.5):** no challenge in any tier may ask what "the chariots of Israel
and its horsemen" *means* or *whom it refers to*. Same instrument as the Gen 1:26 "us/our" rule —
display-only phrase, never the graded subject, and never a multiple-choice stem.

### 4.3 Item 16 (25:11) and the 25:18-21 selection judgment. **Confirmed — the brief chose correctly.**

I verified the actual content of the three passages rather than accepting the brief's
characterization, via [BibleGateway WEB, 2 Kings 25:1-21](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2+Kings+25%3A1-21&version=WEB):

- **25:6-7** (already excluded at `medium`) — explicit: Zedekiah's sons killed before his eyes, then
  his eyes put out, bound in fetters. Graphic.
- **25:18-21** — the chief priest, second priest, doorkeepers, an officer, five royal advisers, a
  scribe and sixty others are taken to Riblah, where "the king of Babylon attacked them and put them
  to death." Less graphic in detail than 25:6-7, but still a mass execution.
- **25:11** — deportation only. No violence narrated.

**The brief's stated judgment call holds**, and it is consistent with `medium`'s choice of 25:8-10
over 25:6-7. The stronger point for the record: 25:18-21 is not merely "less good", it is the
*nearest* remaining graphic passage to the material this tier does use, which is precisely why the
brief was right to name it explicitly as off-limits rather than leave it unmentioned.

This is a sensitivity/child-safety judgment, not a doctrinal one, and I found no cross-tradition
disagreement to check it against — none exists on what the verses narrate.

### 4.4 Item 9 (22:19) — contested ground the brief did NOT flag. **Recommended addition (§6.6).**

The brief says Josiah's scene is "already bounded at `medium`. No new ground." That is true of
22:19 itself. It is **not** true of the verse immediately following, and the brief's confident
"no new ground" is what makes this worth writing down.

Huldah's oracle continues in **22:20**: Josiah will be "gathered to your grave in peace" — yet
**23:29** narrates his death in battle at Megiddo. This is a recognized difficulty, and the
commentators say so in their own words:
[biblehub.com/commentaries/2_kings/22-20.htm](https://biblehub.com/commentaries/2_kings/22-20.htm)
— Barnes concedes a "verbal contradiction… but not in real opposition to its spirit"; the Pulpit
Commentary states outright there is "a seeming contradiction" requiring reconciliation. The
resolutions offered vary (peace as *timing* — spared from seeing Jerusalem's ruin; peace as
*spiritual state*; peace as *burial* rather than manner of death). No commentator in that
aggregation calls it an unfulfilled prophecy, so this is not a live prophecy-failure dispute among
mainstream traditions — but it is unambiguously *interpretation*, and any item touching it would
require grading one resolution over another.

**Nothing is currently wrong.** Item 9 stops at 22:19 and grades "tender". The fix is a
contested-territory bullet plus a forward guard so a future pass extending Huldah's oracle by one
verse doesn't walk into it believing the scene is fully bounded.

### 4.5 Item 11 (25:1) — the brief's "no known dispute" claim. **Verified true.**

The brief asserts the siege's start date carries "no known cross-tradition dispute." I checked
rather than accepted it: [biblehub.com/commentaries/2_kings/25-1.htm](https://biblehub.com/commentaries/2_kings/25-1.htm)
shows commentators unanimous that "his reign" means **Zedekiah's**, with no disagreement on the
dating — Geneva ("That is, of Zedekiah"), Cambridge ("i.e. Of Zedekiah's reign"), Gill likewise;
Keil-Delitzsch corroborates via the same date at Ezekiel 24:1. **Claim confirmed.**

**Accuracy note (recommended, §6.7), not a style-guide violation:** every one of those commentators
found it necessary to *gloss* the antecedent, which tells you it isn't obvious from the verse. The
brief's recall stem reads "In the ___ year of **his** reign… **Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon** came",
placing the pronoun next to the wrong king's name. The answer key is unaffected ("ninth" either
way), but the item as displayed can teach a player that this was Nebuchadnezzar's ninth year — it
was his nineteenth (25:8). Suggest showing the reference with a neutral scene label, or including
25:2's context, so the item doesn't quietly instil a wrong association.

## 6. Required and Recommended Changes

**6.1 — Item 6 (19:15): resolve the WEB edition conflict. [blocking]**
The brief's string and the approved `medium` brief's string differ ("enthroned above the cherubim"
vs "who are enthroned above the cherubim"), and a third printing reads "who sit [above] the
cherubim". Pick one and make both briefs agree, then drop or correct the "already-approved verse"
label, which is currently inaccurate. Also add the "(WEB, trimmed)" tag — the verse begins "Hezekiah
prayed before Yahweh, and said," and ends "You have made heaven and earth." Recommend adopting the
current ebible.org `/web` reading (the brief's own) and updating `medium` to match, since the brief's
fetch is the more recent one — but see 6.4, this needs a decision above the brief level.

**6.2 — Item 2 (2:12): label the quotation as trimmed. [blocking]**
The verse continues "Then he took hold of his own clothes and tore them in two pieces." The brief
presents the first two sentences, in quotation marks, tagged "(WEB, new)" with no indication the
verse continues. The words shown are verbatim, so this is not paraphrase — but presenting part of a
verse as *the* verse is the integrity concern §3's final bullet exists to prevent, and `medium`'s
"(WEB, trimmed)" convention already handles it. Identical to `genesis-easy-review.md` §6.2 (Gen 8:11).

**6.3 — Sensitivity note: fix the item cross-reference. [blocking]**
"Item 12 below (25:11…)" should read **item 16**. Item 12 is 25:29. See §5.

**6.4 — Pin the WEB edition at project level. [recommended; product-owner decision, not a copy-edit]**
Open Decision #1 fixes the translation but not the edition. ebible.org `/web` and `/engwebp` differ
on the **divine name** ("Yahweh" vs "the LORD"), and ebible.org, BibleGateway/BibleHub and
BibleStudyTools serve three different printings of 19:15. Recommend recording a pinned edition +
fetch date in `MEMORY.md` and citing the exact path (e.g. `ebible.org/web/`) in every brief, rather
than "ebible.org". Flagging as a decision because the divine-name rendering carries genuine
cross-tradition weight (already recorded at `genesis-easy-review.md` §4.5) — it should not be
settled implicitly by whichever URL a drafter happened to open.

**6.5 — Contested Territory: add two forward-looking format restrictions. [recommended]**
Same instrument as the Gen 1:26 "us/our" rule:
- **2:12** — "the chariots of Israel and its horsemen" is display-only; no item may grade what it
  means or whom it refers to (Rashi reads it as Elijah himself, not the fiery chariot — §4.2).
- **17:18** — no item may ask *which tribe was left*; every mainstream commentator glosses "the tribe
  of Judah only" as the kingdom, not one literal tribe (§4.1). Typed recall of the text's own word,
  as item 4 does, remains fine.

**6.6 — Contested Territory: add a 22:20 bullet. [recommended]**
Record that Huldah's "gathered to your grave in peace" (22:20) vs Josiah's death at Megiddo (23:29)
is a recognized interpretive difficulty (Barnes: "verbal contradiction"; Pulpit: "a seeming
contradiction"), that 22:20 is therefore **not challenge-ready in any tier**, and that no item may
ask how or whether the promise was fulfilled. The brief's "no new ground" for Josiah's scene is
otherwise correct — this closes the one verse where it isn't (§4.4).

**6.7 — Item 11 (25:1): disambiguate whose reign. [recommended]**
Answer key is fine; the displayed stem can teach that this was Nebuchadnezzar's ninth year rather
than Zedekiah's (§4.5). Add a neutral scene label or retain enough context to fix the antecedent.

**6.8 — Items 5, 6, 10, 12: inline the verbatim verse text. [recommended]**
These four give only the recall stem with the blank, citing text by cross-reference to
`docs/content/2-kings.md`. Style guide §5 requires reference + translation + verbatim text on every
item that displays or requires recall of text, and §5 notes this is enforced technically at
ingestion (`BACKEND_REQUIREMENTS.md` §4), so a cross-reference will not satisfy the pipeline and
leaves the verbatim-QA pass without a self-contained artifact. Identical to
`genesis-easy-review.md` §6.6.

## 7. Verdict

Every verse text the brief asserts is real WEB text; no paraphrase is presented as quotation, and no
