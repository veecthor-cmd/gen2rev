## 3. Contested-Territory Cross-Check

### "The Satan" figure (Job 1:6-12)
Confirmed genuine cross-tradition divergence:
- **Jewish tradition:** "ha-satan" is read as a heavenly prosecutor/accuser role — a subordinate
  member of the divine council performing an investigative/testing function under God's explicit,
  stated limits, distinct from the independently evil cosmic Devil of later theology. [Source:
  Sefaria-sourced overview, "Sympathy for the Devil: Ha-Satan in Jewish Scripture"](https://voices.sefaria.org/sheets/668485).
- **Christian tradition:** some sources (e.g. Duane Garrett, cited via B.H. Roberts Foundation)
  argue the Job 1-2 figure should be identified with "the devil" of later Christian theology, not
  merely read as a prosecuting-attorney role; other Christian sources present this as a live,
  actively-debated question rather than settled. [Sources: overview of the Garrett argument](https://bhroberts.org/records/2ftKjf-0KxTxm/duane_a_garrett_discusses_the_evidence_that_satan_in_job_1_2_is_not_a_prosecuting_attorney_in_gods_heavenly_court_but_instead_is_to_be_identified_as_a_supernatural_evil_being_and_the_devil), [balanced framing of the debate](https://evidenceunseen.com/old-testament/job/difficulties/is-satan-the-evil-spirit-known-as-the-devil-or-is-he-merely-gods-prosecuting-attorney).

**The brief's bounding holds.** Challenge items use only the WEB text's own wording (what Satan
says, what Yahweh permits and limits) and never ask the player to identify what/who Satan
theologically is. This is the same "stay narrative, don't grade the interpretation" pattern
confirmed sound in the Genesis and Leviticus reviews. No change needed beyond the format
restriction the brief already states (narrative-fact-only items, no identity/nature questions).

### Job 19:25-27, "I know that my Redeemer lives"
Confirmed genuine, deep divergence, structurally identical to Genesis 3:15's protoevangelium
question:
- **Christian tradition:** widely reads this as pointing to bodily resurrection, and in some
  traditions specifically to Christ as redeemer. [Source: overview of Christian resurrection-hope
  reading](https://library.abide.edu.kg/job-redeemer-lives-chapter-19/).
- **Jewish tradition / much critical scholarship:** reads the underlying Hebrew *go'el*
  (kinsman-redeemer) as most plausibly a hope for vindication of Job's name within his lifetime or
  shortly after, not a resurrection doctrine — and treats the Hebrew as genuinely ambiguous on
  which reading is correct. [Source: Enter the Bible overview](https://enterthebible.org/passage/job-1923-27-i-know-that-my-redeemer-lives/), [Hebrew go'el/kinsman-redeemer background](https://hebrew.substack.com/p/evening-post-goel-redeemer).

**The brief's bounding holds.** The verse is restricted to recall/fill-in-blank format only, never
a "what does Job mean by Redeemer" or "who is the Redeemer" multiple-choice item — this is exactly
the same structural technique the Genesis review applied to Gen 3:15 and the Leviticus review
applied to Lev 10:1-3. No change needed to challenge content.

### Job 42:6, "I abhor myself, and repent in dust and ashes"
Confirmed this is contested at the translation/Hebrew-syntax level, independent of any later
theological reading: the verb rendered "abhor myself" may lack a direct object in the Hebrew, and
scholars disagree whether Job is repenting of sin, recanting specific statements about God, or
simply relenting/being comforted — a live question given the prologue already establishes Job's
innocence (1:1, 1:22, 2:10, and God's own statement in 42:7 that Job "spoke of me the thing that
is right"). [Source: survey of translation options and scholarly disagreement](https://www.ministrymagazine.org/archive/2014/03/job-426-and-the-absence-of-sin-in-jobs-repentence), [Bible Odyssey overview](https://m.bibleodyssey.org/passages/main-articles/jobs-last-words-job-426/).

**The brief's bounding holds.** This is a case where the brief's own restriction (verbatim recall
only, no "what does Job mean" item) is doing real work — the translators themselves disagree before
any theological reading is applied, so a multiple-choice interpretive item would be unusually
exposed to a "wrong per which scholar" problem, not just a doctrinal-neutrality one. No change
needed.

### Job 42:7, God's rebuke of the friends
Confirmed this is a stated textual fact (safe to include as narrative fact per
`docs/CONTENT_STYLE_GUIDE.md` §2), but also confirmed that even scholarship is divided on *why*
Job was vindicated — some emphasize the content of the friends' claims about retributive justice
being wrong, others emphasize that Job spoke honestly and directly to God while the friends never
interceded for him. [Source: overview of the scholarly debate on Job's vindication](https://enterthebible.org/passage/job-427-9-jobs-friends-are-reprimanded-by-god/), [contrasting reading emphasizing the friends' misapplication of sound theology](https://www.monergism.com/reformation-theology/blog/failure-job%E2%80%99s-friends-warning-against-misrepresenting-god).
This directly confirms the assignment's own instruction was correct to draw a hard line here.
**The brief's bounding holds** — the item tests only that the rebuke happened and to whom, never
why. No change needed.

### Deliberately excluded: the dialogue and Elihu's speech (Job 3-37)
This is the single largest scope decision in the brief (roughly 34 of 42 chapters). Confirmed this
exclusion is well-founded rather than merely cautious: every one of the friends' major claims
(suffering implies guilt), Job's counter-claims (his innocence, his demand for a hearing), and
Elihu's intervening view are themselves the subject matter of the "was the friends' theology
wrong" question the assignment explicitly puts out of bounds — there is no way to write a graded
recall or sequence item from this material that doesn't implicitly ask the player to track *whose
position is correct*, which is exactly the excluded question. **Recommend the exclusion be treated
as structural, not just topical** — i.e., no future challenge-authoring pass should selectively
quote from chs. 3-37 even for a single "safe-looking" line, since any such line's selection would
itself be an editorial judgment about which side's rhetoric is worth immortalizing in a graded
item. The brief's current framing already supports this; flagging it explicitly so it survives
into `BACKEND_REQUIREMENTS.md` §4 authoring.

### Deliberately excluded: Behemoth and Leviathan (Job 40:15-41:34)
Confirmed both stated reasons are real: creature-identification (mythological/chaos-symbol reading
vs. young-earth-apologetic literal-animal reading) is a live, unresolved debate structurally
parallel to the creation-days and flood-scope exclusions already accepted in the Genesis and
Leviticus reviews; separately, the passage's imagery (especially Leviathan breathing fire) is
intense for a child-directed product. Not independently re-searched this pass beyond confirming the
general shape of the creature-identification debate is well known and unresolved — **bounding
holds** on the same reasoning as the prior books' historicity exclusions. No change needed.

### Deliberately excluded: the explicit death-report verses (Job 1:18-19)
This is a sensitivity judgment call, not a cross-tradition doctrinal one (similar in kind to the
Nadab-and-Abihu question in Leviticus, but resolved differently). The brief chose full exclusion
rather than Leviticus's "include with a structural pairing rule" approach, reasoning that the
direct deaths of ten children is a step further on the child-directed-content line than Nadab and
Abihu's deaths. **This reviewer agrees the more conservative choice is reasonable here** — Job's
loss is still fully present in the world (via 1:20-22 and 2:9-10, which presuppose and reference
it) without a graded item built from the report of the deaths themselves. This is a judgment call
within the child-safety guardrail rather than a doctrinal-neutrality one; flagging that if Kachi
disagrees on where this line should sit, that's a product decision this reviewer isn't positioned
to override, not a theological-neutrality finding.

### Why God answers with nature imagery instead of directly addressing Job's suffering (chs. 38-41)
Confirmed this is itself a major, unresolved question in Job scholarship (sovereignty-emphasis
readings, wisdom-humility readings, cosmic-order-vs-human-suffering readings all actively argued).
**The brief's bounding holds** — no item asks why God answers this way, only what the imagery
itself says. No change needed.
