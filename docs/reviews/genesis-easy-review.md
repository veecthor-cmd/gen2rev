## 4. Contested-Territory Cross-Check

### 4.1 Genesis 22:13 — the ram in the thicket (the item the brief asked me to re-confirm)

The brief claims this item stays inside the Genesis 22 bounding already established at `medium`
("what happened, never why God tested Abraham or whether the substitution was necessary"). I did
not take this on the brief's word. Four traditions checked:

- **Classic Protestant commentary** — [Bible Hub, Genesis 22:13](https://biblehub.com/genesis/22-13.htm)
  (Ellicott, Pulpit Commentary, Matthew Henry, Gill). Reads the ram as vicarious substitution and a
  type of Christ; Ellicott: the ram "took Isaac's place, and by its actual death completed the
  typical representation of the Saviour's death."
- **Jewish tradition** — [Sefaria, Genesis 22:13](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Genesis.22.13)
  (JPS: "his eye fell upon a ram, caught in the thicket by its horns… offered it up as a burnt
  offering in place of his son") and [Rashi on Genesis 22:13](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Rashi_on_Genesis.22.13).
  Rashi reads the ram as prepared from the six days of Creation, entangled through Satan's doing,
  and reads Abraham's substitution through his prayer that each act count "as though my son is
  being slain." No Christological typology; a wholly different interpretive frame.
- **Catholic** — [USCCB, Genesis 22 (NABRE)](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/genesis/22). Renders "a
  single ram", and the notes deliberately centre the passage on *Abraham's being tested*, treating
  the ram as the alternate offering rather than developing a typology in the note itself.
- **Orthodox** — [OCA, "Abraham" (The Orthodox Faith, Vol. I)](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/doctrine-scripture/salvation-history/abraham).
  Reads Isaac as a type of Christ and the ram as pointing to the Lamb of God, with the distinctive
  Orthodox emphasis that *no* ram was provided for God's own Son.

**Two genuine divergences exist here, and neither touches the graded item:**

1. **A live textual variant.** The Masoretic Hebrew reads אַחַר ("behind/after"), which WEB renders
   "behind him". Ellicott and the Pulpit Commentary note that the LXX, Samaritan Pentateuch and
   Syriac support אֶחָד ("one ram") — the reading NABRE follows with "a single ram", and JPS
   sidesteps with plain "a ram". Rashi takes אַחַר temporally ("after" the angel spoke), not
   spatially. So translations legitimately differ on *where/how* the ram is described.
2. **Interpretation of the ram's significance** — substitutionary-atonement typology (Protestant),
   Lamb-of-God typology with a distinct Orthodox inflection, Akedah/merit-and-prayer framing
   (Jewish), test-of-Abraham framing (Catholic notes).

**The bounding holds.** The graded question is "What did Abraham find caught in the thicket?" with
answer "a ram". Every one of the four traditions above, and every translation consulted, agrees the
animal was a ram (Hebrew אַיִל). The answer key depends on none of the disputed material: not on
the אַחַר/אֶחָד variant, not on typology, not on why the test was given. Displaying the verse's own
words "instead of his son" is direct verse text, which §2 permits explicitly. **The brief's claim
that this item adds a second in-bounds fact without extending the boundary is accurate.** No change
required to item 6.

**Forward-looking guard (recommended, §6.4):** because the "behind him" wording sits on a real
manuscript dispute, no future challenge in any tier should grade *where* the ram was, or ask a
player to choose between "behind him" and "one ram" phrasings. This should be recorded the same way
the Gen 3:15 and Gen 1:27 format restrictions were, so a later authoring pass can't cross it
unknowingly.

### 4.2 Genesis 12:1 — an item I checked independently, since §3 names the Abrahamic covenant

The style guide's out-of-bounds list explicitly names "covenant theology vs. dispensationalism
readings of the Abrahamic covenant". The brief asserts 12:1 carries "no new contested ground"; that
needed checking rather than accepting.

[Bible Hub, Genesis 12:1 commentaries](https://biblehub.com/commentaries/genesis/12-1.htm) —
Matthew Henry, Ellicott, Gill, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, Keil-Delitzsch, Pulpit Commentary. Result:
commentators are **unanimous on what the verse says God commanded** (sever three ties — country,
kindred, father's house — and go to an unnamed land). The covenant-interpretation dispute attaches
to the *promises* in 12:2-3, not the command in 12:1, and 12:2-3 already passed review at `medium`
under narrative-only bounding. **The brief's assessment holds. No change to item 5.**

However, the same page surfaces a genuine disagreement the brief did not anticipate: **whether the
call was given in Ur or in Haran** (JFB/Keil-Delitzsch argue Ur, citing Acts 7:2; Gill and the
Pulpit Commentary argue Haran or a repeated call). This does not affect item 5, which asks *what*
Abram was told to do. It is a live trap for a future item, and should be flagged now — see §6.4.

### 4.3 Genesis 1:5 and 1:31 — creation-days proximity

Item 2 displays "the first day" and item 8's verse contains "a sixth day". Under the inherited
creation-days bounding, displaying the text is fine; grading duration is not. Neither item grades
duration — item 2 grades what the light was called, item 8 grades the word "good". **Bounding
holds, no change.**

### 4.4 Genesis 8:11 — Flood-scope proximity

Item 4 sits inside the Flood narrative, whose global/regional scope is flagged as out-of-bounds.
The item grades only what the dove carried. **No scope claim is made or required. Bounding holds**
(the separate problem with this item, §6.1, is factual precision, not doctrine).

### 4.5 Inherited: the divine name "Yahweh"

Items 5 and 10 display WEB's rendering "Yahweh" where most English Bibles read "the LORD". This is a
real cross-tradition sensitivity — Jewish practice avoids vocalizing the Tetragrammaton, and the
Catholic Church's 2008 directive instructs that it not be used in liturgy. It is **inherited,
already-settled ground**: WEB is fixed as the MVP translation by Open Decision #1, and the `medium`
brief already displays "Yahweh" (Gen 3:23-24, 15:6) under an approved review. Recording it here for
the trail only; no action, and no re-litigation. Mildly mitigating: item 5's question stem says
"God", not "Yahweh".

## 5. Additional Observations (non-blocking)

**5.1 Quote nesting, for the verbatim-QA pass.** WEB prints Gen 1:5 with double quotes: `God called
the light "day", and the darkness he called "night".` The brief nests them as single quotes because
the whole verse sits inside a quoted block. That is correct brief formatting, not a text error, but
the ingestion pipeline must store the WEB inner double quotes, not the brief's single quotes. Same
pattern applies to Gen 12:1 and (in the `medium` brief) Gen 22:14. Flagging for
`docs/qa/genesis-easy-verbatim-qa.md` rather than treating as a review defect.

**5.2 The "a lamb" distractor in item 6.** Textually the key is right — 22:13 says ram, and every
tradition agrees. But Gen 22:8, four verses earlier, has Abraham say "God will provide himself the
lamb for a burnt offering", and the Lamb-of-God connection is prominent in both Protestant and
Orthodox readings. A beginner-tier player marked wrong for answering "lamb" may be drawing on the
text itself. Not a style-guide violation and not a required change; consider swapping the distractor
(e.g. "an ox") if playtesting shows confusion.

**5.3 Distractor reuse.** "a dove" appears as a wrong answer in item 6 while being the subject of
item 4. Design nit only, no theological weight.

**5.4 Boss-item weighting.** Item 8 is a level-1 recall item placed in the boss set, where the
`medium` brief specifies level 3-5 weighting. Appropriate for an easy tier and outside my remit —
noting only so it is a deliberate choice rather than an oversight.

## 6. Required and Recommended Changes

**6.1 — Item 4 (Gen 8:11): fix the question stem. [required]**
The stem "What did the dove bring back to Noah?" has more than one textually-correct answer, and one
of them is offered as a distractor. Genesis 8 records **three** dove sendings
([WEB, Gen 8:8-12](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis+8%3A6-12&version=WEB)):
8:9 — the dove finds no rest and returns with *nothing*; 8:11 — returns with the olive leaf; 8:12 —
does not return at all. The distractor "nothing" is therefore a defensible answer to the stem as
written. Fix: anchor the stem to the reference — e.g. "According to Genesis 8:11, what did the dove
bring back to Noah?" or "…the second time Noah sent out the dove" — and/or replace the "nothing"
distractor.

**6.2 — Item 4 (Gen 8:11): label the quote as trimmed. [required]**
The verse continues "So Noah knew that the waters were abated from the earth." Either restore the
full verse or re-tag the item "(WEB, trimmed)" per the `medium` brief's existing convention, so no
partial verse is displayed to a player as the whole verse.

**6.3 — Scene 4 scope: drop "17", or flag it. [required]**
The brief states it reuses the approved scenes with "no new scenes added", but its scene list reads
"Abraham's Call and the Binding of Isaac (Gen 12, 15, 17, 22)" where the approved `medium` brief
reads "(Genesis 12, 15, 22)". Genesis 17 is the covenant of circumcision — precisely the Abrahamic-
covenant ground §3 names as out-of-bounds for interpretation, and it carries no contested-territory
note here because the brief believes it changed nothing. No challenge item currently draws on
Genesis 17, so nothing has actually gone wrong; but the stated scope now silently exceeds what was
reviewed. Fix: remove "17" from the scene definition, or keep it and add an explicit
contested-territory note covering it.

**6.4 — Contested Territory: add two forward-looking format restrictions. [recommended]**
In the style of the existing Gen 3:15 / Gen 1:27 structural rules, so future authoring passes can't
cross these unknowingly:
- **Gen 22:13** — no challenge may grade *where* the ram was ("behind him" vs "one ram" is a live
  manuscript variant across the MT, LXX, Samaritan and Syriac; WEB, NABRE and JPS render it three
  different ways). Grading *what* was found is fine and is what item 6 does.
- **Gen 12:1** — no challenge may grade *where Abram was* when the call came (Ur vs Haran is
  genuinely disputed among mainstream commentators, with Acts 7:2 in play). Grading *what he was
  told to do* is fine and is what item 5 does.

**6.5 — Contested Territory: fix the reference typo. [required]**
The section reads "note 1:15/1:27 aren't used in this tier at all". The inherited restriction is on
**3:15** and 1:27, not 1:15. This sentence is the load-bearing statement that the inherited rules
remain binding, so the reference needs to be right.

**6.6 — Items 3, 8, 9, 10: inline the verbatim text. [recommended]**
Style guide §5 requires every challenge item to carry reference, translation, *and* verbatim text
where the challenge displays or requires recall of text. Items 3 and 8 are fill-in-the-blank recall
items and items 9-10 display verse text, but all four cite the text by cross-reference to
`docs/content/genesis.md` rather than reproducing it. §5 says this is enforced technically at
ingestion (`BACKEND_REQUIREMENTS.md` §4), so a cross-reference will not satisfy the pipeline, and it
leaves the verbatim-QA pass without a self-contained artifact to check. Paste the WEB text inline.

## 7. Verdict

**`approved-with-changes`.**

Every verse text the brief asserts is verbatim-correct against live WEB sources. Every one of the
10 items is in-bounds under `CONTENT_STYLE_GUIDE.md` §2 and clear of all five §3 prohibitions. The
Genesis 22:13 bounding the brief asked to have re-confirmed **does hold**, verified against
Protestant, Jewish, Catholic and Orthodox sources that disagree substantially about the ram's
meaning while agreeing completely about the narrative fact being graded.

The six changes in §6 are fixes to precision, labelling and scope hygiene — none requires removing
or rewriting a challenge item, and none reflects a doctrinal problem. The most substantive is §6.1,
which is a factual-accuracy defect (an answer key with a second defensible answer), not a
theological one.

**Not escalated.** Both genuine cross-tradition/textual disagreements found (the אַחַר/אֶחָד variant
at 22:13; Ur vs Haran at 12:1) were checked directly against the graded answer keys and neither
bears on them. §6.4 exists so they stay that way.

**Outstanding, not this reviewer's job:** independent verbatim-text QA per `QA_REQUIREMENTS.md` §3
(`docs/qa/genesis-easy-verbatim-qa.md`), which should prioritise the quote-nesting issue in §5.1 and
must re-run against items 3, 8, 9 and 10 once §6.6 puts their text inline. Logging this review in
`docs/CONTENT_REVIEW_LOG.md` is the calling session's task, not this reviewer's.
