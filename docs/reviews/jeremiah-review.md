## 3. Contested-Territory Cross-Check

### Jeremiah 1:5, the womb/personhood question
Confirmed this exact verse is genuinely, currently invoked on multiple sides of the abortion/
personhood debate in American public discourse — pro-life advocacy sources cite it as evidence of
prenatal personhood, while other commentators (including a Democratic officeholder quoted publicly
using the same verse) argue the verse is about Jeremiah's prophetic calling specifically, not a
general claim about personhood, and note classical Jewish commentary (Rashi) reads it as being
about Jeremiah's prophetic mission rather than fetal personhood. [Sources: Sojourners overview of
the verse's use on anti-abortion signage](https://sojo.net/articles/about-bible-verse-you-see-anti-abortion-signs-jeremiah),
[Catholic Answers' pro-life use of the verse](https://www.catholic.com/bible-navigator/abortion/jeremiah14-5),
[Christian Post report on a pro-choice legislator citing the same verse](https://www.christianpost.com/news/democrat-cites-jeremiah-15-to-defend-abortion.html).
**The brief's bounding holds** — the challenge item is recall/fill-in-blank only, reciting the
verse's own words about Jeremiah's own call, with no multiple-choice framing that could be read as
taking a position on the present-day debate. No change needed.

### Jeremiah 7, the Temple Sermon — NT cross-reference
Confirmed the "den of robbers" line is quoted by Jesus in all three Synoptic Gospels (Matthew 21:13,
Mark 11:17, Luke 19:46) at the temple-cleansing scene — this is a well-established, uncontested
literary fact, not itself a point of cross-tradition disagreement. Per this assignment's explicit
instruction, **the brief correctly omits any reference to the later NT usage**, keeping the sermon
in its own eighth-century-BC context. No change needed; this is a scope instruction rather than a
contested-territory item in the usual sense, but confirmed compliant.

### Jeremiah 28, testing true and false prophecy
Confirmed via classic cross-tradition commentary aggregation that Jeremiah 28:9's fulfillment
criterion is uniformly read as the text's own stated standard (tracing to Deuteronomy 18:21-22),
with no cross-tradition divergence found on the narrative facts themselves. [Source: Bible Hub
commentary aggregation on Jeremiah 28:9](https://biblehub.com/commentaries/jeremiah/28-9.htm) —
Matthew Henry, Gill, Barnes, and Jamieson-Fausset-Brown all read the verse the same way, focused
entirely on the historical test applied to Hananiah, with none extending it to a claim about
present-day prophecy. Separately confirmed that whether prophetic gifts continue today
(cessationism vs. continuationism) is a live, unresolved debate within contemporary Christianity.
[Source: The Gospel Coalition on cessationism](https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/themelios/article/it-all-depends-upon-prophecy-a-brief-case-for-nuanced-cessationism/).
**The brief's bounding holds** — no challenge item extends Jeremiah 28:9 into a claim about modern
prophecy or how to identify a false prophet today; every item stays on the Hananiah/Jeremiah
narrative itself. No change needed.

### Jeremiah 37-38, the cistern imprisonment
No cross-tradition doctrinal dispute found over the narrative facts; this is a sensitivity question
per this assignment's content notes, not a theological one. **The brief's bounding holds** — the
scene description narrates the danger in prose, and the challenge-ready verses (38:9-10, 38:13) are
drawn from Ebedmelech's appeal and the rescue itself, not the raw peril. This is the same technique
already used for Job's exclusion of the children's-death report (Job 1:18-19). No change needed.

### Jeremiah 39, the fall of Jerusalem and the exclusion of 39:6-7
Confirmed 39:6-7 (Zedekiah's sons killed before him, his own eyes put out) is the exact parallel
account to 2 Kings 25:6-7, which `docs/content/2-kings.md` and `docs/reviews/2-kings-review.md`
already excluded from challenge-ready content for the same reason (graphic violence, not needed for
a child-directed MVP given how much other material exists). **Confirming this exclusion is
consistent, not an oversight** — this brief correctly matches the prior book's precedent at the
same historical event. No change needed.

### Jeremiah 31:31-34, the New Covenant
Confirmed this is the deepest cross-tradition divergence in this brief, comparable in kind to
Leviticus's sacrifice/atonement item and Job's "Redeemer lives" item:
- **Christian tradition**, especially following Hebrews 8:8-12 (which quotes this passage in full)
  and Hebrews 10:16-17, has a long history of supersessionist readings in which the new covenant
  in Christ is understood to replace or supersede the covenant with Israel — a reading traceable at
  least to the Epistle of Barnabas in the early second century. [Source: Bible Odyssey on
  supersessionism, discussing Jeremiah 31 specifically](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/supersessionism/).
  Not all Christian scholarship reads it this way — some sources explicitly reject the
  supersessionist reading and stress the covenant's continuity with, not replacement of, God's
  "ongoing relationship with the house of Israel and the house of Judah," treating Noahic,
  Abrahamic, Davidic, and new covenants as coexisting rather than sequentially replacing one
  another, and note the promise remains not yet fully realized. [Source: Enter the Bible /
  Luther Seminary commentary on Jeremiah 31:31-34](https://enterthebible.org/passage/jeremiah-3131-34-the-new-covenant/).
- **Jewish tradition** reads the passage as addressed to the Jewish people specifically, and the
  "newness" as being about faithful observance rather than a different covenant or a different
  people: Rashi's comment is that the covenant is called new because both God and Israel will
  finally keep it fully, and Radak states its newness "is that it will be kept, and not broken
  like the covenant that b'nei Yisrael cut with God at Mount Sinai" — i.e., a renewal, not a
  replacement, of the Sinai covenant with the same people. [Source: Sefaria, Jeremiah 31:31 with
  Rashi and Radak commentary](https://www.sefaria.org/Jeremiah.31.31?with=Commentary); confirmed via
  secondary summary at [Rabbi Ian Adams' overview](https://rabbiianadams.com/the-new-covenant-of-jeremiah-31/).
  Jewish tradition also anchors the covenant's eternality in the very next verses (31:35-36 — as
  long as the sun, moon, and sea endure, so does Israel), reinforcing that "new" does not mean "for
  a different people."

**The brief's bounding holds, and is the correct approach given how deep this divergence runs.**
The challenge-ready verses (31:31, 31:33-34) recite only the text's own words, including its own
stated addressees ("the house of Israel, and... the house of Judah"), with no challenge item
asserting fulfillment in Christ, replacement of a prior covenant, or any claim about who the
covenant applies to today. This is exactly the "stay narrative, don't grade the interpretation"
pattern already validated for Genesis 22, Leviticus 1/17, and Job 19:25-27. **This item is not being
escalated** — the divergence is real and deep, but the brief's format restriction (recall-only,
verbatim recitation) fully neutralizes it for grading purposes, the same way the equally deep
sacrifice/atonement divergence was handled in Leviticus's review. No change needed.
