## 3. Contested-Territory Cross-Check

### 3.1 Deliberate exclusions (Isaiah 7:14, 9:6-7, Isaiah 53) — reconfirmed absent

Directly checked every reference cited in `docs/content/isaiah-hard.md`: all twelve items draw from
Isaiah 1, 2, 6, 12, 38, 40, 41, or 55. None draws from Isaiah 7, 9, or 53. This reviewer independently
re-derives, not merely re-accepts, the medium-tier finding that no narrowing technique closes the gap
for these three passages (`docs/reviews/isaiah-review.md` §3.1-3.3). The hard tier's push toward
less-iconic, more-precise material created no pressure to reach into this excluded ground — Isaiah's
other 8 passages contain enough under-used verses (1:16, 2:3, 6:2/4/5, 12:3-5, 40:29-30, 55:7/9) to
supply a full 12-item hard tier without it. **Exclusion confirmed to hold.**

### 3.2 Isaiah 2:3 (item 2), "the law shall go out of Zion" — independently checked, bounding holds

This is a different clause than the "latter days"/"mountain of Yahweh's house" language in v.2 that
carries the confirmed timing/referent dispute (`docs/reviews/isaiah-review.md` §3.4: Jewish tradition
reads a still-future messianic age; Christian tradition commonly reads the "latter days" as inaugurated
at Christ's first coming, sometimes reading "the mountain" as the Church). "The law shall go out of
Zion, and Yahweh's word from Jerusalem" states a place of origin only. Checked Bible Hub's aggregated
commentary on Isaiah 2:3 specifically ([biblehub.com/commentaries/isaiah/2-3.htm](https://biblehub.com/commentaries/isaiah/2-3.htm)):
classic Christian commentators (Henry, Barnes, Gill, Pulpit Commentary) read "the law" itself as gospel
doctrine fulfilled through apostolic preaching from Jerusalem at Pentecost — a Christian-specific
reading of *what* "the law" is, distinct from a Jewish reading of it as Torah instruction proceeding
from a (still-future, per Jewish tradition) rebuilt Zion. **This is a real, if narrower, disagreement
worth naming: the two traditions differ on what "the law" refers to, even though both agree Zion/
Jerusalem is named as its point of origin.** Item 2's blank falls only on the printed place-name
"Zion" — it does not ask what "the law" is, so this narrower disagreement doesn't reach the graded
token either, the same way the wider "latter days" dispute doesn't. **Structural rule carried forward
and slightly sharpened: no challenge built from Isaiah 2:2-4 may ask when "the latter days" occur, what
"the mountain of Yahweh's house" refers to, or what "the law" refers to.** Item 2 complies. **Bounding
holds.**

### 3.3 Isaiah 6:5 (item 4), "a man of unclean lips" — independently checked, bounding holds

Item 4 grades Isaiah's own self-description only. The confirmed 6:1 identity question
(`docs/reviews/isaiah-review.md` §3.5 — John 12:41's Christological reading of "the Lord" Isaiah saw,
vs. Jewish tradition's unidentified theophany) is not engaged: the blank falls on "lips," never on "the
King, Yahweh of Armies" in the same verse's closing clause. **Same bounding `medium` already applied to
this passage; no new restriction needed.**

### 3.4 Isaiah 38:1 (item 6) — bounding confirmed to hold

Item 6 grades only what Isaiah tells Hezekiah, in the text's own words, staying inside Isaiah 38's own
wording. No comparison to or claim about 2 Kings 20 or 2 Chronicles 32:24-26 is made — same scope-only
bounding `docs/reviews/isaiah-review.md` §3.6 already confirmed for this passage. **Bounding holds.**

### 3.5 Isaiah 40-55 authorship (items 5, 7, 9, 12, and the boss sequence's 40:31/55:8 quotations) —
bounding confirmed to hold

No item drawing from chapters 12, 40, or 55 makes or implies any authorship claim; every item cites its
passage simply as "Isaiah," book-chapter-verse. Same no-authorship-claim framing
`docs/reviews/isaiah-review.md` §3.7 confirmed for `medium`. **Bounding holds.**

### 3.6 Isaiah 55:7, "he will freely pardon" (item 9) — independently checked, no new contested ground
found

Checked Bible Hub's aggregated commentary on Isaiah 55:7
([biblehub.com/commentaries/isaiah/55-7.htm](https://biblehub.com/commentaries/isaiah/55-7.htm)):
classic Christian commentators (Gill, Poole, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown) read this as an unconditional,
abundant promise of pardon, with "remarkable unanimity" and no internal disagreement found. Checked the
JPS Tanakh (Gender-Sensitive Edition) translation of the same verse via Sefaria's API for a
Jewish-tradition comparison point ([sefaria.org API, Isaiah 55:7](https://www.sefaria.org/api/v3/texts/Isaiah.55.7?version=english)):
"Let the wicked give up their ways... Let each one turn back to God so as to be pardoned; to our God,
who freely forgives" — substantively the same promise as WEB's "he will freely pardon," no divergence
in what the clause states. Both traditions read this verse as a straightforward statement that Yahweh
forgives the repentant — no live doctrinal dispute (e.g. over atonement mechanism or the basis of
forgiveness) attaches to this specific clause the way it does to NT atonement-theory passages flagged
in `docs/CONTENT_STYLE_GUIDE.md` §6. Item 9 grades only that the text states Yahweh "will freely
pardon," not any doctrine of how or why. **No new restriction needed.**

### 3.7 Remaining newly-isolated clauses (items 1, 3, 7, 10, 12) — independently checked, no new
contested ground found

Isaiah 1:16's "before my eyes," 6:4's "filled with smoke," 6:2's "six wings," 40:29's "power to the
weak," and 55:9's "my ways are higher" were each independently checked for any cross-tradition
disagreement not already catalogued for their parent passages. None found — these are plain
sensory/descriptive narrative details and a transcendence-affirming statement in near-universal use
across traditions, consistent with `docs/reviews/isaiah-review.md`'s existing characterization of
Isaiah 1, 6, 40, and 55 as low-risk material outside the specifically-flagged 6:1 identity question
(already addressed in §3.3 above).

## 4. Additional Item Found

One minor sharpening, not a new exclusion: §3.2's check of Isaiah 2:3 surfaced that classic Christian
commentary reads "the law" in this verse as gospel doctrine (fulfilled at Pentecost) where a Jewish
reading would take it as Torah instruction — a real, if narrow, disagreement not previously named in
`docs/reviews/isaiah-review.md`'s §3.4 (which covered only the "latter days" timing and "the mountain"
referent). Item 2 doesn't reach this disagreement since its blank falls on "Zion," not on "the law,"
but the structural rule for Isaiah 2:2-4 has been sharpened to name it explicitly for future
authoring passes. §3.6's check of Isaiah 55:7 resolved clean with no new finding.

**`approved`** — every check above passed with no unresolved concerns and no fix required. Eight
verses (1:16, 2:3, 6:2, 6:4, 6:5, 12:4, 40:29, 55:7, 55:9) are promoted from "quoted but unused" to
"standalone graded item" for the first time in this brief; each was individually checked in §3 above
rather than assumed safe by association with its already-approved parent passage.

**Not escalated.** Every contested item checked resolved to a bounding either already established by
`medium` and confirmed to still hold, or newly checked in this review (2:3, 55:7) and found free of any
live cross-tradition disagreement. No item in this brief requires a player to affirm anything beyond
