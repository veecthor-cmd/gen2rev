## 3. Contested-Territory Cross-Check

### Deliberately excluded: the Zerubbabel "signet ring" oracle (2:20-23)

This is the book's single most theologically interesting item, and got the deepest cross-tradition
check this pass even though the brief correctly excludes it from graded content:

- **Christian typological/messianic reading:** multiple mainstream evangelical sources read the
  "signet ring" language as reversing the curse on Jehoiachin/Coniah in Jeremiah 22:24 and read
  Zerubbabel's Davidic-line restoration as pointing forward to Christ, since Zerubbabel appears in
  Matthew's and Luke's genealogies of Jesus — "Zerubbabel is a Messianic symbol that will be
  fulfilled in Jesus." [Source: search synthesis of GotQuestions, Ligonier, and related evangelical
  commentary on Haggai 2:23](https://www.gotquestions.org/Zerubbabel-signet-ring.html)
- **Mainline/ecumenical reading (Enter the Bible, Luther Seminary):** reads the same passage as a
  "bounded historical/political promise" to Zerubbabel personally — restoration of Davidic-line
  status and protection as Judah's governor in the immediate post-exilic period (538-332 BCE) —
  with no explicit messianic-fulfillment claim made in the commentary itself. [Source: Enter the
  Bible, Haggai 2:20-23](https://enterthebible.org/passage/haggai-223-zerubbabel-the-signet-ring/)
- **Jewish-tradition angle:** reference material confirms Zerubbabel's Davidic lineage "rekindled
  Jewish messianic hopes" in his own period — i.e., a genuine messianic-hope reading exists within
  Jewish tradition too, but tied to the general hope for a future Davidic redeemer rather than to
  any specific historical figure identified as its fulfillment. [Source: search synthesis citing
  Zerubbabel's Davidic lineage and its significance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zerubbabel)

**Conclusion:** confirmed genuine three-way divergence — explicit Christian typological/Christ
fulfillment reading, a bounded historical/political reading with no fulfillment claim, and a
Jewish messianic-hope reading tied to the (still future, unspecified) Davidic redeemer rather than
Jesus specifically. **The brief's exclusion is the correct call.** Rather than trying to bound a
challenge item to "what the text says without taking a side" (as the brief does elsewhere for less
sharply divided material), this brief excludes 2:20-23 entirely — appropriate given how directly a
narrative-fact recall of "Zerubbabel is called God's signet ring" would invite a player or future
content-author to reach for the fulfillment question this brief is right to stay out of. No change
needed.

### Deliberately excluded: "shaking of the nations" (2:6-7)

verse from this range as challenge-ready content, there is no graded item to check for compliance.
The exclusion itself is the correct call. No change needed.

### Deliberately excluded: ritual-purity dialogue (2:10-19)

Confirmed this is real content (a priestly-law question-and-answer about whether holiness and
uncleanness transmit by contact) but does not touch denominational/doctrinal dispute in the way the
signet-ring oracle does — it was excluded for scope/thinness reasons per the brief, not sensitivity,
and that framing is accurate. No change needed.

### The people's excuse (1:2) and modern stewardship/tithing application

Confirmed this is a live homiletical pattern (Haggai 1 is a common sermon proof-text for
prioritizing religious commitment over comfort, and for building-fund appeals specifically) but not
itself a cross-tradition doctrinal dispute — the brief's safeguard (no challenge item asks a player
to apply the text to modern giving practice) is the right kind of guardrail regardless, keeping the
challenge content at "what the text says the people said," which is what this review checked and
confirmed the brief's example items (Level 5: "what excuse did the people give") actually do. No
change needed.
