## 3. Paraphrase-Presented-As-Quotation Check (`CONTENT_STYLE_GUIDE.md` §3, final bullet)

Eight of nine quoted verse blocks are clean — verified verbatim in §2. One item is not.

### Finding 1 — Item 9 (boss sequence): prose summaries in quotation marks, attributed to verses. **Required fix.**

Item 9 reads:

> Order the judges' cycle: "Israel does evil and is handed over to raiders" (Judg 2:14) → "Yahweh
> raises up a judge who delivers them" (Judg 2:16) → "When the judge dies, the people fall back into
> the same pattern" (Judg 2:18-19).

None of those three strings is WEB text. Compare the live verses:

- 2:14 — "Yahweh's anger burned against Israel, and he delivered them into the hands of raiders who
  plundered them…"
- 2:16 — "Yahweh raised up judges, who saved them out of the hand of those who plundered them."
- 2:18-19 — "…But when the judge was dead, they turned back, and dealt more corruptly than their
  fathers…"

The card labels are accurate *summaries* of those verses — nothing in them is false, and they are
lifted almost word-for-word from the medium brief's own **scene-description prose** (`judges.md`
Scene 1: "Israel turns to other gods, Yahweh hands them over to raiders, Yahweh raises up a judge
who delivers them, and when that judge dies, the people fall back into the same pattern"). But that
prose is a narrator's summary written *by the brief*, not scripture, and here it is presented inside
quotation marks with a verse citation immediately following — the exact shape `CLAUDE.md`'s
content-integrity guardrail and style guide §3 prohibit ("a challenge must never present invented or
reworded text as if it were a direct verse; if it's not verbatim WEB text, it isn't presented in
quotation marks or as 'the verse'"). A player sees quotation marks plus "(Judg 2:14)" and reasonably
concludes they are reading Judges 2:14.

Note this is the *reason* the medium brief's equivalent ladder entry (level 3) is safe: it writes
the same cycle **without quotation marks and without per-step verse citations** ("Order the cycle:
Israel does evil → Yahweh hands them to an enemy → they cry out → Yahweh raises up a judge →
deliverance (Judg 2:11-19)"). The easy brief added the quotes and the per-card citations.

**Concrete fix — pick one:**

1. **Drop the quotation marks and the per-card citations** and cite the block once, as the medium
   brief does: `Order the judges' cycle (Judges 2:11-19): Israel does evil and is handed over to
   raiders → Yahweh raises up a judge who delivers them → when the judge dies, the people fall back
   into the same pattern.` (Preferred — smallest change, matches approved precedent.)
2. **Replace each card with verbatim WEB text** from the cited verse, e.g. card 2 becomes "Yahweh
   raised up judges, who saved them out of the hand of those who plundered them." (Judg 2:16). This
   keeps the citations honest but makes the cards long for an easy tier.

Either way the item stays in-bounds doctrinally — §2 "Sequencing" explicitly permits ordering events
as the text narrates them. This is a text-integrity fix, not a content one. **Item 9 is not eligible
for ingestion until fixed.** Note the same string appears as item 9 of `docs/content/judges-hard.md`
("reused verbatim from `docs/content/judges-easy.md`"), so the fix must be applied in both briefs.

No other item presents non-verbatim text as scripture.

### 4.2 Deborah (Judges 4:4-5) — bounding re-verified against live cross-tradition sources

I did not take the medium review's finding on trust, because this tier grades a *different* token
than the medium brief contemplated (the word "judged" itself).

- **Classic Christian commentators** — [Bible Hub, Judges 4:4](https://biblehub.com/commentaries/judges/4-4.htm)
  (Ellicott, Cambridge, Barnes, Gill, Pulpit). Unanimous that the text states she was a prophetess
  who judged Israel; Cambridge distinguishes her judicial from her military function. Several remark
  on her exceptional status as the only female judge in Israel's history. **No commentator disputes
  the narrative facts.**
- **Jewish tradition** — [Sefaria, Judges 4:4](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Judges.4.4?context=0)
  (JPS 2023: "Deborah, wife of Lappidoth, was a prophet; she led Israel at that time"; Hebrew
  שֹׁפְטָה) and [Rashi on Judges 4:4](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Rashi_on_Judges.4.4?context=0).
  Deborah's office is not disputed; Rashi's comment goes elsewhere entirely (see §4.5).
- **Catholic** — [USCCB, Judges 4 (NABRE)](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/judges/4): "At that time
  the prophet Deborah, wife of Lappidoth, was judging Israel. She used to sit under Deborah's palm
  tree, between Ramah and Bethel…" — same narrative content, no qualifying footnote.

**Bounding holds.** The live disagreement is entirely about *implication for present-day church
leadership* (the complementarian/egalitarian question documented in `docs/reviews/judges-review.md`
§3), not about what 4:4 narrates. WEB "judged", NABRE "was judging" and JPS "led" are three
renderings of one uncontested office. A player typing "judged" into the displayed WEB verse affirms
nothing any of these traditions would dispute. The recall-only rule remains the right instrument and
is satisfied.

### 4.3 Gideon's fleece (Judges 6:37) — bounding re-verified

- [Bible Hub, Judges 6:37](https://biblehub.com/commentaries/judges/6-37.htm) (Barnes, Ellicott,
  MacLaren, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, Matthew Henry). Two results, both relevant: (a) commentators
  **agree completely on the physical facts** — a fleece of wool laid on the threshing floor, dew on
  the fleece only; Ellicott specifically confirms "fleece" rather than a whole sheepskin is the
  correct rendering; (b) they **genuinely diverge on motive** — MacLaren: the request "was not the
  voice of unbelief or of doubt or of presumption, but in it spoke real, though struggling faith";
  JFB, by contrast, holds that "Gideon himself seems to have been conscious of incurring the
  displeasure of God by his hesitancy and doubts."

**The medium brief's flagged disagreement is real and still live, and the bounding holds.** The
graded token is "wool" — a physical detail on which the divergent commentators are identical. No
item asks whether the test showed faith or doubt. **Compliant, no change.**

### 4.4 Previously-unflagged contested item: "the angel of Yahweh" → "Yahweh" in Judges 6 (item 4)

The brief asserts "no other new contested ground." Item 4 quotes 6:14-16, where the speaker is named
"Yahweh" — but in 6:11-12, three verses earlier and inside the same approved scene, the speaker is
"the angel of Yahweh." Who that figure is, is a genuine cross-tradition question, and I confirmed it
is live rather than assuming it:

[Bible Hub, Judges 6:14](https://biblehub.com/commentaries/judges/6-14.htm) — the Geneva Study Bible
identifies the speaker as "Christ appearing in visible form" and Gill as "no other than Jehovah
himself"; Ellicott offers the alternative that "the angel only speaks as the mouth of God,"
comparing Revelation 22:6, and explicitly leaves both readings open. Cambridge treats the narrator's
switch as deliberately disclosing the speaker's identity.

**The current item is safe as written.** Item 4 asks "What did Yahweh tell Gideon to do?" against a
verse in which the text itself supplies the name "Yahweh"; the graded answer is the content of the
command, which every reading above renders identically. Nothing requires a player to affirm who the
angel is. But per style-guide §4, this should be *flagged deliberately* rather than left to surface
after ingestion — that is what §6.3 asks for.

### 4.5 Forward-looking: "the wife of Lappidoth" must not become a graded token

Surfaced while checking §4.2 and worth recording in the same style as the Gen 22:13 / Gen 12:1
guards in `docs/reviews/genesis-easy-review.md` §6.4. Item 2 *displays* "the wife of Lappidoth" but
grades "judged", so nothing is wrong now. However, [Rashi on Judges 4:4](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Rashi_on_Judges.4.4?context=0)
reads *eshet Lappidot* non-onomastically — "She fashioned wicks for the sanctuary" — i.e. not as a
husband's proper name at all. WEB, NABRE and JPS all treat it as a name; Rashi does not. A future
item blanking "Lappidoth" or asking "who was Deborah's husband?" would grade a reading that a major
Jewish commentator rejects. Cheap to prevent now, expensive to catch later. **The hard tier already
blanks a different word from this same verse ("Bethel"), which is safe — this guard is about
protecting the next authoring pass.**

### 4.6 Inherited and settled: the divine name "Yahweh"

Items 1, 3, 4, 6, 7, 9 and 11 display WEB's "Yahweh" where most English Bibles read "the LORD" — a
real cross-tradition sensitivity (Jewish practice avoids vocalizing the Tetragrammaton; the Catholic
Church's 2008 directive instructs against liturgical use). **Inherited, already-settled ground**: WEB
is fixed by Open Decision #1 and the approved medium brief already displays it. Recorded for the
trail only; no action, no re-litigation.

## 6. Required and Recommended Changes

**6.1 — Item 9: stop presenting summary prose as quoted scripture. [required]**
Per Finding 1 (§3). Apply option 1 (drop the quotation marks and per-card citations; cite Judges
2:11-19 once for the block, matching the approved medium ladder) or option 2 (replace each card with
verbatim WEB text). **Apply the same fix to item 9 of `docs/content/judges-hard.md`, which reuses
this string.** Re-run verbatim QA on the corrected item.

**6.2 — Item 8: reframe the stem from causal "why" to narrative fact. [required]**
Current stem: *"According to the last verse of Judges, why did everyone do what was right in their
own eyes?"* → answer "There was no king in Israel." The verse does not assert that causal link; it
juxtaposes two clauses with no causal connective in the Hebrew, which
[Sefaria/JPS 2023](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Judges.21.25?context=0) renders with a
semicolon ("In those days there was no king in Israel; everyone did as they pleased"). Grading a
"why" therefore grades an inference, which §1 of the style guide excludes ("never from a theological
claim, interpretation, or application the text itself doesn't state outright").

I checked whether the inference is at least uncontested, and it is *mostly* but not entirely:
[Bible Hub, Judges 21:25](https://biblehub.com/commentaries/judges/21-25.htm) shows Ellicott,
Benson, Gill and Barnes all reading the absence of a human king as the cause of the disorder (Barnes:
the disorders "arose from the want of sufficient authority"), with Keil & Delitzsch adding that the
deeper problem was Israel's failure to confess its fault to God. Against that, mainstream
evangelical reference material reads the refrain as *also* about divine kingship —
[BibleRef on Judges 21:25](https://www.bibleref.com/Judges/21/Judges-21-25.html) gives both a
literal sense ("Israel had not yet formed a central monarchy") and a symbolic one ("Israel is
spiritually lawless, ignoring their Lord God"), tying it to Israel's rejection of God as king in
1 Samuel 8.

So the mainstream reading is not unanimous about what "no king" is doing in the sentence, and an
easy-tier player should not be graded on it. **Fix (trivial, preserves the item):** make the stem
about what the verse states — e.g. *"According to Judges 21:25, what was true of Israel in those
days?"* → "There was no king in Israel, and everyone did what was right in his own eyes," or a
fill-in-blank on "king". The three distractors work unchanged under the reworded stem.

**6.3 — Contested Territory: add the "angel of Yahweh" bullet and structural guard. [recommended]**
Per §4.4. Record the divergence (Geneva/Gill: theophany or pre-incarnate Christ; Ellicott: the angel
as God's mouthpiece, both left open) with the Bible Hub source, plus the rule: **no challenge item
in any Judges tier may ask who appeared or spoke to Gideon at the oak (Judges 6:11-12), or grade the
identity of "the angel of Yahweh."** Grading the *content* of the command in 6:14-16 is fine and is
what item 4 does. Same pattern as the Gen 1:2 fix in `docs/reviews/genesis-hard-review.md` §3.3.

**6.4 — Contested Territory: add the "Lappidoth" guard, and anchor items 3 and 7. [recommended]**
Three small precision items:
- **Lappidoth (§4.5):** add the rule that "wife of Lappidoth" is display-only and never a graded
  token, since [Rashi](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Rashi_on_Judges.4.4?context=0) does not read
  it as a personal name.
- **Item 7 (§2):** anchor the stem to the reference. Judges 7:18 records Gideon *instructing* a
  differently-worded shout — "For Yahweh and for Gideon!" — while 7:20 records what was actually
  shouted, "The sword of Yahweh and of Gideon!"
  ([ebible.org JDG07](https://ebible.org/web/JDG07.htm)). No current distractor collides with 7:18,
  so the answer key is safe today; "According to Judges 7:20…" makes it safe permanently. This is
  the genesis-easy §6.1 failure mode caught early rather than late.
- **Item 3 (§5.2):** make the stem explicitly about the army, e.g. "According to Judges 4:16, what
  happened to Sisera's army…", so the elision around Sisera's escape can't read as a claim that he
  died in the battle.

**6.5 — Items 6 and 10 are the same question. [recommended]**
Item 6 (regular MC on 7:2 — "Why did Yahweh say Gideon's army was too large?" → so Israel couldn't
boast) and item 10 (boss MC on 7:2 — "why did Yahweh tell Gideon to reduce his army?" → so Israel
couldn't claim credit) grade the identical fact from the identical verse in the identical format
within one 11-item tier. Doctrinally fine; pedagogically it makes the boss item free for anyone who
just answered item 6. Suggest re-cutting item 10 from a different approved verse (7:20-21 and
4:14-16 are both already approved and only lightly used at this tier), or dropping item 6.

**6.6 — Label the trimmed quotations. [recommended]**
Items 3, 5, 6 and 7 quote trimmed text but are tagged only "(WEB, already-approved verse, new
format)", while the medium brief's convention is "(WEB, trimmed)" — a convention
`docs/reviews/genesis-easy-review.md` §6.2 made a required change when trimming was invisible. Here
the ellipses are present, so the trimming *is* visible and this is a consistency fix rather than an
integrity one. Item 11 already does it correctly ("already-approved verse, trimmed"). Also consider
citing item 11's recall prompt as **Judges 2:19** specifically: the displayed block is 2:18-19, but
the blanked clause ("dealt more ___ than their fathers") lies wholly in v19. The words are in text
order and nothing is spliced across the verse boundary — so this is **not** a repeat of the
genesis-hard Finding A defect — but a prompt-level citation of 2:19 is more precise for the
pipeline's citation check.
