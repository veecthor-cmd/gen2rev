## 3. Contested-Territory Cross-Check

### Ten Commandments numbering (Deut 5)
Checked cross-tradition sources on how the Commandments are divided/numbered:
[My Jewish Learning](https://www.myjewishlearning.com/article/the-ten-commandments/) — Jewish
tradition treats "I am Yahweh your God, who brought you out" (v. 6) as the first of ten sayings;
most Protestant traditions (per the same source) combine "no other gods" and "no carved image" into
one commandment; Catholic and Lutheran traditions combine that material differently again and split
the closing coveting clause into two separate commandments. **Confirmed as a genuine, real,
multi-way cross-tradition difference over how to divide the identical underlying text** — this is
not a fringe disagreement, it's the standard difference taught in comparative-religion contexts.

**The brief's bounding holds and is the right structural response**: referencing verses by
chapter:verse only, never by ordinal commandment number ("the sixth commandment"), makes the
challenge format itself neutral to a dispute that exists entirely at the level of how the text is
*divided*, not what it *says*. This is directly analogous to the Genesis review's Gen 3:15/1:27
format-only fix — no change needed here since the brief already applied this rule proactively in
its "Numbering note," but confirming it holds up under an actual cross-tradition check rather than
taking the brief's own reasoning on faith.

### The Shema's centrality (Deut 6:4-9)
Not independently re-searched for cross-tradition dispute this pass, because there is none to find

### Deuteronomy 30:19-20, "choose life" and free will/covenant theology
This is a live and real debate within Christian tradition specifically (Arminian/free-will readings
vs. Reformed/predestination readings of texts like this one) — well-documented, standard
seminary-level disagreement, not independently re-sourced this pass given how well-established it
is, but flagged with the same seriousness as the Genesis 22 Binding-of-Isaac item. **The brief's
narrative-only bounding holds**: challenge items test only what Moses is recorded as saying, not a
position on free will or sovereignty. **Recommendation:** apply the same structural format
restriction used for Genesis 3:15/1:27 and (in the companion Numbers review) Numbers 23:19 —
recall/fill-in-blank/sequence-ordering only for this verse, never a multiple-choice "what does this
imply" format. Applied directly to `docs/content/deuteronomy.md` as part of this review.

### Moses' unmarked grave (Deut 34:6, not used as challenge text)
Checked both traditions on why the grave location is unknown:
[Sefaria — commentary on Deuteronomy 34:6](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Deuteronomy.34.6)
(Ralbag: prevention of idolatry, explicitly citing the bronze-serpent precedent; Meshekh Chokhmah:
Moses' spiritual detachment; other sources: protection during exile) and
[Bible Hub commentary aggregation](https://biblehub.com/commentaries/deuteronomy/34-6.htm) (Benson
and Poole: prevention of idolatry/superstition, essentially converging with the Jewish reading;
Ellicott, Barnes, Pulpit Commentary: possible bodily translation like Enoch/Elijah, connected by
some to the Transfiguration, NT and out of MVP scope).

**Interesting finding: substantial convergence, not real divergence**, on the primary
"prevent idolatry" explanation across both traditions — the traditions differ more in *supplementary*
theories (translation/resurrection is a distinctly Christian addition tied to NT texts) than in
contradicting each other on the base explanation. Since the brief does not use this clause (v. 6) as
challenge-ready text at all, this is moot for grading purposes regardless. No change needed; noting
the finding for completeness since the brief flagged it as worth checking.

### Violent conquest language adjacent to the Joshua-commissioning scene (Deut 31:3-5, not fully quoted)
The brief already excludes the destruction-of-nations clauses from challenge-ready text and flags

(World 6). This is a forward-looking flag rather than a live issue in *this* brief's actual
challenge content, since the chosen verses (31:2-3, 7-8) stay on the succession/encouragement
material. No change needed for this brief; useful context to hand off to whoever authors Joshua's
