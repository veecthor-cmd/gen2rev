## 4. Contested-Territory Cross-Check

### 4.1 Item 10 — "Gaza" as the graded answer token. **This is the escalation.**

Item 10 asks a player to type **Gaza** to complete: *"Joshua struck them from Kadesh Barnea even to
___, and all the country of Goshen, even to Gibeon."*

What I verified, rather than assumed:

- **The place name is not a dead ancient toponym.** Brown-Driver-Briggs, via
  [Blue Letter Bible, H5804 *ʿazzâ*](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/h5804/kjv/wlc/0-1/),
  identifies it as the Philistine city and gives its modern Arabic name and coordinates — i.e. the
  standard lexical authority itself identifies the biblical site with the present-day city.
- **Joshua's conquest narrative is actively, currently invoked in present-day politics — this is
  documented, not speculative, and it is documented from within Jewish scholarship**, not only by
  outside critics: [TheTorah.com, "Joshua's Conquest: A Cultural and Pedagogical Dilemma in Modern
  Israel"](https://www.thetorah.com/article/joshuas-conquest-a-cultural-and-pedagogical-dilemma-in-modern-israel)
  treats teaching Joshua's conquest to children as a named pedagogical dilemma; Rachel Havrelock,
  ["The Joshua Generation: Conquest and the Promised Land," *Critical Research on Religion*
  (2013)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/2050303213506473) documents Ben-Gurion's Bible
  study group mining Joshua for national ideology and the derivation of *kibush* ("occupation")
  from Joshua's conquests. Evangelical media confirms the same material is live in Christian
  discussion: [Christianity Today, "For Messianic Jews, Debate Over Hamas Gets
  Biblical"](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2023/12/amalekites-today-israel-hamas-war-messianic-jews-bible/).
- **The medium brief already recognised this category of risk** — its third contested-territory
  bullet creates a structural rule precisely because Joshua's land material "is sometimes invoked in
  present-day political debate." It applied that rule to 21:43-45. **It never considered 10:41**,
  because 10:41 had never been made into a challenge item before.

**Why I am not resolving this myself.** The item is *formally* compliant with everything Kachi
signed off on: recall format, one of the two approved geographic/summary verses, no motive or
justification claim. On the style guide alone I would pass it. My concern is not doctrinal and I
want to be precise about that: it is that this tier is the **first-time-player, child-facing** tier,
and the item makes an intensely contested modern place name the *reward token* — the word a child
types and is scored correct for — in a sentence whose verb is "struck them," positioned immediately
after the permanently excluded 10:40. Kachi approved *displaying* 10:41-42; nobody has yet decided
*which word inside it gets graded*, and that is a materially different question that arose only when
this brief created the item.

This is `.claude/agents/theological-reviewer.md` step 7(b) and 7(d): content intersecting the
child-safety guardrail in `GEN2REV_MASTER_PROMPT.md` Section 5, on the one book where the named

### 4.2 Item 9 — Joshua 21:43-45. **A contested item the brief did not flag. Bounding still holds.**

The brief inherits the medium brief's *geopolitical-invocation* note for these verses but misses a
second, entirely separate dispute that `CONTENT_STYLE_GUIDE.md` §3 names **by name** as
out-of-bounds ("covenant theology vs. dispensationalism readings"). Joshua 21:43-45 is one of the
standard battlegrounds of exactly that dispute — whether "Yahweh gave to Israel **all** the land"
records complete, closed fulfilment of the Abrahamic land promise, or a partial fulfilment awaiting
future restoration:

- **Classic Protestant commentators (aggregated)** —
  [Bible Hub, Joshua 21:43 commentaries](https://biblehub.com/commentaries/joshua/21-43.htm):
  Benson, Gill, Barnes, Matthew Henry, Cambridge and Keil-Delitzsch all wrestle with the tension
  against Joshua 13:1 / Judges 1 (land remaining unconquered); Barnes calls the conquest "*ex parte
  Dei* a perfect work," Gill attributes the remainder to Israel's "slothfulness."
- **Dispensational position** — [The Master's Seminary Journal 24/1, "Did God Fulfill Every Good
  Promise? Toward a Biblical Understanding of Joshua 21:43–45"](https://tms.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/tmsj24d.pdf)
  argues these verses cannot mark completed fulfilment of the land covenant.
- **Covenantal/Reformed position** — [Expository Thoughts, "Joshua 21:43-45 and the Promise of
  Land"](https://expositorythoughts.wordpress.com/2008/06/30/joshua-2143-45-and-the-promise-of-land/)
  and [Fundamentally Reformed, "Understanding the Land Promise"](https://www.fundamentallyreformed.com/2008/06/04/understanding-the-land-promise-part-2/)
  read them as fulfilment already accomplished.
- **Jewish rendering** — [Sefaria, Joshua 21:43 (Revised JPS 2023)](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Joshua.21.43?context=0):
  "Israel was given the whole country that GOD had sworn to their fathers to assign them; they took
  possession of it and settled in it."

**The bounding holds, and here is why it holds rather than merely seeming to:** the graded token is
**"land"** — the noun itself. Every source above, across every position, renders that noun the same
way ("land" / "country"); nobody disputes *that the verse says land*. The dispute attaches entirely
to the quantifier "all" and to the verb "gave," neither of which is graded, displayed as a question,
or offered as an option. A player typing "land" affirms nothing a dispensationalist, a covenant
theologian, or a Jewish reader would contest. **No change required to item 9** — but see Finding D
for the structural rule that should be written down so a future pass cannot blank "all."

## 5. Findings and Required Changes

**Finding A — Item 1 (Josh 2:1): a trimmed verse is presented as the whole verse. [required]**
The brief quotes 2:1 as ending at "Go, view the land, including Jericho." Both live sources agree
the verse continues: **"They went and came into the house of a prostitute whose name was Rahab, and
slept there."** ([ebible.org](https://ebible.org/web/JOS02.htm),
[biblehub WEB](https://biblehub.com/web/joshua/2.htm)). The words shown are verbatim, so this is not
paraphrase — but presenting part of a verse as *the* verse in content that will be displayed to a
player is the same integrity concern §3's final bullet exists to prevent, and it is the identical
defect flagged as `genesis-easy-review.md` §6.2. **Fix:** tag it "(WEB, trimmed)" and add a trailing
ellipsis, per the medium brief's own existing convention.
*Upstream note:* `docs/content/joshua.md` presents 2:1 the same way and
`docs/reviews/joshua-review.md` §1 recorded it as a clean "Match." The medium brief should get the
same one-word label fix. Flagging so it is corrected at the source rather than only downstream.

**Finding B — Item 6 (Josh 6:25): the distractor set reaches the excluded material by inference.
[required]**
The stem "Who did Joshua spare when Jericho was taken?" combined with the distractor **"No one was
spared"** (and, secondarily, "All the city's soldiers") requires the player to reason about the fate
of the rest of Jericho's population in order to answer. That content is Joshua 6:21 — the
permanently excluded verse — reached by inference rather than by quotation. The exclusion is not
technically breached (no excluded text appears), but the brief's own stated standard is that no item
touches that material "directly **or by proximity**" (line 18), and this item does not meet the
brief's own standard. It is also the tier aimed at first-time players and children.
**Fix (both parts):**
1. Re-anchor the stem to the verse's own language: *"According to Joshua 6:25, whom did Joshua save
   alive?"* — this also removes the unanchored-stem ambiguity class that produced
   `genesis-easy-review.md` §6.1.
2. Replace **"No one was spared"** with a distractor that carries no claim about the population's
   fate — e.g. *"The seven priests who carried the trumpets"* or *"The two spies' own families."*
   ("The king of Jericho" may stay; it asserts nothing.)
Applied, the item grades only what 6:25 states outright, which §2 permits and all traditions agree
on (§4.3).

**Finding C — Item 4 (Josh 4:6-7): the stated answer key matches none of the options. [required]**
The options list *"That the waters of the Jordan were cut off before the ark of Yahweh's covenant"*
but the answer key reads *"Correct: the Jordan crossing."* Every other item in the brief states the
key as the exact option string. Ambiguity in an answer key is the defect class that broke Genesis
easy item 4. **Fix:** state the key as the verbatim option string.
*Secondary (recommended):* the stem says "the **twelve** stones," but the word "twelve" is not in
4:6-7 — it is in 4:8 and 4:20. The stem is a legitimate narrative fact from the scene, but if the
item displays only 4:6-7 the player cannot see the number. Either say "the stones set up at the
Jordan" or additionally cite 4:8.

**Finding D — Contested Territory: add the 21:43-45 fulfilment rule. [required]**
The brief's contested-territory section does not mention that 21:43-45 is a named
covenant-vs-dispensationalism battleground (§4.2), which `CONTENT_STYLE_GUIDE.md` §3 lists by name
as out-of-bounds. Item 9 is safe, but the section must record the dispute and add the structural
rule, in the same style as the existing ones: **the quantifier "all" in 21:43 may never be the
blanked token, and no item at any tier may ask whether the promise was completely fulfilled, or
offer "all"/"part" of the land as options.** Without this written down, a later authoring pass can
cross the line without knowing it exists.

**Finding E — Item 8: label the sequence cards as narrative summaries. [recommended]**
The four ordering cards are in quotation marks with verse citations attached ("Israel crosses the
Jordan on dry ground" (Josh 3:15-17)), but they are the drafter's narrative summaries, not WEB text.
The sequence itself is correct (ch.2 → 3 → 4 → 6, verified against the text). **Fix:** drop the
quotation marks or tag the block "card labels — narrative summary, not verse quotation," so neither
the ingestion pipeline's citation check (`BACKEND_REQUIREMENTS.md` §4) nor a player mistakes them
for scripture.

**Finding F — Item 3: citation range. [recommended]**
Labelled "Josh 3:15-17"; the quoted text is entirely from 3:16-17 (3:15 is the priests' feet
dipping in the water, which is omitted). The leading ellipsis is honest, but citing 3:16-17 is more
precise for the pipeline. Same as the medium brief — fix in both or neither.

**Finding G — Items 1 and 4: inner quotation marks dropped. [QA, not a review defect]**
WEB prints nested quotations: 4:6 as `saying, "What do you mean by these stones?"` and 4:7 as
`then you shall tell them, "Because the waters…"` ([biblehub WEB Joshua 4](https://biblehub.com/web/joshua/4.htm)).
The brief renders them without quotes. This is brief-level formatting, not a text error, but the
ingested record must carry WEB's punctuation. For `docs/qa/joshua-easy-verbatim-qa.md`.

**Finding H — Item 7: casing deviation. [QA, not a review defect]**
The brief reads "…now, behold, I am eighty-five years old, today." Both sources print **"Now,
behold, I am eighty-five years old, today."** — capital N, since it opens the second sentence of
14:10. A one-character deviation, inherited from the medium brief. Flag for verbatim QA.

**Finding I — the "forty years old" distractor in item 7. [note only]**
Textually the key is right (14:10, "eighty-five years old, today"). But Joshua 14:7 has Caleb say
"Forty years old was I when Moses… sent me," so a player answering "forty" may be drawing on the
chapter itself. The stem is anchored ("when he asked Joshua for the hill country"), so there is no
ambiguity defect — same class as the Genesis "a lamb" distractor note. Consider swapping if
playtesting shows confusion.

## 6. Verdict

**`escalate-to-human`** — scoped to **item 10's graded token only**.

**One-line reason:** the ḥerem exclusion holds cleanly and every verse is verbatim-correct against
two live WEB sources, but item 10 — the brief's one genuinely new graded item — makes "Gaza" the
typed answer key in a child-facing tier, and that specific decision has never been put to the named
human reviewer who set this book's bounding.

**What is *not* escalated:** items 1–9. With Findings A–D applied they are `approved-with-changes`.
None of them requires a doctrinal or denominational position, and both contested items I checked
independently (21:43-45's fulfilment dispute; 6:25's framing) resolve to graded tokens that
disagreeing traditions render identically.

**Recommended path for the calling session:**
1. Put §4.1's four options to Kachi. Any of the four is a legitimate outcome; option 1 (blank
   "Gibeon" instead) costs one word and closes it.
2. Apply Findings A–D regardless of how item 10 is resolved.
3. Re-run this review only if item 10 changes verse; if only the blank moves within 10:41, a
   re-check of the new token against §1 is sufficient.

**Known limitation, stated plainly per this agent's definition:** escalation on this project has
historically had nowhere to land (Open Decision #7), though for *this book specifically* it does —
Kachi Okere took the named-human-reviewer role on 2026-08-08 and personally set the bounding this
escalation sits inside. **This escalation is unresolved as of this review and should be surfaced,
not silently dropped.**

**Residual risk:** this review was performed by an AI agent standing in for the human theological
reviewer scoped in `GEN2REV_MASTER_PROMPT.md` (substitution logged in `MEMORY.md`, 2026-08-08). A
human specialist may weigh Finding B or §4.1 differently — most likely more strictly, not less.

**Outstanding, and not this reviewer's job:** independent verbatim QA per `QA_REQUIREMENTS.md` §3
(`docs/qa/joshua-easy-verbatim-qa.md`), which should prioritise Findings G and H and re-check item
10's blank if it moves. `docs/CONTENT_REVIEW_LOG.md` is to be updated by the calling session, not by
