## 3. Contested-Territory Cross-Check

### Edom as a later cipher for Rome/Christendom in Jewish tradition
This is the book's most theologically interesting item, and received the deepest check this pass:
- **Rabbinic tradition (via Cambridge University Press's *Jacob & Esau* and related scholarship,
  surfaced through general search):** Rabbi Akiva was the earliest explicitly-attested Jewish
  authority to identify Esau/Edom with Rome, reading Genesis 27:22's "the voice is the voice of
  Jacob, but the hands are the hands of Esau" as a reference to Rome's destruction of Betar during
  the Bar Kokhba revolt. After Rome's Christianization in the 4th century, "Edom" became a cipher not
  just for the Roman state but for Christendom as a religious entity, a reading that persisted and
  developed throughout later rabbinic and medieval Jewish literature.
- **On Obadiah specifically:** [Chabad.org's "Who Was the Prophet Obadiah?"](https://www.chabad.org/library/article_cdo/aid/3514794/jewish/Who-Was-the-Prophet-Obadiah.htm)
  notes a distinct rabbinic tradition (unrelated to the Rome/Christendom typology, but illustrating
  how much interpretive tradition has accreted around this short book) that Obadiah was himself a
  convert from Edom, chosen to prophesy against his own former people; a second Chabad source, ["On
  the Haftarah: The Vision of Ovadia"](https://www.chabad.org/parshah/article_cdo/aid/3532385/jewish/On-the-Haftarah-The-Vision-of-Ovadia-and-What-It-All-Means.htm),
  situates the book within the annual Torah-reading cycle's own framing of Jacob-and-Esau material.

**Conclusion: confirmed genuine, real interpretive layer specific to Jewish tradition**, with no
directly comparable typological tradition on the Christian side identifying a later specific
entity with Edom in the same programmatic way (mainstream Christian commentary on Obadiah, per a
general survey including [enduringword.com's Obadiah commentary](https://enduringword.com/bible-commentary/obadiah-1/),
reads the book primarily as historical judgment oracle plus a type of eschatological judgment on
nations generally, not as a coded reference to a specific later empire or religion the way the
rabbinic Rome/Christendom reading functions). **The brief's bounding holds**: its challenge items
(vv. 3-4, 10, 12, 15, 17) test only what Obadiah's own text states about the historical nation of
Edom named in the oracle, never what "Edom" symbolizes in later tradition or any identification with
a later or contemporary group. No change needed.

### Dating of Obadiah
Confirmed via general search (e.g. the Jewish Encyclopedia entry on Obadiah, surfaced during
research) that most scholars place the book shortly after Jerusalem's fall in 586 BC based on its
description of foreigners entering the city's gates, though this reading is an inference from the
text's content rather than a stated date, consistent with the brief's own careful "most scholars
place... though this dating is not settled" phrasing. No change needed.
