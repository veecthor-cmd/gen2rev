### Finding A — item 8 (Deut 34:9): trim presented as the whole verse. **Must fix.**

The brief renders item 8 as:

> **Deut 34:9** (WEB, new): "Joshua the son of Nun was full of the spirit of wisdom, for Moses had
> laid his hands on him."

The live WEB verse is longer:

> **Deut 34:9** — "Joshua the son of Nun was full of the spirit of wisdom, for Moses had laid his
> hands on him. **The children of Israel listened to him, and did as Yahweh commanded Moses.**"
> ([ebible.org DEU34](https://ebible.org/web/DEU34.htm))

The words shown *are* verbatim — this is not paraphrase — but a partial verse is presented, tagged
"(WEB, new)", with no indication that it continues, in an item that displays that text to a player
as the verse. That is the same integrity concern `CONTENT_STYLE_GUIDE.md` §3 (final bullet) and §5
(citation must carry the verbatim text) exist to prevent, and it is the identical defect
`docs/reviews/genesis-easy-review.md` §6.2 caught on Gen 8:11. Both the `medium` and `easy` briefs
in this pipeline already have a convention for this ("WEB, trimmed").

**Concrete fix — pick one:**

1. **Restore the full verse** in the displayed text, keeping the blank where it is: "Joshua the son
   of Nun was full of the spirit of wisdom, for Moses had laid his ___ on him. The children of
   Israel listened to him, and did as Yahweh commanded Moses." → **hands**. *(Preferred — it
   preserves the answer token, and the restored second sentence is itself plain narrative fact.)*
2. **Re-tag the item "(WEB, trimmed)"** and add a leading/trailing ellipsis per the convention used
   elsewhere in this pipeline.

Doctrinally the item is in-bounds either way; this is a text-integrity fix. **Item 8 is not eligible
for ingestion until fixed and re-run through verbatim QA per `QA_REQUIREMENTS.md` §3.**

Note on why this slipped: the brief's stated method was to fetch each *reference* fresh, and 34:9's
first sentence is the memorable half. The Genesis pilot's lesson generalizes — the risk is not
mis-remembered words, it's mis-drawn verse *edges*. Which is also why §2 below exists.

## 2. Verse-Boundary Integrity of Item 2 (Deut 5:9-10) — checked specifically. **Clean.**

The review request asked me to check item 2 against the exact defect
`docs/reviews/genesis-hard-review.md` Finding A caught (a blank cut from a block that spliced two
verses' clauses into one quotation, cited to the wrong single verse). Item 2 is **not** an instance
of that defect. The verse division per both live sources:

- **Deut 5:9** — "You shall not bow yourself down to them, nor serve them, for I, Yahweh your God,
  am a jealous God, visiting the iniquity of the fathers on the children and on the third and on the
  fourth generation of those who hate me"
- **Deut 5:10** — "and showing loving kindness to thousands of those who love me and keep my
  commandments."

The brief's quotation is these two verses concatenated **in their original order, with no words
added, removed, or re-arranged**, and it is cited as **"Deut 5:9-10"** — a citation that correctly
covers both. That is materially different from Gen 9:13, where the brief presented a *re-ordered*
splice under a *single wrong* verse number. Confirmed identical on both sources.

Three precision points that do follow from the boundary, all non-blocking:

- **The graded token lives in v10, not v9.** The blank ("thousands") falls entirely inside verse 10.
  Recommend the item carry a secondary, blank-level citation ("blank: Deut 5:10") so the ingestion
  pipeline's citation check (`BACKEND_REQUIREMENTS.md` §4) and the verbatim-QA pass both have the
  precise reference for the graded string, not just the display block. See §6.1.
- **The displayed text opens on a dangling referent.** Verse 9 begins "You shall not bow yourself
  down to **them**" — the antecedent is the carved image of **v8**, which is deliberately not
  displayed. This is not a text error and I do **not** recommend adding v8 (v8 is the carved-image
  material that sits squarely on the Ten Commandments numbering fault line — see §4.1). Recommend
  instead that the item's stem anchor the reference explicitly rather than leaving a player to guess
  what "them" is.
- **For verbatim QA:** confirm whether WEB prints any punctuation at the v9/v10 join. Both sources
  rendered the concatenation as "...those who hate me and showing loving kindness..." with no comma,
  and the brief matches that; flagging it because a verse-join is exactly where a stray comma would
  hide.

## 4. Contested-Territory Cross-Check

I did not accept the brief's proposed bounding on any of the four flagged items. Each was checked
against live sources representing genuinely different traditions.

### 4.1 Deut 5:9-10 — generational punishment. **Bounding holds. Tension confirmed real but narrower than the brief implies.**

Sources consulted, three traditions:

- **Jewish — Revised JPS (2023) via Sefaria**: "visiting the guilt of the parents upon the children,
  upon the third and upon the fourth generations of those who reject Me."
  [sefaria.org/api/texts/Deuteronomy.5.9](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Deuteronomy.5.9?context=0)
- **Jewish — Rashi on the Exodus 20:5 parallel via Sefaria**: on "of them that hate me," Rashi reads
  "when they retain in their hands (follow the example of) the evil doings of their ancestors,"
  citing Sanhedrin 27b — i.e. punishment is **conditional on the children continuing the conduct**,
  not automatic by descent.
  [sefaria.org/api/texts/Rashi_on_Exodus.20.5](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Rashi_on_Exodus.20.5?context=0)
  *(Note: Sefaria returns empty commentary bodies for Rashi/Ibn Ezra at Deuteronomy 5:9 itself — the
  substantive rabbinic material sits on the Exodus 20 parallel, which is where I went.)*
- **Classic Protestant commentators — Bible Hub aggregation, Exodus 20:5**: Ellicott — "the penalty
  upon the children is not final or irreversible… as respects another world, their parents'
  iniquities will not be visited on them," **citing Ezekiel 18:20 directly**; Poole raises the
  objection himself ("Quest. How can this be just?") and answers that it speaks "not of eternal
  damnation, but of temporal punishments, in which there is no shadow of injustice";
  Keil-Delitzsch — "natural consequences" transmitted through family structure; Pulpit Commentary —
  inherited *disadvantage*, "the seeds both of physical and moral evil."
  [biblehub.com/commentaries/exodus/20-5.htm](https://biblehub.com/commentaries/exodus/20-5.htm)
- **Classic Protestant — Bible Hub, Deuteronomy 5:9**: thinner; only Ellicott engages substantively
  ("it is not meant here that the soul of the son shall die for the father. The penalty extends only
  'to them that hate me'").
  [biblehub.com/commentaries/deuteronomy/5-9.htm](https://biblehub.com/commentaries/deuteronomy/5-9.htm)
- **Contemporary evangelical — GotQuestions**: puts the Ezekiel tension in the foreground explicitly
  ("So, do children bear responsibility for their parents' sin? Yes and no"), resolving it as
  inherited *effects* but not inherited *guilt*.
  [gotquestions.org/sins-of-the-father.html](https://www.gotquestions.org/sins-of-the-father.html)

**Assessment.** The drafter is right that this is live ground and right to have flagged it — the
Ezekiel 18 adjacency is not hypothetical, it is raised by name in the commentary tradition itself.
But the finding is more favourable than the brief assumed: the mainstream Jewish and mainstream
Christian readings **converge substantially** on the operative point (Rashi's conditionality via
Sanhedrin 27b is functionally the same move as Ellicott's "extends only to them that hate me"). The
live disagreement is over *mechanism* — temporal vs. eternal, natural consequence vs. covenantal
sanction — not over whether innocent descendants inherit judicial guilt, which essentially no
mainstream source affirms.

**Why the bounding holds, not merely sounds plausible:** the graded token is **"thousands,"** in the
mercy clause of v10. Every source consulted renders that clause the same way (Revised JPS: "showing
kindness to the thousandth generation"; WEB: "showing loving kindness to thousands") and **none of
them disputes the number**. A player typing "thousands" affirms nothing a Jewish, Catholic, Orthodox
or Protestant reader would contest. The punishment clause is display-only and is never the graded
string. The brief's structural rule is the right instrument and is correctly applied.

**One thing the brief's rule does not yet cover** — see §6.2: the rule forbids the blank falling on
the punishment clause and forbids asking what the verse *means*, but does not forbid a future
authoring pass blanking a *neutral-looking* word inside the punishment clause (e.g. "on the third
and on the ___ generation" → "fourth"). That would be textually valid and would still foreground the
contested clause as the object of the exercise for no pedagogical gain. Close it explicitly, same
way `docs/reviews/genesis-hard-review.md` Recommendation 1 closed the Gen 1:26 "us/our" opening.

**Numbering fault line, separately confirmed clear.** Displaying 5:9-10 as a quoted unit takes no
position on the Ten Commandments division — Jewish tradition groups 5:7-10 as the second saying,
Catholic/Lutheran tradition folds 5:6-10 into the first commandment, Reformed tradition splits 5:7
from 5:8-10. Because the item carries no ordinal label and does not display v8, it is neutral to all
three. The brief's inherited chapter:verse-only rule does the work here. (Versification itself is
not an issue: Sefaria returns the same content at Deuteronomy 5:9 as WEB does, so the Hebrew and
English verse numbers align at this point in the chapter.)

### 4.2 Deut 6:8-9 — tefillin/mezuzah, literal vs. figurative. **Divergence confirmed live and sharp. Bounding holds.**

This is the strongest of the four flags — the drafter's claim is not only correct, the divergence is
stated *explicitly and by name* in the sources, which is unusual.

- **Jewish — Rashi on Deut 6:8 via Sefaria**: "These are the Tephillin that are placed on the arm" /
  "these are the Tephillin that are placed upon the head," with the טטפת etymology from Sanhedrin 4b.
  A direct, literal, halakhic derivation.
  [sefaria.org/api/texts/Rashi_on_Deuteronomy.6.8](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Rashi_on_Deuteronomy.6.8?context=0)
- **Jewish — My Jewish Learning on tefillin**: describes four Torah passages commanding "bind[ing]
  them as a sign upon [their] hands," with tefillin "intended to fulfill that commandment" and those
  passages handwritten on the parchments inside.
  [myjewishlearning.com/article/tefillin](https://www.myjewishlearning.com/article/tefillin/)
- **Classic Protestant commentators — Bible Hub, Deuteronomy 6:8**: the **Cambridge Bible** states
  the injunction is "probably… to be taken metaphorically and not literally, **as the later Jews
  understood it**"; **Keil-Delitzsch** — the words "are figurative, and denote an undeviating
  observance of the divine commands"; **Benson** — "the spirit of the command… was, that they should
  give all diligence… to keep God's laws always in remembrance"; **Matthew Henry (Concise)** — "This
  seems to have been binding in the letter of it to the Jews, as it is to us in the intent of it."
  Ellicott, JFB, Gill and the Pulpit Commentary describe the Jewish literal practice descriptively
  and without polemic.
  [biblehub.com/commentaries/deuteronomy/6-8.htm](https://biblehub.com/commentaries/deuteronomy/6-8.htm)

**Assessment.** This is a textbook `CONTENT_STYLE_GUIDE.md` §3 "denominational position" trap. An
item of the shape "Is this command meant literally or figuratively?" — or even a seemingly innocent
MC "What does 'bind them for a sign on your hand' refer to?" — would have a correct answer only by
picking a tradition. The Cambridge Bible sentence above is a mainstream Christian commentary
explicitly contradicting the normative Jewish reading of the same verse. This must never become a
graded item.

**Why the bounding holds:** the graded token is **"hand,"** which is what the verse says in every
tradition's own text (Revised JPS renders the parallel "bind them as a sign on your hand"). Rashi
and Cambridge disagree completely about what to *do* about the sentence while agreeing completely
about what the sentence *says*. Recall-only on the plain instructional wording is exactly the right
instrument, and the brief's rule ("grades only the plain instructional text… no challenge in any
tier may ask whether the instruction is meant literally or figuratively") is well-drafted.

**One addition recommended (§6.2):** Rashi's own wording is "the Tephillin that are placed on the
**arm**" — Hebrew *yad* covers hand and arm, and English versions and traditions split on the
rendering. Add to the structural rule that **no challenge may grade the hand/arm rendering**, and
that "frontlets" (a genuinely obscure and variously-rendered word — Revised JPS uses the
transliterated *totafot*, the sources above call it "enigmatic") **may never be the blanked token**.
Both are translation-level traps that would produce an unfair answer key without any doctrinal
question ever being asked.

### 4.3 Deut 34:6 — Moses' unmarked grave. **Bounding holds — and is more necessary than the prior review concluded.**

- **Jewish — Revised JPS (2023) via Sefaria**: "who buried him in the valley in the land of Moab,
  near Beth-peor; and no one knows his burial place to this day."
  [sefaria.org/api/texts/Deuteronomy.34.6](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Deuteronomy.34.6?context=0)
- **Classic Christian commentators — Bible Hub, Deuteronomy 34:6**: genuinely and explicitly divided
  on *why*. **Prevention of veneration:** Benson — God concealed the place "to prevent their
  superstition and idolatry"; JFB — "to prevent its being ranked among holy places."
  **Explicitly rejecting that explanation:** **Barnes** — "**Hardly**, lest the grave of Moses should
  become an object of superstitious honor," instead suggesting Moses "was shortly translated
  (transported) from it" like Enoch and Elijah. **Bodily translation:** Keil-Delitzsch — the
  concealment aimed "to place him in the same category with Enoch and Elijah"; Pulpit Commentary —
  "like Enoch and Elijah, he was transferred to the invisible world." Several cite **Jude 1:9**
  (Michael disputing over Moses' body) in support.
  [biblehub.com/commentaries/deuteronomy/34-6.htm](https://biblehub.com/commentaries/deuteronomy/34-6.htm)

**Correction to `docs/reviews/deuteronomy-review.md`.** That review recorded "substantial
convergence, not real divergence" on the prevent-idolatry explanation. On a fresh look that is not
what the sources show: **Barnes rejects the idolatry explanation outright**, and the
translation/assumption reading held by Barnes, Keil-Delitzsch and the Pulpit Commentary is a
substantively different claim about what happened to Moses' body — one anchored in a **New Testament
text (Jude 9) that is outside MVP scope entirely**. So this is a live, three-way, partly
*intra*-Christian disagreement, not a converging one. The earlier review reached its conclusion
while the clause was not being used as challenge text, so nothing shipped wrongly — but that
conclusion should not be relied on now that this tier actually grades the verse.

**The bounding still holds, and this is why it matters.** The graded token is **"tomb"** — the
narrative fact that the location is unknown, which is the one thing every source above states
outright ("All agree the burial location remains factually unknown to this day"; Revised JPS "no one
knows his burial place to this day"). The brief's rule — grades only *that* it is unknown, never
*why*, no challenge in any tier may ask for or imply a reason — is precisely the rule that keeps this
safe, and it is now doing real work rather than being belt-and-braces. **Do not weaken it, and do not
let a future tier add a "why" item on the strength of the earlier review's convergence finding.**

**One addition recommended (§6.2):** the item displays "**He** buried him in the valley…" — WEB makes
Yahweh the subject; Revised JPS's relative clause "who buried him" does the same; commentators
variously assign the act to God directly or to an angel (Geneva: "the angel of the Lord"; Gill:
Michael). Add that **no challenge may grade *who* buried Moses**. The current item does not, and
should not start.

### 4.4 Deut 30:11-14 — "the word is very near you." **Bounding holds. The drafter under-stated the divergence; it is Jewish/Christian, not just an NT adjacency.**

The brief frames this as a Romans 10 *adjacency* with "no grading risk." The adjacency framing is
correct as far as it goes, but the live disagreement is larger than the brief says — it is about the
**referent of "this commandment"/"the word" in the OT passage itself**, and the traditions genuinely
differ:

- **Jewish — Ramban (Nachmanides) on Deut 30:11 via Sefaria**: "this commandment" refers
  **specifically to repentance** (*teshuvah*), not to the Torah as a whole — "this commandment refers
  to [the commandment of] repentance aforementioned," accessible to all Israel "at all times and in
  all places."
  [sefaria.org/api/texts/Ramban_on_Deuteronomy.30.11](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Ramban_on_Deuteronomy.30.11?context=0)
- **Christian commentators — Bible Hub, Deuteronomy 30:14**: read it as **the law** and its
  practicability, then divide over Paul's reuse. **JFB** — "the apostle Paul (Ro 10:6-8) has applied
  this passage to the Gospel," holding "the law of Christ is substantially the same as that of
  Moses." **Matthew Henry (Concise)** — the passage is "especially true of the gospel of Christ."
  **Keil-Delitzsch** — refers to the law, but "the declaration, 'the word is in thy heart,' only
  attains its full realization through the preaching of the gospel of grace." **Cambridge Bible** —
  pointedly distinguishes Paul's use as "his inspired conviction and experience of the Gospel"
  rather than the original author's intent.
  [biblehub.com/commentaries/deuteronomy/30-14.htm](https://biblehub.com/commentaries/deuteronomy/30-14.htm)

**Assessment.** So there are at least three live positions on what the passage is *about*: repentance
(Ramban), the law's practicability (Cambridge, Keil), and the gospel by Pauline reapplication
(Henry, JFB) — with Cambridge and Henry effectively disagreeing with each other about whether the
gospel reading is the passage's own sense or a later application. Any item asking "what is 'the
word' that is near?" would require picking one, and would be out-of-bounds under §3 on two counts at
once (doctrinal interpretation *and* denominational position).

**Why the bounding holds:** the graded token is **"near."** The nearness is the one thing all three
positions assert — it is the shared premise of the disagreement, not a party to it (Ramban:
repentance is "not hard, nor far off from you"; Keil/Cambridge: the law is practicable; Henry/JFB:
the gospel is accessible). The item grades the adjective and nothing else. **The brief's conclusion
is right; its reasoning is incomplete.** Recommend (§6.2) that the Contested Territory entry be
rewritten to record the Ramban/Cambridge/Henry divergence about the *referent* — not just the Romans
10 adjacency — and to add the structural rule: **no challenge in any tier may ask what "this
commandment" or "the word" refers to**, and neither phrase may be a blanked token.
