## 3. Contested-Territory Cross-Check

### 2 Samuel 7, the Davidic covenant — messianic reading
Confirmed as genuine, significant, live cross-tradition divergence, and confirmed it runs deeper
than a simple "Christians read it messianically, Jews don't":
- **Christian tradition:** reads "I will establish the throne of his kingdom forever" (7:13) and
  "your throne will be established forever" (7:16) as ultimately fulfilled in Jesus as the eternal
  Davidic heir, building on the New Testament's own use of this passage (Peter's citation in Acts,
  per search-aggregated commentary). [Source: search results summarizing GotQuestions.org and
  related evangelical commentary on the Davidic covenant](https://www.gotquestions.org/Davidic-covenant.html).
- **Jewish tradition:** reads the covenant primarily as a promise about David's dynastic line,
  with a real and proximate fulfillment in Solomon (who built the temple, per 7:13's immediate
  narrative context) — but this is not simply "no messianic reading at all." The Jewish Bible
  Quarterly source and the Amidah liturgy itself ("May the offshoot of Your servant David soon
  flower") show later Jewish tradition also connects Davidic kingship to messianic hope, in a
  form distinct from the Christian Christological reading. [Source: Jewish Bible
  Quarterly, "The Eternal Davidic Covenant in II Samuel Chapter 7"](https://jbqnew.jewishbible.org/jbq-past-issues/2016/442-april-june-2016/eternal-davidic-covenant-ii-samuel-chapter-7-later-manifestations-bible/).

This is exactly the kind of "genuine, live, unresolved disagreement among mainstream traditions
bearing directly on a graded item" the reviewer procedure's escalation criterion (a) describes —
except here the brief's narrative-only bounding (test only the covenant's stated terms, never
whose fulfillment reading is correct) does neutralize it, provided the *format* can't imply a
fulfillment answer. A multiple-choice item asking "who does 'his kingdom' refer to" would force a
correct answer that sides with one tradition. **Recommend the same structural fix used in the
Genesis and 1 Samuel reviews:** restrict 7:13 and 7:16 to recall/fill-in-blank format only.

### 2 Samuel 11-12, Bathsheba/Uriah/Nathan — the sensitivity-flagged scene
This item was checked against both the theological-neutrality axis and the content-sensitivity/
audience-appropriateness axis, per the assignment's explicit instruction to handle this scene with
extra care.

**Theological-neutrality axis:** the brief's chosen verses (12:1, 12:7, 12:13) are narrative fact
and direct quotation only — no doctrinal claim is graded. This axis is clean.

**Content-sensitivity axis:** researched how mainstream children's/general-audience Christian
education actually handles this exact scene, since that's the closest available real-world
precedent for a "Disciple tier / general audience" product. Multiple sources converge on the same
approach this brief already takes — focus on Nathan's parable and David's repentance, and do not
explain or depict the encounter itself in the youngest-audience material, with detail added only
for older audiences. [Sources: Ministry-to-Children lesson on David and
Bathsheba](https://ministry-to-children.com/lesson-david-and-bathsheba-david-does-wrong-and-is-punished-by-god/),
[MinistrySpark, "How to Teach Kids About the Difficult Parts of David and
Bathsheba"](https://ministryspark.com/how-to-teach-kids-david-bathsheba/).

This is reassuring precedent, and the brief's bounding is not obviously wrong — if anything it
matches common practice closely. **However**, per `PRODUCT_REQUIREMENTS.md` §2, "Disciple" tier is
explicitly *general audience*, not the child-directed "Seeker" tier — meaning the precedent found
above (children's ministry materials) is analogous but not a perfect match for who will actually
play this world, and the product itself is shared informally ("opened from a link shared in a text
thread or church group"), so the reviewer cannot be fully confident about the actual age range
reached in practice. Combined with the fact that this scene includes both sexual content and
premeditated killing — the reviewer procedure's escalation criterion (b) explicitly names content
intersecting child-safety/content-integrity guardrails as grounds for escalation regardless of how
well-reasoned the bounding looks on paper.

**Per the assignment's explicit instruction** ("if you're not confident your bounding is
appropriately conservative for the target audience, use `escalate-to-human` rather than forcing an
approval") **and the reviewer procedure's own framing that escalation is a successful outcome, not
a failure: this item is escalated to human review.** The brief's current bounding (12:1, 12:7,
12:13 only, no chapter 11 verses) is the reviewer's recommendation as the safest currently-known
option, and is left in place in the brief rather than being pulled — but a named human reviewer
should confirm it before this book moves to `approved` status in `docs/CONTENT_REVIEW_LOG.md`, per
the "Known limitation" section of `.claude/agents/theological-reviewer.md`: Open Decision #7 has no
named human theological reviewer yet, so this escalation currently has nowhere to formally land.
That gap is being surfaced here explicitly, not silently.
