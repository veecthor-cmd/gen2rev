## Design intent for this tier

Per Kachi's "hard... needs very deep scripture knowledge" direction. This tier draws on less
commonly memorized, secondary details from the SAME 5 scenes — the second half of an oath, a
supporting character's name, a less-quoted clause — and requires typed recall wherever the format
allows, in contrast to the easy tier's multiple-choice favoring. As with the easy tier, this brief
draws **only** on verse text already quoted verbatim and reviewed in the medium brief.

**Unused-but-approved verse identified and put to use:** `docs/content/ruth-easy.md` notes that
`docs/content/ruth.md` lists five challenge-ready verses medium's actual item set never turned into a
standalone item — Ruth 1:8-9, 2:11-12, 3:1-2, 4:13-14, and 4:21-22. The easy tier used four of the
five (1:8-9, 2:11-12, 3:1-2, 4:21-22); this brief picks up the fifth, **Ruth 4:13-14**, which in
`medium` is quoted only inside the boss sequence item and never appears as its own standalone item.

## Contested Territory

Everything already flagged in `docs/content/ruth.md` applies unchanged. **No new *verse text* is
introduced by this brief — that claim is true and verified.** *(Softened per review's consequential
edit, required: the original claim, "Nothing new is introduced by this brief: no new verse, no new
scene," did not hold at the level of contested *ground* — items 1 and 6 promote two verses to
standalone graded status for the first time in any tier, and each sits on a real cross-tradition
divergence not previously flagged. Both are recorded below; both graded tokens survive the
disagreement intact, so this is a documentation fix, not a content problem.)*

- **Ruth's Moabite identity and Deuteronomy 23:3 — confirmed still convergent, not re-litigated.**
  Item 3 (2:2-3) displays "Ruth the Moabitess" and item 7 (4:9-10, boss) displays it again — both
  direct from already-approved verse text. Per `docs/reviews/ruth-review.md`, this tension resolves
  via cross-tradition convergence (not a live split), and this brief's items test only narrative fact
  (that Ruth was a Moabite, that she was taken as Boaz's wife), never the lawfulness question. No new
  bounding required — same conclusion as the easy tier.
- **Ruth 4:14, the identity of the `go'el` — new bullet, added per review Finding A, required.** Item
  6 makes Ruth 4:13-14 a standalone graded item for the first time in any tier. Who is the "near
  kinsman" the women bless, and whose name is to be famous — the newborn Obed, or Boaz? This is a live
  disagreement running *across and within* traditions: classic Protestant commentary is genuinely split
  against itself (Pulpit Commentary, Keil-Delitzsch and Gill read the child as the kinsman; Barnes and
  Matthew Poole read Boaz); the Catholic NABRE's notes identify the child as the one who "becomes the
  redeemer in the family"; the Revised JPS (Sefaria) leaves the referent open. **Why the bounding
  holds:** the graded token is "famous" — the Catholic NABRE independently arrives at "famous" despite
  reading the referent differently from half the Protestant commentators, so the word is stable across
  the disagreement. **Structural rule: the blank on 4:14 stays on "famous"; no challenge in any tier
  may grade who the "near kinsman" of 4:14 is, or whose name is to be famous, and 4:14 must never
  become a multiple-choice item whose options turn on that referent** (or on competing renderings —
  JPS renders the same clause "perpetuated" rather than "famous," a translation divergence for the
  QA/ingest trail, not a doctrinal one).
- **Ruth 1:16-17, the declaration as conversion (`giyur`) vs. loyalty oath — new bullet, added per
  review Finding B, required.** Item 1 grades a token from Ruth's oath (1:17). Jewish tradition reads
  1:16-17 as the paradigm conversion text — Rashi on Ruth 1:16 (Sefaria) states that "from here our
  Rabbis derived that if a prospective proselyte comes to convert, we inform him of some punishments,"
  reading Naomi as teaching Ruth Shabbat boundaries, the 613 commandments, and the prohibition of
  idolatry — a formal conversion catechesis. Christian commentary overwhelmingly frames the same speech
  as a declaration of loyalty and personal faith rather than a halakhic procedure. **Why the bounding
  holds:** the graded token is "death," in the clause "if anything but death parts you and me" — a
  word every tradition and translation renders identically, carrying none of the conversion/loyalty
  framing. **Structural rule: the blank on 1:17 stays on "death"; no challenge may grade what Ruth's
  declaration made her, accomplished, or constituted.** This rule is intended to bind the easy and
  medium tiers as well, since medium already ships a 1:16 item and this brief's sibling
  `docs/content/ruth-easy.md` ships a 1:16 item — recorded here per the review's recommendation; this
  editing pass has not modified `docs/content/ruth.md` (out of scope, already-approved medium tier).
- **Ruth 4:9-10 (the redemption transaction language) format compliance.** The existing structural
  rule restricts this verse to recall/fill-in-blank or narrative-sequence format only, never
  multiple-choice. Item 7 is recall/fill-in-blank, grades only the word "witnesses," and does not ask
  whether the transaction or the kinsman-redeemer custom was fair or appropriate by modern standards.
  **Compliant.**
- **The kinsman-redeemer / levirate custom more broadly (items 6, 7, 9).** Item 6 (4:13-14) tests only
  the women's blessing over Naomi after Obed's birth; item 9 (2:11-12) tests only Boaz's stated
  praise. Neither invites a player to evaluate the custom itself by modern standards — consistent with
  the comprehension-only bounding already established in `docs/content/ruth.md`.
- **No other new contested ground.** Every item above draws on verse text already verified in
  `docs/reviews/ruth-review.md` §1; no new quotation or narrative claim beyond what that review
  already checked.
