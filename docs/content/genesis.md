## Setting / Era

Genesis spans the widest time range of any book in the MVP list: from Creation itself through the
patriarchal era (Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, Joseph) down to the family of Israel settling in Egypt.
As World 1, it establishes the game's premise — the created order, humanity's rupture with it, and
God's chosen way forward through one family — before the story narrows into Israel's national
history in the following 12 worlds (Exodus onward). No historicity/dating claims are made or
implied by this brief; see Contested Territory below.

## Pivotal Scenes (5)

### 1. Creation (Genesis 1)
God speaks the world into being over six days, culminating in humanity made in his image, and
declares it very good.

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- Genesis 1:1-5 (WEB): "In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth. The earth was
  formless and empty. Darkness was on the surface of the deep and God's Spirit was hovering over
  the surface of the waters. God said, 'Let there be light,' and there was light. God saw the
  light, and saw that it was good. God divided the light from the darkness. God called the light
  'day', and the darkness he called 'night'. There was evening and there was morning, the first
  day."
- Genesis 1:27 (WEB): "God created man in his own image. In God's image he created him; male and
  female he created them."
- Genesis 1:31 (WEB): "God saw everything that he had made, and, behold, it was very good. There
  was evening and there was morning, a sixth day."

### 2. The Fall (Genesis 3)
The serpent tempts the woman; she and the man eat the forbidden fruit; consequences follow,
including expulsion from the garden.

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- Genesis 3:6 (WEB): "When the woman saw that the tree was good for food, and that it was a
  delight to the eyes, and that the tree was to be desired to make one wise, she took some of its
  fruit, and ate."
- Genesis 3:15 (WEB): "I will put hostility between you and the woman, and between your offspring
  and her offspring. He will bruise your head, and you will bruise his heel."
- Genesis 3:23-24 (WEB): "Therefore Yahweh God sent him out from the garden of Eden, to till the
  ground from which he was taken. So he drove out the man; and he placed cherubim at the east of
  the garden of Eden, and a flaming sword which turned every way, to guard the way to the tree of
  life."

### 3. The Flood (Genesis 6-9)
Human wickedness grieves God; Noah finds favor and builds the ark; the flood comes; God covenants
never to flood the whole earth again, with the rainbow as sign.

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- Genesis 6:8 (WEB): "But Noah found favor in Yahweh's eyes." *(paired with 6:5-7 for context —
  display both, grade recall on 6:8 as the pivot line)*
- Genesis 7:17 (WEB, trimmed for challenge use): "The flood was forty days on the earth. The
  waters increased, and lifted up the ship, and it was lifted up above the earth."
- Genesis 9:12-13 (WEB): "God said, 'This is the token of the covenant which I make between me and
  you and every living creature that is with you, for perpetual generations: I set my rainbow in
  the cloud, and it will be a sign of a covenant between me and the earth.'"

### 4. Abraham's Call and the Binding of Isaac (Genesis 12, 15, 22)
God calls Abram out of his country with a promise of blessing; Abram believes and it's credited to
him as righteousness; later, God tests Abraham by asking for Isaac, then provides a ram in his
place.

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- Genesis 12:2-3 (WEB, trimmed): "I will make of you a great nation. I will bless you and make
  your name great... All the families of the earth will be blessed through you."
- Genesis 15:6 (WEB): "He believed in Yahweh, who credited it to him for righteousness."
- Genesis 22:2 (WEB): "Now take your son, your only son, Isaac, whom you love, and go into the
  land of Moriah. Offer him there as a burnt offering on one of the mountains which I will tell
  you of."
- Genesis 22:14 (WEB, trimmed): "Abraham called the name of that place 'Yahweh Will Provide.'"

### 5. Joseph Sold and Reconciled (Genesis 37, 45, 50)
Joseph's brothers, jealous of their father's favoritism, sell him into slavery in Egypt; years
later, now powerful in Egypt, Joseph reveals himself to them and reframes their betrayal as part
of God's providence.

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- Genesis 37:3-4 (WEB): "Now Israel loved Joseph more than all his children, because he was the
  son of his old age, and he made him a tunic of many colors. His brothers saw that their father
  loved him more than all his brothers, and they hated him, and couldn't speak peaceably to him."
- Genesis 45:4-5 (WEB, trimmed): "I am Joseph, your brother, whom you sold into Egypt. Now don't
  be grieved, nor angry with yourselves, that you sold me here, for God sent me before you to
  preserve life."
- Genesis 50:20 (WEB): "As for you, you meant evil against me, but God meant it for good, to save
  many people alive, as is happening today."

## Difficulty Ladder

| Level | Type | Example (from this book) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — Recall | Verse fill-in-blank | "In the beginning, ___ created the heavens and the earth." (Gen 1:1) |
| 2 — Recall | Multiple choice, narrative fact | "What did Noah's father-figure covenant sign become after the flood?" → rainbow (Gen 9:12-13) |
| 3 — Sequence | Order scenes within a story | Order the days of creation 1–6 by what was made each day; order the Joseph story beats (favored by father → sold by brothers → rises in Egypt → reveals himself) |
| 4 — Sequence + recall | Order + attribute a quotation | Given three quotations from the Abraham scenes (12:2-3, 15:6, 22:14), place them in the order they occur and match each to its reference |
| 5 — Application (narrative-fact only, not moral judgment) | Multiple choice on textual detail/consequence | "According to Genesis 3:23-24, what did God place at the entrance of Eden after sending the man out?" → cherubim and a flaming sword (tests attentive reading of the text, not interpretation of *why*) |

Boss battle (world 1 capstone): a mixed-format sequence spanning all 5 scenes, weighted toward
level 3–5 items, since by boss-battle time the player has seen every recall-level item already in
earlier challenges.

## Contested Territory

Per `docs/CONTENT_STYLE_GUIDE.md` §4 — flagged for the theological reviewer, not resolved here:

- **Genesis 1 creation days.** Young-earth (literal 24-hour days), old-earth, and
  literary-framework readings are all live positions among mainstream, orthodox Christians. No
  challenge item may assert or require a position on this — challenges stay strictly on "what the
  text narrates happened, in what order," never "how long it took" as a scientific claim.
- **The Flood's scope (global vs. regional).** Same handling — challenges test the narrative
  (Noah, the ark, the covenant sign) never the historicity/scope debate.
- **Genesis 22, the Binding of Isaac.** Touches sacrifice and testing theology directly. Challenge
  items here are scoped tightly to *what happened* (Abraham was tested, he prepared to offer
  Isaac, an angel stopped him, a ram was provided) — no challenge asks *why God tested Abraham* or
  invites a player to evaluate whether the test was justified. This scene is pivotal to the book
  and to the Abrahamic-covenant thread the following 12 worlds build on, so it isn't cut — it's
  bounded per the style guide's "stay narrative, don't grade the interpretation" rule.
- **Genesis 3, the Fall.** Fairly uncontested among mainstream Christian traditions as narrative,
  but Genesis 3:15 ("he will bruise your head...") is read by many traditions as a messianic
  prophecy (the "protoevangelium"), while critical scholarship (e.g. the Cambridge Bible) declines
  that reading. **Structural rule (added per theological review, `docs/reviews/genesis-review.md`):
  this verse may only be used in recall/fill-in-blank format — never multiple-choice "what does
  this verse mean/refer to" format** — so the boundary can't be crossed by a future
  challenge-authoring pass.
- **Genesis 1:27** ("male and female he created them"). Uncontested as narrative between
  mainstream traditions, but a verse often invoked in present-day debates unrelated to the book's
  content. **Same structural rule applies:** recall/fill-in-blank format only, never
  multiple-choice — added per theological review as a prudence measure, not because the current
  brief does anything wrong with it.
