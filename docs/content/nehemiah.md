## Setting / Era

Nehemiah picks up roughly a decade after Ezra's return (on the traditional chronology; see Ezra's
brief for the chronology caveat), still under Persian rule. Nehemiah, a Jewish cupbearer to King
Artaxerxes in the Persian capital of Susa, hears that Jerusalem's walls remain broken down and its
gates burned — over a century after the first return under Cyrus. He obtains the king's permission
and resources to travel to Jerusalem and rebuild the wall, which he accomplishes in fifty-two days
despite organized opposition. The book's centerpiece is a public reading of the Law by Ezra at the
Water Gate (Nehemiah 8), followed by national confession and covenant renewal, and closes with
Nehemiah's later reforms correcting practices that had drifted during his absence. This is World 16
in the post-exilic History-group expansion (`docs/CANON_STRUCTURE.md` §6), following directly after
World 15 (Ezra).

## Pivotal Scenes (5)

### 1. Nehemiah's Prayer and Request to the King (Nehemiah 1-2)
Nehemiah, hearing from his brother Hanani that Jerusalem's wall is broken down and its gates burned,
weeps, fasts, and prays for days. Months later, the king notices his sadness; Nehemiah, after a
silent prayer, asks permission to go rebuild the city of his fathers' tombs. The king grants it,
along with letters of safe passage and timber.

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- Nehemiah 1:3 (WEB): "The remnant who are left of the captivity there in the province are in great
  affliction and reproach. The wall of Jerusalem is also broken down, and its gates are burned with
  fire."
- Nehemiah 1:4 (WEB, trimmed): "When I heard these words, I sat down and wept, and mourned several
  days; and I fasted and prayed before the God of heaven."
- Nehemiah 2:4-5 (WEB, trimmed): "The king said to me, 'What is your request?' So I prayed to the
  God of heaven. I said to the king, '...I ask that you would send me to Judah, to the city of my
  fathers' tombs, that I may build it.'"

*Source: [ebible.org WEB, Nehemiah 1](https://ebible.org/web/NEH01.htm) and
[ebible.org WEB, Nehemiah 2](https://ebible.org/web/NEH02.htm), cross-checked against
[biblegateway.com WEB, Nehemiah 1-2](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Nehemiah+1%2C+Nehemiah+2%2C+Nehemiah+8&version=WEB) — exact match, two independent sources.*

### 2. Rebuilding the Wall Amid Opposition (Nehemiah 2-6)
Nehemiah surveys the ruined wall by night, then rallies the people to rebuild. Sanballat and Tobiah
mock and threaten the effort; the builders work with a tool in one hand and a weapon in the other.
Despite continued opposition and plots against Nehemiah personally, the wall is completed in
fifty-two days, and even Israel's enemies recognize the work was done "by our God."

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- Nehemiah 2:17-18 (WEB, trimmed): "'Come, let's build up the wall of Jerusalem, that we won't be
  disgraced.' ...They said, 'Let's rise up and build.' So they strengthened their hands for the
  good work."
- Nehemiah 4:17 (WEB, trimmed): "Those who built the wall, and those who bore burdens loaded
  themselves; everyone with one of his hands did the work, and with the other held his weapon."
- Nehemiah 6:15-16 (WEB): "So the wall was finished in the twenty-fifth day of Elul, in fifty-two
  days. When all our enemies heard of it, all the nations that were around us were afraid, and they
  lost their confidence; for they perceived that this work was done by our God."

*Source: [ebible.org WEB, Nehemiah 2](https://ebible.org/web/NEH02.htm),
[ebible.org WEB, Nehemiah 4](https://ebible.org/web/NEH04.htm),
[ebible.org WEB, Nehemiah 6](https://ebible.org/web/NEH06.htm). 2:17-18 cross-checked against
biblegateway.com (see Scene 1 link) — exact match; 4:17 and 6:15-16 not yet independently
cross-checked against a second source — flag for `docs/reviews/nehemiah-review.md` §1 and QA §3.*

### 3. Ezra Reads the Law at the Water Gate (Nehemiah 8)
With the wall finished, the people gather as one and ask Ezra to bring out the Book of the Law. He
reads it aloud from early morning until midday; the people weep on hearing it, but Nehemiah and Ezra
tell them not to mourn — the day is holy, and they should celebrate instead, "for the joy of Yahweh
is your strength." The people then keep the Feast of Booths, celebrated (the text notes) more fully
than at any time since Joshua's day.

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- Nehemiah 8:3 (WEB, trimmed): "He read from it before the wide place that was in front of the water
  gate from early morning until midday, in the presence of the men and the women, and of those who
  could understand."
- Nehemiah 8:9-10 (WEB, trimmed): "'Today is holy to Yahweh your God. Don't mourn, nor weep.' ...
  'Don't be grieved, for the joy of Yahweh is your strength.'"
- Nehemiah 8:17 (WEB, trimmed): "All the assembly of those who had come back out of the captivity
  made temporary shelters and lived in the temporary shelters, for since the days of Joshua the son
  of Nun to that day the children of Israel had not done so."

*Source: [ebible.org WEB, Nehemiah 8](https://ebible.org/web/NEH08.htm), cross-checked against
[biblegateway.com WEB, Nehemiah 8](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Nehemiah+1%2C+Nehemiah+2%2C+Nehemiah+8&version=WEB) — exact match, two independent sources, for 8:9-10.*

### 4. National Confession and Covenant Renewal (Nehemiah 9-10)
Shortly after the Feast of Booths, the people assemble again, fasting and confessing their own sins
and their ancestors' — the Levites lead a long prayer reviewing Yahweh's faithfulness across
Israel's history. The chapter closes with the people making a binding, written, sealed covenant to
follow the Law.

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- Nehemiah 9:1-2 (WEB, trimmed): "Now in the twenty-fourth day of this month the children of Israel
  were assembled with fasting, with sackcloth, and dirt on them. ...and stood and confessed their
  sins and the iniquities of their fathers."
- Nehemiah 9:3 (WEB, trimmed): "They stood up in their place, and read in the book of the law of
  Yahweh their God a fourth part of the day; and a fourth part they confessed and worshiped Yahweh
  their God."
- Nehemiah 9:38 (WEB): "Yet for all this, we make a sure covenant, and write it; and our princes,
  our Levites, and our priests, seal it."

*Source: [ebible.org WEB, Nehemiah 9](https://ebible.org/web/NEH09.htm). Not yet independently
cross-checked against a second source — flag for `docs/reviews/nehemiah-review.md` §1 and QA §3.
The long prayer's historical content (9:4-37, reviewing the Exodus, wilderness, and conquest) is
summarized narratively above rather than quoted, and is not itself selected as challenge-ready
text — see Contested Territory below.*

### 5. Nehemiah's Reforms (Nehemiah 13) — see Contested Territory for bounding
After a return trip to Persia and back, Nehemiah finds earlier reforms had lapsed: people were
trading on the Sabbath, and some had again intermarried with surrounding peoples, to the point that
some children could no longer speak the language of Judah. Nehemiah restores Sabbath observance by
shutting the city gates before the Sabbath begins, and confronts the recurrence of intermarriage.

**Challenge-ready verses** (Sabbath reform only — see note below on what is excluded from this
scene):
- Nehemiah 13:15 (WEB, trimmed): "In those days I saw some men treading wine presses on the Sabbath
  in Judah, bringing in sheaves, and loading donkeys with wine, grapes, figs, and all kinds of
  burdens which they brought into Jerusalem on the Sabbath day..."
- Nehemiah 13:19 (WEB, trimmed): "It came to pass that when the gates of Jerusalem began to be dark
  before the Sabbath, I commanded that the doors should be shut, and commanded that they should not
  be opened until after the Sabbath..."
- Nehemiah 13:22 (WEB, trimmed): "I commanded the Levites that they should purify themselves, and
  that they should come and keep the gates, to sanctify the Sabbath day..."

**Deliberately excluded from challenge-ready content:** Nehemiah 13:23-27, describing the recurrence
of intermarriage, is **not** selected as challenge-ready verse text, and its most severe clause
(13:25: Nehemiah "contended with them, cursed them, struck certain of them, plucked off their hair")
is not narrated in the scene description above either. This follows the same pattern as
`docs/content/2-samuel.md`'s exclusion of Uzzah's death (2 Sam 6:6-7) from its ark scene: the
underlying event is real narrative content in the book, but it depicts the protagonist committing
physical violence in a way the text itself does not narrate as wrong, which is not appropriate as
graded or recalled content in a general-audience/child-directed game regardless of the theological
question. Unlike Ezra 9-10 (see `docs/content/ezra.md` Scene 5), this brief does not treat the
underlying reform *itself* as escalation-worthy, since no verse describing it is selected as
challenge-ready and the scene as authored does not require a player to process or affirm it in any
form — see `docs/reviews/nehemiah-review.md` for the reviewer's reasoning on why exclusion (rather
than escalation) is judged sufficient here.

## Contested Territory

Per `docs/CONTENT_STYLE_GUIDE.md` §4 — flagged for review, not resolved here:

- **Nehemiah 13:23-27, the recurrence of intermarriage and Nehemiah's violent response.** See Scene
  5 above. Genuinely difficult content (physical violence by the book's protagonist, narrated
  without in-text criticism) but handled by full exclusion from challenge-ready content and from the
  scene description itself, following the `docs/content/2-samuel.md` Uzzah precedent rather than the
  escalation path used for `docs/content/ezra.md` Scene 5. The two brief authors (this session, for
  both books) judge these as meaningfully different in degree: Ezra 9-10 is a full central scene
  built around the expulsion itself with named participants; Nehemiah 13:23-27 is a brief coda that
  this brief excludes entirely rather than bounds. If a future reviewer disagrees with that
  distinction, the correct fix is to escalate this scene too — see `docs/reviews/nehemiah-review.md`
  for the explicit reasoning so a later reader can evaluate whether that call was right, rather than
  it being assumed silently.
- **Nehemiah 9's historical review prayer (9:4-37).** The Levites' prayer recounts the Exodus,
  wilderness wandering, and conquest of Canaan — the same events covered by earlier worlds' brief,
  including Joshua's ḥerem/conquest material (`docs/content/joshua.md`). This brief does not select
  any verse from 9:4-37 as challenge-ready content precisely to avoid re-surfacing that
  already-bounded contested ground inside a different book's brief; Scene 4's challenge-ready verses
  are limited to the fasting/confession framing (9:1-3) and the covenant-sealing statement (9:38)
  only.
- **Chronology of Ezra and Nehemiah.** See `docs/content/ezra.md`'s Contested Territory section —
  same handling applies here: no challenge item asserts a specific cross-book chronological
  reconstruction; sequencing challenges use each book's own internal narrative order only.
- **Sanballat/Tobiah opposition and regional-identity questions (Nehemiah 2, 4, 6).** Not selected
  as challenge-ready content beyond the general fact that opposition occurred and was overcome (see
  Scene 2) — no verse naming or characterizing the opposing figures' ethnic/religious identity in
  more specific terms is selected, avoiding any need to adjudicate later traditions' identification
  of these figures.
