## Design intent for this tier

This tier draws on verses that are narratively significant but less likely to be immediately
recognized — exact numbers, conditional promises, secondary dialogue beats — and requires typed
recall (no multiple-choice scaffolding) wherever the format allows. It stays inside the same
guardrails as every other tier: verbatim WEB text only, narrative fact only, no doctrinal position
required.

## Contested Territory

Everything already flagged in `docs/content/1-kings.md` applies unchanged: the 12:15 causation
question (not engaged — 12:15 is not selected in this tier), the 1 Kings 11 gap note, the exclusion
of 18:40, the 3:16 "prostitutes" detail (not selected; "two women" framing kept), and the 8:27
transcendence question (item 8 here reuses that exact already-bounded verse, recall-only, same
bounding).

**New for this tier — reviewed against live cross-tradition sources in
`docs/reviews/1-kings-hard-review.md` §5, bounding confirmed to hold in every case:**

- **1 Kings 3:14 (item 3)**, "If you will walk in my ways... then I will lengthen your days." This
  is a conditional divine promise linking obedience to lifespan — adjacent to broader
  retribution-theology questions (does righteousness reliably produce a long life? Job and
  Ecclesiastes are frequently read in tension with passages like this one). Checked against Rashi
  (Sefaria) and classic Christian commentators (Bible Hub aggregation): both traditions independently
  converge on reading this verse as a *specific, personal, conditional promise to Solomon* — riches
  and honor unconditional, long life conditional on Solomon personally — not as a general statement
  of retribution theology; "lengthen" is the plain rendering of Hebrew H748 with no tradition-specific
  loading. **Structural rule: this item is recall-only, never multiple-choice; it grades only the
  word "lengthen," never a claim about whether the principle holds generally or in every case; and no
  challenge in any tier may ask whether Solomon received the promised long life, or why he did not —
  both answers require inference from 1 Kings 11, a deliberately unincluded gap. "Walk in my ways"/
  "statutes"/"commandments" are display-only, never eligible blanks.**
- **1 Kings 6:1 (item 7)**, the "four hundred and eightieth year" chronology. This exact verse is
  central to a real scholarly dispute over the date of the Exodus (an "early date," c. 1446 BC,
  built substantially on this verse's arithmetic from Solomon's fourth year, vs. a "late date," c.
  1250 BC, argued from archaeological and other textual evidence) — a historicity/dating debate the
  style guide places out-of-bounds for graded interpretation. Review found the risk is broader than
  the dating dispute alone: **the number itself sits on a live manuscript variant** (most Septuagint
  manuscripts read 440, not 480), and mainstream Christian commentators disagree among themselves
  about whether the phrase is even original (Pulpit and Barnes read it as a possible interpolation;
  Keil & Delitzsch defend it as original). Jewish tradition (Revised JPS, Radak) reads 480 and builds
  no dating argument on it at all. **Structural rule: the number may only be graded with the
  reference cited and the verbatim WEB clause displayed — never as a free-standing question (e.g.
  "how many years passed between the Exodus and the temple?"), since that would leave a genuine
  three-way answer-key ambiguity (480 MT/WEB, 440 LXX, "not a literal figure"). The item's stem and
  answer never ask "when was the Exodus" or "is the number original" — only what the displayed WEB
  clause of 1 Kings 6:1 states.**
- **1 Kings 18:37 (item 16)**, "turned their heart back again." Not flagged in the original draft;
  added per theological review. WEB's "back again" (God restoring the people to himself) is one of
  two live readings — Revised JPS renders the same Hebrew "backward" and flags the clause's meaning
  as uncertain, and classic commentators record (while rejecting) an opposite reading in which God
  hardens hearts toward error. **Structural rule: the "turned their heart back again" clause is
  display-only — never the graded token, never an MC subject, and no item may ask what Elijah said
  God had done to the people's hearts.** The graded token remains "God," in "that you, Yahweh, are
  ___," which every tradition renders identically.
- **1 Kings 3:24-28 dialogue (items 4-6)**: plain narrative dialogue, no known cross-tradition
  dispute; the "sword" and "yearned" details carry no theological weight beyond the scene bounding
  already reviewed at `medium`.
- **1 Kings 12:8, 12:19 (items 10-11)**: narrated political detail (whose counsel Rehoboam took; that
  Israel's rebellion persisted) — distinct from and does not touch the 12:15 causation claim, which
  remains unselected in this tier.
- **1 Kings 18:26 (item 12)**: narrative content preceding the excluded 18:40; no new contested
  ground — stays inside the existing Carmel-scene bounding (what happened/was said, not why the
  contest was framed as it was).
- **The divine name "Yahweh"** (items 7, 16) is inherited, already-settled ground (Open Decision #1,
  medium brief approved), recorded here per review §6.10 for the trail only.
