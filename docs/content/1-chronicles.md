## A note on this brief's scope and thinness

**This brief is intentionally thinner than the other MVP books' briefs, and that is by design, not
an oversight.** Two-thirds of 1 Chronicles (chapters 1–9) is genealogical material — name lists
tracing lineage from Adam through the twelve tribes. Per the differentiation guidance, this content
is kept to a single light-touch scene with minimal, low-density challenge material; it is
deliberately not built out into full recall/sequence challenges the way a narrative scene would be,
because name lists are poor game material and forcing them into graded challenges would produce
low-quality, rote-memorization content the style guide's "narrative fact" spirit doesn't really
fit. The remaining narrative material this brief draws on (chapters 21-29, David's late reign) is
also narrower in scope than a full "reign" brief would be, since the bulk of David's reign is
Samuel's material. Four scenes total, one of them minimal — this is the expected shape for this
book, not a gap to fill later.

## Setting / Era

1 Chronicles retells Israel's history from a priestly, post-exilic perspective (traditionally
understood as written for a community returned from Babylonian exile, looking back at what had been
lost and re-establishing continuity with it). It opens with nine chapters of genealogies from Adam
down to the tribes of Israel, then picks up the narrative abruptly at Saul's death, moving quickly
to David's kingship. Distinct from Samuel's more personal narrative, Chronicles omits David's
adultery with Bathsheba and most other material that doesn't bear on the temple's establishment,
and instead spends its final chapters on David's preparations for a temple he is told he will not
be permitted to build himself — preparations his son Solomon will complete in the following book,
2 Chronicles (World 14).

## Pivotal Scenes (4)

### 1. The Genealogies (1 Chronicles 1-9) — light touch, minimal challenge density
Nine chapters trace lineage from Adam through Noah's sons, the patriarchs, and the twelve tribes of
Israel, down to residents of Jerusalem after the return from exile. Per the differentiation
guidance and the scope note above, this scene is represented with a single identification-level
detail rather than built out into sequence or application-level challenges.

**Challenge-ready verses (minimal, recall-only):**
- 1 Chronicles 1:1 (WEB): "Adam, Seth, Enosh,"

This is the *only* challenge-ready verse drawn from chapters 1-9. No sequence, application, or
multiple-choice challenge should be built from the genealogical lists — see Difficulty Ladder note
below.

### 2. David's Census and the Altar at Ornan's Threshing Floor (1 Chronicles 21)
David takes a census of Israel's fighting men — an act the text says he was incited to by Satan,
and for which a plague follows as consequence. When David intercedes, the prophet Gad directs him to
build an altar on the threshing floor of Ornan the Jebusite. David does, offers sacrifices, and fire
falls from heaven onto the altar. This site becomes the location of Solomon's temple (established
explicitly in 2 Chronicles 3:1, this brief's companion book).

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- 1 Chronicles 21:1 (WEB): "Satan stood up against Israel, and moved David to take a census of
  Israel."
- 1 Chronicles 21:18 (WEB, trimmed): "Yahweh's angel commanded Gad to tell David that David should
  go up and raise an altar to Yahweh on the threshing floor of Ornan the Jebusite."
- 1 Chronicles 21:26 (WEB): "David built an altar to Yahweh there, and offered burnt offerings and
  peace offerings, and called on Yahweh; and he answered him from the sky by fire on the altar of
  burnt offering."

### 3. David's Preparations for the Temple (1 Chronicles 22)
David tells Solomon that although he himself wanted to build a house for Yahweh's name, he was told
he could not, because he was a man of war who had shed much blood. Instead, David gathers massive
quantities of gold, silver, bronze, iron, timber, and stone for Solomon to use, and charges his son
to build the temple in his place.

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- 1 Chronicles 22:7-8 (WEB): "As for me, it was in my heart to build a house to the name of Yahweh
  my God. But Yahweh's word came to me, saying, 'You have shed blood abundantly and have made great
  wars. You shall not build a house to my name, because you have shed much blood on the earth in my
  sight.'"
- 1 Chronicles 22:5 (WEB, trimmed): "Solomon my son is young and tender, and the house that is to be
  built for Yahweh must be exceedingly magnificent... I will therefore make preparation for it."
- 1 Chronicles 22:14 (WEB, trimmed): "In my affliction I have prepared for Yahweh's house one
  hundred thousand talents of gold, one million talents of silver, and bronze and iron without
  weight... I have also prepared timber and stone."

### 4. David's Charge to Solomon and the People's Offering (1 Chronicles 28-29)
David publicly charges Solomon before the assembly of Israel to build the temple and to serve
Yahweh wholeheartedly. The people, following David's own gift of personal treasure, give
generously toward the temple's construction; David closes with a prayer recognizing that everything
given ultimately came from God in the first place.

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- 1 Chronicles 28:9-10 (WEB, trimmed): "You, Solomon my son, know the God of your father, and serve
  him with a perfect heart and with a willing mind... Take heed now, for Yahweh has chosen you to
  build a house for the sanctuary. Be strong, and do it."
- 1 Chronicles 28:20 (WEB, trimmed): "Be strong and courageous, and do it. Don't be afraid, nor be
  dismayed, for Yahweh God, even my God, is with you."
- 1 Chronicles 29:14 (WEB): "But who am I, and what is my people, that we should be able to offer so
  willingly as this? For all things come from you, and we have given you of your own."

## Contested Territory

Per `docs/CONTENT_STYLE_GUIDE.md` §4 — flagged for the theological reviewer, not resolved here:

- **1 Chronicles 21:1 vs. 2 Samuel 24:1 — who incited David's census.** This is the single most
  theologically interesting divergence in this brief. 1 Chronicles 21:1 says "Satan stood up against
  Israel, and moved David"; the parallel account in 2 Samuel 24:1 (World 10's material, not this
  book's) says "Yahweh's anger burned against Israel again, and he incited David against them." Both
  are genuine WEB text, not a paraphrase error — this is the two books' own differing tellings of
  the same event. **This brief's challenge items test only what 1 Chronicles itself says (21:1's own
  wording, attributed only to this book)** — no challenge item asks a player to reconcile the two
  accounts, decide which is "correct," or explain the theological relationship between Yahweh's
  sovereignty and Satan's agency. That reconciliation question is a live, real cross-tradition topic
  (see review for sourcing) and stays entirely out of graded content.
- **1 Chronicles 22:8 / 28:3, David barred from building the temple as a "man of war... [who] shed
  blood."** The text states its own reason for the restriction; this brief's challenge items recall
  that stated reason without asking a player to evaluate whether the restriction was just or explain
  its underlying ritual/moral logic (which would cross into interpretation the style guide places
  out of bounds).
- **The genealogies (chapters 1-9), scope decision.** Beyond the low-challenge-density guidance
  already noted, genealogical lists in the ancient Near Eastern sense sometimes compress or skip
  generations for structural/theological reasons (a point some source-critical scholarship raises).
  This brief does not surface any claim about the genealogies' historical completeness or accuracy
  one way or the other — the single challenge-ready verse (1:1) is a bare name sequence with no
  claim attached beyond what it says.
