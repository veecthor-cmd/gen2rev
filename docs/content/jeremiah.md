## Setting / Era

Jeremiah prophesied in Judah's final decades before the Babylonian exile, from the thirteenth year
of King Josiah's reign (Jeremiah 1:2, roughly a generation before Jerusalem's fall) through the
reigns of Jehoiakim and Zedekiah, to the city's destruction in 587/586 BC and shortly after. Where
2 Kings (World 12) told this same era's ending from the palace's vantage point, Jeremiah narrates it
from inside a single prophet's decades-long, largely unwelcome ministry — called as a young man,
resisted by kings and priests, imprisoned more than once, and ultimately an eyewitness to the very
catastrophe he had spent his life warning about.

## Pivotal Scenes (7)

### 1. The Call of Jeremiah (Jeremiah 1:4-10, 1:17-19)
Yahweh's word comes to Jeremiah, telling him he was known and appointed before he was even formed. 
Jeremiah objects that he is too young; Yahweh answers that Jeremiah must go and speak whatever he is
commanded, promises to be with him, and gives him a mission "to uproot and to tear down, to destroy
and to overthrow, to build and to plant."

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- Jeremiah 1:5 (WEB): "Before I formed you in the womb, I knew you. Before you were born, I
  sanctified you. I have appointed you a prophet to the nations." *(Handled per the format
  restriction below — see Contested Territory: Jeremiah 1:5.)*
- Jeremiah 1:6-7 (WEB, trimmed): "Then I said, 'Ah, Lord Yahweh! Behold, I don't know how to speak;
  for I am a child.' But Yahweh said to me, 'Don't say, "I am a child;" for you must go to whomever
  I send you, and you must say whatever I command you.'"
- Jeremiah 1:9-10 (WEB): "Then Yahweh stretched out his hand and touched my mouth. Then Yahweh said
  to me, 'Behold, I have put my words in your mouth. Behold, I have today set you over the nations
  and over the kingdoms, to uproot and to tear down, to destroy and to overthrow, to build and to
  plant.'"
- Jeremiah 1:19 (WEB): "'They will fight against you, but they will not prevail against you; for I
  am with you,' says Yahweh, 'to rescue you.'"

### 2. The Temple Sermon (Jeremiah 7:1-15)
Jeremiah stands at the gate of Yahweh's house and confronts the people for trusting in the temple's
mere presence ("Yahweh's temple, Yahweh's temple, Yahweh's temple, are these") while living unjustly
and worshiping other gods. He warns that the temple offers no automatic protection, pointing to
Shiloh — Israel's earlier worship site, which Yahweh had already allowed to be destroyed — as
precedent. *(This brief stays inside Jeremiah's own text and historical context; it does not
reference the sermon's later quotation in the New Testament Gospels, per this book's own scope.)*

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- Jeremiah 7:3-4 (WEB, trimmed): "'Amend your ways and your doings, and I will cause you to dwell in
  this place. Don't trust in lying words, saying, "Yahweh's temple, Yahweh's temple, Yahweh's
  temple, are these."'"
- Jeremiah 7:9-11 (WEB, trimmed): "'Will you steal, murder, commit adultery, swear falsely, burn
  incense to Baal, and walk after other gods that you have not known, then come and stand before me
  in this house, which is called by my name... Has this house, which is called by my name, become a
  den of robbers in your eyes? Behold, I myself have seen it,' says Yahweh."
- Jeremiah 7:12 (WEB): "'But go now to my place which was in Shiloh, where I caused my name to dwell
  at the first, and see what I did to it for the wickedness of my people Israel.'"

### 3. The Potter's House (Jeremiah 18:1-6)
Yahweh sends Jeremiah down to a potter's house, where he watches a potter reshape a marred vessel
into a new one, as seemed good to him. Yahweh applies the image directly: the house of Israel is in
his hand as the clay is in the potter's hand.

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- Jeremiah 18:2-4 (WEB, trimmed): "'Arise, and go down to the potter's house, and there I will cause
  you to hear my words.' Then I went down to the potter's house, and behold, he was making something
  on the wheels. When the vessel that he made of the clay was marred in the hand of the potter, he
  made it again another vessel, as seemed good to the potter to make it."
- Jeremiah 18:6 (WEB): "'House of Israel, can't I do with you as this potter?' says Yahweh. 'Behold,
  as the clay in the potter's hand, so are you in my hand, house of Israel.'"

### 4. Jeremiah and Hananiah — True and False Prophecy (Jeremiah 28)
The prophet Hananiah publicly declares, in Yahweh's house, that within two years Yahweh will break
the king of Babylon's yoke and bring back the temple vessels and the exiled king Jeconiah. Jeremiah
initially says "Amen! May Yahweh do so" but then states the text's own criterion for testing a
prophet who proclaims peace: such a word is confirmed true only when it comes to pass. Hananiah
breaks the wooden yoke-bar off Jeremiah's neck as a dramatic sign; Jeremiah, sent back with a further
word, tells Hananiah that Yahweh has not sent him and that he will die within the year — which the
text reports happened.

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- Jeremiah 28:2-4 (WEB, trimmed): "'Yahweh of Armies, the God of Israel, says, "I have broken the
  yoke of the king of Babylon. Within two full years I will bring again into this place all the
  vessels of Yahweh's house... I will bring again to this place Jeconiah the son of Jehoiakim, king
  of Judah, with all the captives of Judah, who went to Babylon," says Yahweh; "for I will break the
  yoke of the king of Babylon."'"
- Jeremiah 28:9 (WEB): "'As for the prophet who prophesies of peace, when the word of the prophet
  happens, then the prophet will be known, that Yahweh has truly sent him.'" *(This is the text's
  own stated, in-text criterion for testing a prophet — see Contested Territory.)*
- Jeremiah 28:10-11 (WEB, trimmed): "Then Hananiah the prophet took the bar from off the prophet
  Jeremiah's neck, and broke it. Hananiah spoke in the presence of all the people, saying, 'Yahweh
  says: "Even so I will break the yoke of Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon from off the neck of all
  the nations within two full years."'"
- Jeremiah 28:15-17 (WEB, trimmed): "Then the prophet Jeremiah said to Hananiah the prophet, 'Listen,
  Hananiah! Yahweh has not sent you, but you make this people trust in a lie. Therefore Yahweh says,
  "Behold, I will send you away from off the surface of the earth. This year you will die..."' So
  Hananiah the prophet died the same year in the seventh month."

### 5. Jeremiah Imprisoned and Rescued (Jeremiah 37-38)
As the Babylonian siege continues, Jeremiah is arrested on suspicion of defecting to the Chaldeans
and imprisoned. Later, officials — angered that Jeremiah keeps telling the people that surrender
means survival — have King Zedekiah's permission to put him in a cistern with no water, only mud,
where he is left with no food. Ebedmelech, a Cushite official in the king's house, appeals directly
to the king that Jeremiah is "likely to die in the place where he is." The king authorizes a rescue;
Ebedmelech and thirty men pull Jeremiah out with rags cushioning the ropes under his arms. *(Per
this assignment's content notes, the danger itself — the cistern, the mud, being left to die — is
handled at the narrative-summary level above rather than built into challenge-ready verbatim recall
content; see Contested Territory.)*

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- Jeremiah 38:9 (WEB): "'My lord the king, these men have done evil in all that they have done to
  Jeremiah the prophet, whom they have cast into the dungeon. He is likely to die in the place where
  he is, because of the famine; for there is no more bread in the city.'"
- Jeremiah 38:10 (WEB, trimmed): "Then the king commanded Ebedmelech the Ethiopian, saying, 'Take
  from here thirty men with you, and take up Jeremiah the prophet out of the dungeon, before he
  dies.'"
- Jeremiah 38:13 (WEB): "So they lifted Jeremiah up with the cords, and took him up out of the
  dungeon; and Jeremiah remained in the court of the guard."

### 6. The Fall of Jerusalem, as Jeremiah Witnessed It (Jeremiah 39)
Nebuchadnezzar's army finally breaches Jerusalem's wall after a long siege. Zedekiah attempts to
flee but is captured. *(This brief does not use Jeremiah 39:6-7 — Zedekiah's sons killed before him
and his own eyes put out — as challenge-ready content; the same graphic material is already excluded
from 2 Kings' brief at its parallel passage, 2 Kings 25:6-7. See Contested Territory.)* Distinct from
2 Kings' account, this book then reports Nebuchadnezzar's own personal order that Jeremiah be
protected and treated well, and Yahweh's promise of deliverance to Ebedmelech, the official who had
rescued Jeremiah from the cistern — Jeremiah's own place inside the catastrophe he had long
foretold.

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- Jeremiah 39:1-2 (WEB): "In the ninth year of Zedekiah king of Judah, in the tenth month,
  Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon and all his army came against Jerusalem, and besieged it. In the
  eleventh year of Zedekiah, in the fourth month, the ninth day of the month, a breach was made in
  the city."
- Jeremiah 39:11-12 (WEB, trimmed): "Now Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon commanded Nebuzaradan the
  captain of the guard concerning Jeremiah, saying, 'Take him and take care of him. Do him no harm;
  but do to him even as he tells you.'"
- Jeremiah 39:17-18 (WEB, trimmed): "'But I will deliver you in that day,' says Yahweh; 'and you
  will not be given into the hand of the men of whom you are afraid. For I will surely save you. You
  won't fall by the sword, but you will escape with your life, because you have put your trust in
  me,' says Yahweh."

### 7. The New Covenant Promise (Jeremiah 31:31-34)
In the middle of a chapter of restoration promises to "the house of Israel and the house of Judah,"
Jeremiah records Yahweh's promise of a coming "new covenant," distinct from the covenant made at the
Exodus, in which the law would be written on the heart rather than only on stone or scroll, and sin
forgiven and no longer remembered. *(Per this assignment's instructions, this brief bounds the
challenge content to reciting the verse's own words — who it addresses in the text itself ("the
house of Israel, and... the house of Judah") and what it states — and makes no claim about later
fulfillment or who the covenant applies to today. See Contested Territory.)*

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- Jeremiah 31:31 (WEB): "'Behold, the days come,' says Yahweh, 'that I will make a new covenant with
  the house of Israel, and with the house of Judah.'"
- Jeremiah 31:33 (WEB): "'But this is the covenant that I will make with the house of Israel after
  those days,' says Yahweh: 'I will put my law in their inward parts, and I will write it in their
  heart. I will be their God, and they shall be my people.'"
- Jeremiah 31:34 (WEB, trimmed): "'They will no longer each teach his neighbor... for they will all
  know me, from their least to their greatest,' says Yahweh, 'for I will forgive their iniquity, and
  I will remember their sin no more.'" *(Handled per the format restriction below — see Contested
  Territory: the New Covenant.)*

## Contested Territory

Per `docs/CONTENT_STYLE_GUIDE.md` §4 — flagged for the theological reviewer, not resolved here.

- **Jeremiah 1:5, "Before I formed you in the womb, I knew you."** Not contested as narrative (this
  is Yahweh describing his relationship to Jeremiah specifically, in a call narrative), but this
  exact verse is frequently invoked in present-day debates about personhood and life issues that are
  entirely outside this book's own content and this brief's scope. Consistent with the same prudence
  restriction already applied to Genesis 1:27 and Job 1:21: **structural rule — recall/fill-in-blank
  format only, never a multiple-choice "what does this verse teach about..." format.**
- **Jeremiah 7, the Temple Sermon — no New Testament cross-reference.** This sermon's "den of
  robbers" line is quoted by Jesus in the Gospels (Matthew 21:13, Mark 11:17, Luke 19:46) when he
  overturns the tables in the temple. Per this assignment's explicit instruction, this brief stays
  entirely inside Jeremiah's own text and eighth-century-BC context — no challenge item, scene
  description, or flavor text references the later New Testament quotation or draws any typological
  connection to it.
- **Jeremiah 28, testing true and false prophecy.** The in-text criterion Jeremiah states (28:9 —
  fulfillment confirms a peace-prophecy's source) is itself drawn from the same principle stated
  directly in Deuteronomy 18:21-22, so it is squarely narrative fact/direct verse text, not this
  brief's interpretive gloss. However, whether prophecy in this sense continues today (a live
  continuationist/cessationist debate within modern Christianity) is a real, separate question this
  brief does not touch. **No challenge item asks whether prophecy continues today or how to identify
  a false prophet in the present** — every item stays on what happened between Jeremiah and Hananiah
  and what the text itself states as the test applied to Hananiah's specific claim.
- **Jeremiah 37-38, the cistern imprisonment.** Per this assignment's content notes, this is
  safe narrative with no doctrinal issue but is graphic-adjacent (a person left to die, then
  rescued) — handled the way this project has handled comparable real-danger material elsewhere
  (e.g. Job's exclusion of the children's-death report, Job 1:18-19, rather than building a graded
  item on the raw danger itself). **This brief's bounding:** the danger is narrated in scene
  description only; no challenge-ready verse describes the mud or the threat of death directly — the
  challenge-ready verses instead center Ebedmelech's appeal and the rescue itself (38:9-10, 38:13),
  so graded content lands on the rescue, not the peril.
- **Jeremiah 39:6-7, Zedekiah's sons killed before him and his own eyes put out.** Not selected as
  challenge-ready content anywhere in this brief, and not part of the scene description above,
  matching the identical exclusion already made in 2 Kings' brief (`docs/content/2-kings.md`) at
  2 Kings 25:6-7, the parallel account of the same event. Flagged explicitly per that document's own
  practice, so the exclusion reads as a considered, cross-book-consistent decision rather than a gap.
- **Jeremiah 31:31-34, the New Covenant.** This is the single largest contested-territory item in
  this brief. Christian tradition, following its use in Luke 22:20, 1 Corinthians 11:25, and
  especially Hebrews 8:8-12 (which quotes this passage in full), reads this promise as fulfilled in
  Christ and the church — with real, live disagreement *within* Christian tradition over whether the
  church has replaced, been grafted into, or runs alongside ethnic/national Israel and Judah as this
  covenant's recipient (supersessionist, covenantal, and dispensationalist readings all differ here).
  Jewish tradition reads the passage on its own terms, addressed in the text itself to "the house of
  Israel, and... the house of Judah," as a promise of future national/covenantal renewal, without
  reference to Christian fulfillment claims. **Per this assignment's explicit instruction, this
  brief's bounding is: challenge items recite the verse's own words only** — including its own
  stated addressees ("the house of Israel... the house of Judah") exactly as the text names them —
  **and make no claim about later fulfillment or about who the covenant applies to today.**
  **Structural rule: Jeremiah 31:31-34 may only be used in recall/fill-in-blank format (reciting the
  verbatim WEB text) — never a multiple-choice "who is this covenant for," "has this been fulfilled,"
  or "what does this covenant mean" format.**
