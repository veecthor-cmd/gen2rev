## Setting / Era

Ruth opens "in the days when the judges judged" (Ruth 1:1), placing it chronologically alongside
World 7 (Judges), though its tone and scale are entirely different — a short, complete, domestic
story about loyalty, provision, and redemption rather than national cycles of apostasy and
deliverance. A famine sends a Bethlehem family to Moab; a Moabite woman, Ruth, ends up committed to
her Israelite mother-in-law Naomi and, through the ancient kinsman-redeemer custom, marries Boaz.
The book closes with a short genealogy that identifies their descendants: Obed, Jesse, and David —
directly setting up the Samuel worlds (9-10) where David becomes king. Of the three books in this
assignment, this is the lowest-sensitivity: no ḥerem language, no excluded dark chapters, and only
one real cross-tradition item to flag (below).

## Pivotal Scenes (5)

### 1. Loyalty — Ruth's Declaration (Ruth 1)
Naomi, widowed and having lost both sons (one married to Ruth, one to Orpah), urges her two
daughters-in-law to return to their own people. Orpah does; Ruth refuses, declaring her loyalty to
Naomi and to Naomi's God in one of the most quoted passages in the Old Testament.

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- Ruth 1:8-9 (WEB, trimmed): "Naomi said to her two daughters-in-law, 'Go, return each of you to
  her mother's house... May Yahweh grant you that you may find rest, each of you in the house of
  her husband.'"
- Ruth 1:16 (WEB): "Don't urge me to leave you, and to return from following you, for where you go,
  I will go; and where you stay, I will stay. Your people will be my people, and your God my God."
- Ruth 1:17 (WEB): "Where you die, I will die, and there I will be buried. May Yahweh do so to me,
  and more also, if anything but death parts you and me."

*Source: [ebible.org WEB, Ruth 1](https://ebible.org/web/RUT01.htm), cross-checked against
[biblegateway.com WEB, Ruth 1:16-17](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Ruth+1%3A16-17&version=WEB) — exact match, two independent sources (ebible's initial extraction truncated the second sentence of 1:16; the full verse was re-fetched and confirmed against both sources).*

### 2. Gleaning in Boaz's Field (Ruth 2)
Ruth goes to glean leftover grain to support herself and Naomi, and "happens" to end up in a field
belonging to Boaz, a relative of Naomi's late husband. Boaz notices her, extends protection and
provision beyond what the law required, and credits her for her loyalty to Naomi.

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- Ruth 2:2-3 (WEB, trimmed): "Ruth the Moabitess said to Naomi, 'Let me now go to the field, and
  glean among the ears of grain after him in whose sight I find favor.'... she happened to come to
  the portion of the field belonging to Boaz, who was of the family of Elimelech."
- Ruth 2:8-9 (WEB, trimmed): "Then Boaz said to Ruth, 'Listen, my daughter. Don't go to glean in
  another field, and don't go from here, but stay here close to my maidens... Haven't I commanded
  the young men not to touch you?'"
- Ruth 2:11-12 (WEB, trimmed): "I have been told all about what you have done for your
  mother-in-law since the death of your husband... May Yahweh repay your work, and a full reward be
  given to you from Yahweh, the God of Israel, under whose wings you have come to take refuge."

*Source: [ebible.org WEB, Ruth 2](https://ebible.org/web/RUT02.htm).*

### 3. The Threshing Floor (Ruth 3)
On Naomi's instruction, Ruth approaches Boaz at the threshing floor at night and asks him, using the
customary language of the kinsman-redeemer institution, to take on that role for her family. Boaz
agrees to pursue it, praising her loyalty.

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- Ruth 3:1-2 (WEB, trimmed): "Naomi her mother-in-law said to her, 'My daughter, shall I not seek
  rest for you, that it may be well with you? Now isn't Boaz our kinsman...'"
- Ruth 3:9 (WEB): "She answered, 'I am Ruth your servant. Therefore spread the corner of your
  garment over your servant; for you are a near kinsman.'"
- Ruth 3:11 (WEB): "Now, my daughter, don't be afraid. I will do to you all that you say; for all
  the city of my people knows that you are a worthy woman."

*Source: [ebible.org WEB, Ruth 3](https://ebible.org/web/RUT03.htm).*

### 4. The Redemption at the Gate (Ruth 4:1-12)
Boaz raises the matter publicly before the town elders and a nearer kinsman who has first right of
redemption. That kinsman declines once he understands taking on the responsibility includes Ruth;
Boaz formally takes on the redeemer role, buying back the family's property and marrying Ruth, with
the elders as witnesses.

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- Ruth 4:9-10 (WEB, trimmed): "Boaz said to the elders and to all the people, 'You are witnesses
  today, that I have bought all that was Elimelech's, and all that was Chilion's and Mahlon's, from
  the hand of Naomi. Moreover, Ruth the Moabitess, the wife of Mahlon, I have purchased to be my
  wife... You are witnesses today.'"

*Source: [ebible.org WEB, Ruth 4](https://ebible.org/web/RUT04.htm).*

### 5. Ancestor of David (Ruth 4:13-17, 4:21-22)
Boaz and Ruth marry and have a son, Obed. The women of Bethlehem bless Naomi, who had lost
everything and now has an heir in the family line. The book closes with a short genealogy naming
Obed's descendants, ending with David — the throughline into the Samuel worlds.

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- Ruth 4:13-14 (WEB, trimmed): "So Boaz took Ruth and she became his wife... and she bore a son.
  The women said to Naomi, 'Blessed be Yahweh, who has not left you today without a near kinsman.
  Let his name be famous in Israel.'"
- Ruth 4:17 (WEB): "The women, her neighbors, gave him a name, saying, 'A son is born to Naomi'.
  They named him Obed. He is the father of Jesse, the father of David."
- Ruth 4:21-22 (WEB): "...and Salmon became the father of Boaz, and Boaz became the father of Obed,
  and Obed became the father of Jesse, and Jesse became the father of David."

*Source: [ebible.org WEB, Ruth 4](https://ebible.org/web/RUT04.htm), cross-checked against
[biblegateway.com WEB, Ruth 4:13-17](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Ruth+4%3A13-17&version=WEB) — exact match, two independent sources.*

## Contested Territory

Per `docs/CONTENT_STYLE_GUIDE.md` §4 — flagged for the theological reviewer, not resolved here:

- **Ruth's Moabite identity and Deuteronomy 23:3.** Deuteronomy 23:3 states that no
  Moabite may enter "the assembly of Yahweh," yet Ruth, a Moabite, not only joins the community but
  becomes a direct ancestor of King David. This is a long-noted point across both Jewish and
  Christian commentary, but unlike the Joshua ḥerem question, the cross-tradition sources here
  largely converge rather than genuinely split: commentary generally resolves it by distinguishing
  formal "assembly" membership/civil standing from covenant-community membership (which Ruth's own
  declaration in 1:16 establishes for herself), and rabbinic tradition specifically reads the
  Deuteronomy 23:3 exclusion as applying grammatically to Moabite men, not Moabite women. This
  brief's bounding: no challenge item asks whether Ruth's inclusion was lawful or resolves the
  Deuteronomy question — challenge items test only that Ruth was a Moabite (2:2, stated directly in
  the text) and that she became David's ancestor (4:17), both plain narrative fact. Given the
  cross-tradition convergence (not a live split), this item does not need the same
  format-restriction treatment as Joshua's ḥerem material.
- **The kinsman-redeemer (go'el) and levirate-marriage custom (Ruth 4, cf. Deuteronomy 25:5-10).**
  Not a doctrinal dispute, but an ancient legal/economic institution unfamiliar to a modern
  audience — Boaz's language ("I have purchased...to be my wife," 4:10) can read to modern ears as
  transactional in a way that invites an anachronistic judgment. This brief's bounding: challenge
  items test what happened (the redemption transaction, the marriage, the elders as witnesses),
  never invite a player to evaluate the custom itself by modern standards. This is a comprehension
  note for challenge-authoring more than a theological fault line, but flagged per the style
  guide's "even if it says none identified" requirement — here it isn't "none," but it is
  low-severity relative to the other two books in this batch. **Structural rule (added per
  theological review, `docs/reviews/ruth-review.md`): Ruth 4:9-10 challenge items are restricted to
  recall/fill-in-blank or narrative-sequence format only — never multiple-choice framed around
  whether the transaction/custom was fair or appropriate by modern standards.**
