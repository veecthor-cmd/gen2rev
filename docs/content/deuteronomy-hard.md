## Design intent for this tier

Per the difficulty-mode pattern established on Genesis: "hard... needs very deep scripture
knowledge." This tier draws on verses that are significant but less likely to be immediately
recognized — precise phrasing, secondary passages within the same 5 scenes — and requires typed
recall (no multiple-choice scaffolding) wherever the format allows. It stays inside the same
guardrails as every other tier: verbatim WEB text only, narrative fact only, no doctrinal position
required. Both inherited structural rules carry forward: Deuteronomy 30:19-20 is used only in recall
format, never multiple choice; every item touching Deuteronomy 5 is referenced by chapter:verse only,
never by ordinal commandment number.

## Pivotal Scenes — reused from `docs/content/deuteronomy.md`, no new scenes added

Same 5 scenes as `medium` and `easy`: The Ten Commandments Restated (Deuteronomy 5), The Shema
(Deuteronomy 6:4-9), The Covenant Renewed: Blessing, Curse, and "Choose Life" (Deuteronomy 11, 27-28,
30), Moses Commissions Joshua (Deuteronomy 31:1-8), The Death of Moses on Mount Nebo (Deuteronomy 34).

## Contested Territory

Everything already flagged in `docs/content/deuteronomy.md` and `docs/content/deuteronomy-easy.md`
applies unchanged: the Ten Commandments numbering difference (items 1, 2, 12 — referenced by
chapter:verse only, never by ordinal number); Deuteronomy 28's graphic curse-list content (excluded
entirely); the Shema's later liturgical role (item 9, reused under existing bounding); the
choose-life/free-will framing and Deuteronomy 30:19-20's inherited "recall/fill-in-blank/sequence
only, never multiple choice" rule (item 11, applied); violent-conquest language adjacent to the
Joshua-commissioning scene (item 6 — stays on the succession/encouragement content, avoids the
destruction-of-nations clauses in the surrounding verses).

**New for this tier — reviewed against the sources already cited in `docs/reviews/
deuteronomy-review.md` plus additional checking below, flagged for the theological reviewer, not
resolved here:**

- **Deuteronomy 5:9-10** (item 2), "visiting the iniquity of the fathers on the children... to the
  third and on the fourth generation... showing loving kindness to thousands." This is a genuine,
  live theological question — the tension between this verse's generational-consequence language and
  passages like Ezekiel 18:20 ("the soul who sins shall die... the son shall not bear the iniquity of
  the father," outside MVP scope) is a real and ongoing pastoral/theological discussion about
  corporate versus individual responsibility, read differently across and even within traditions
  (most classic commentators read it as natural/covenantal consequence rather than judicial guilt
  transfer, but this is not a settled, uncontested reading). **Structural rule: this item is
  recall-only, never multiple-choice; the blank must fall on "thousands" (the mercy clause) and must
  never fall on or ask about the "third and fourth generation"/"iniquity of the fathers" clause; no
  challenge in any tier may ask what this verse means about inherited guilt or corporate
  punishment.** **Tightened per review §6.2: no blank may fall anywhere inside the punishment
  clause, including on textually neutral words within it ("third," "fourth," "generation,"
  "jealous") — the existing rule forbade grading the clause's meaning; this additionally forbids
  grading its words**, so a future authoring pass cannot blank a neutral-looking word (e.g. "on the
  third and on the ___ generation" → "fourth") and still foreground the contested clause for no
  pedagogical gain.
- **Deuteronomy 6:8-9** (item 3), "You shall bind them for a sign on your hand, and they shall be for
  frontlets between your eyes. You shall write them on the door posts of your house and on your
  gates." A genuine, live cross-tradition divergence distinct from the Shema's already-flagged
  liturgical-role note: Jewish tradition and continuous practice (attested from antiquity, including
  at Qumran) reads this literally, producing tefillin (phylacteries) and mezuzot; many Christian
  traditions read the same instruction figuratively, as a call to constant remembrance rather than a
  literal physical practice. **Structural rule: this item is recall-only, never multiple-choice;
  grades only the plain instructional text (what it says to do); no challenge in any tier may ask
  whether the instruction is meant literally or figuratively, or which tradition's practice is
  correct.** **Extended per review §6.2: no challenge may grade the hand/arm rendering** (Hebrew
  *yad* covers both, and Rashi's own gloss is "the Tephillin that are placed on the **arm**," which
  English versions and traditions render differently), **and "frontlets" may never be the blanked
  token** — a genuinely obscure and variously-rendered word (Revised JPS transliterates it
  *totafot*) that would produce an unfair answer key without any doctrinal question ever being
  asked.
- **Deuteronomy 34:6** (item 7), "no man knows where his tomb is to this day." Both
  `docs/content/deuteronomy.md` and `docs/reviews/deuteronomy-review.md` already identified this
  clause as touching varied interpretive traditions about *why* the grave location is unknown —
  but both prior briefs deliberately left it unused as challenge-ready text. This tier is the first
  to use it, applying the same "stay narrative, don't grade the interpretation" approach already used
  elsewhere in this pipeline (e.g., Genesis 22:13's "ram" item). **Structural rule: this item grades
  only the narrative fact that the tomb's location is unknown — never why it was kept unknown; no
  challenge in any tier may ask for or imply a reason.** **Correction and extension per review
  §6.2/§4.3:** `docs/reviews/deuteronomy-review.md`'s earlier conclusion of "substantial convergence,
  not real divergence" on this point does not hold on a fresh look and should not be relied on — the
  divergence is real and partly intra-Christian: Barnes explicitly **rejects** the prevent-idolatry
  reading ("Hardly, lest the grave of Moses should become an object of superstitious honor") and
  instead reads bodily translation like Enoch/Elijah, a reading Keil-Delitzsch and the Pulpit
  Commentary share, several citing **Jude 1:9** (NT, out of MVP scope) in support — a substantively
  different claim from the prevention-of-idolatry reading shared by Ralbag, Meshekh Chokhmah, Benson,
  and Poole. This makes the item's narrative-fact-only bounding load-bearing rather than merely
  prudent. **Additional rule: no challenge may grade *who* buried Moses** — WEB and Revised JPS both
  make Yahweh/a divine agent the subject, while commentators variously assign the act to God directly
  or to an angel (Geneva: "the angel of the Lord"; Gill: Michael). The current item does not, and
  should not start.
- **Deuteronomy 30:11-14** (item 5), "the word is very near to you, in your mouth and in your heart."
  **Rewritten per review §6.2/§4.4** — the original entry framed this only as a Romans 10:6-8
  adjacency with "no grading risk." The live disagreement is larger than that: it is about the
  **referent** of "this commandment"/"the word" in the OT passage itself, and traditions genuinely
  differ — Ramban (Nachmanides) reads "this commandment" as referring specifically to repentance
  (*teshuvah*), not the Torah as a whole; classic Christian commentators read it as the law's own
  practicability (Keil-Delitzsch, Cambridge Bible), then further divide over Paul's reuse (JFB and
  Matthew Henry read it as pointing toward the gospel; Cambridge treats that as Paul's own
  application, not the original sense). Any item asking "what is 'the word' that is near?" would
  require picking one of these positions. The item survives because the graded token is **"near,"**
  the one premise every position shares (nearness/accessibility), not a party to their disagreement.
  **Structural rule: no challenge in any tier may ask what "this commandment" or "the word" refers
  to, and neither phrase may be a blanked token.**
- **No other new contested ground** — items 1, 4, 6, 8, and boss items 9-10 (verbatim reuse) are
  plain narrative description or exact reuse of already-bounded `medium`/`easy` text.
- **Cross-tier note, not a defect of this brief (review §5.2/§6.7):** item 10 (Deut 34:10) is reused
  verbatim from the approved `medium` brief and displays a terminal period; live WEB ends the verse
  with a comma, the sentence continuing into vv.11-12. This is the same trim-presented-as-whole
  pattern as item 8's original defect, but it is inherited, already-approved text, not a defect
  introduced by this brief, so it is not changed here. It should be raised against
  `docs/content/deuteronomy.md` and picked up by the verbatim-QA pass across all three tiers.
