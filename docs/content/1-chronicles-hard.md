## Design intent for this tier

Draws on verses that are narratively significant but less likely to be immediately recognized —
precise phrasing, secondary detail — and requires typed recall (no multiple-choice scaffolding)
wherever the format allows.

## Contested Territory

Everything already flagged in `docs/content/1-chronicles.md` applies unchanged: the 1 Chronicles
21:1 vs. 2 Samuel 24:1 divergence (not itself re-used as a new item in this tier, but item 2's scene
sits inside the same chapter), the 22:7-8/28:3 "man of war" restriction on David building the temple
(item 5 reuses 22:7-8 verbatim under the same bounding already reviewed at `medium`), and the
genealogies' scope decision (item 1 stays a bare name recall, per the inherited structural rule).

**New for this tier:** items 3 (22:5, Solomon's youth and the temple's planned magnificence) and 4
(29:14, the people's willing offering) are plain narrative description and direct quotation with no
live cross-tradition disagreement identified, and require no denominational position or moral
judgment. Items 2 and 6, however, each carry ground the original draft missed — see the two required
bullets below (review §3.1/Finding A and §3.2/Finding B).

- **1 Chronicles 21:18 — Ornan vs. Araunah cross-book divergence. [required, review Finding A]** Item
  2's graded token, "Ornan," is a Chronicles-vs-Samuel name divergence of the same class as the
  already-flagged 21:1 census-agent divergence: 2 Samuel 24:16-18 calls the same man "Araunah." Cross-
  tradition check: Jewish (Sefaria, Revised JPS 2023 + Masoretic text of I Chronicles 21:18 —
  https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/I_Chronicles.21.18?context=0) renders "the threshing floor of
  Ornan the Jebusite," Hebrew `אׇרְנָ֥ן`; classic Christian commentators (Bible Hub aggregation, 1 Chr
  21:18 — https://biblehub.com/commentaries/1_chronicles/21-18.htm) treat the two names as the same
  person under two forms without dispute (Keil & Delitzsch: "Ornan's (Araunah's)"; Cambridge Bible
  cross-references 2 Samuel 24:18 without comment). **Conclusion: not contested territory in the §3
  doctrinal sense** — both traditions converge that the Chronicles form is "Ornan," and no tradition
  builds a doctrinal position on the name — **but it is a live answer-key risk**: a player who knows
  the Samuel telling may type "Araunah," which is a correct name for the person and a wrong answer for
  the verse displayed here. **Structural rule: the accepted answer for item 2 is "Ornan" only; the
  prompt cites 1 Chronicles 21:18 explicitly so the player knows which book's telling is on screen**
  (applied to item 2's prompt above).
- **1 Chronicles 28:9's elided seek/forsake clause. [required, review Finding B]** Item 6 displays
  28:9 up to "with a willing mind" and resumes at "Take heed now" (28:10), eliding "If you seek him,
  he will be found by you; but if you forsake him, he will cast you off forever." Cross-tradition
  check (Bible Hub aggregation, 1 Chr 28:9 —
  https://biblehub.com/commentaries/1_chronicles/28-9.htm) found genuinely differing scope readings of
  that clause among classic Christian commentators: Gill confines "cast thee off for ever" to
  temporal/dynastic loss; Benson reads it against the dynastic promise; Poole reads it as aimed at
  insincere profession; Matthew Henry's Concise stays relational/experiential — the same clause read
  as royal-office loss, covenant standing, or sincerity of profession depending on the commentator.
  **The item itself is safe as written** — "mind" is graded from the "how to serve" clause, and the
  contested clause is never displayed — but per the style guide this must be flagged deliberately
  rather than left unnoticed. **Structural rule: the display window for 28:9-10 never extends past
  "with a willing mind" into the seek/forsake material, and "forsake" / "cast you off forever" is
  never a graded token or a multiple-choice subject at any tier** (applied to item 6 above).
