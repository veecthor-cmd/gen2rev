## Design intent for this tier

Per the difficulty-mode design rules: this tier draws the most iconic, broadly-recognizable facts
from 1 Kings's 5 scenes and favors multiple-choice recognition over typed recall, so a first-time
player with no prior Bible knowledge can succeed. It does not simplify or paraphrase verse text —
the style guide's "verbatim WEB text only" rule applies identically at every tier.

## Pivotal Scenes — reused from `docs/content/1-kings.md`, no new scenes added

Same 5 scenes: Solomon's Request for Wisdom (1 Kings 3:5-14), Solomon's Judgment: The Two Women
(1 Kings 3:16-28), The Temple's Construction and Dedication (1 Kings 6, 8), The Kingdom Splits
(1 Kings 12), Elijah vs. the Prophets of Baal on Mount Carmel (1 Kings 18:20-39). 1 Kings 18:40 (the
killing of the defeated prophets) remains excluded, per the medium brief. The "two women" framing is
kept as-is — 1 Kings 3:16 (which names the women as prostitutes) is not selected as challenge-ready
here, consistent with the medium brief's handling.

## Challenge Items (16: 12 regular + 4 boss)

### Regular (12)

**Scene 1 — Solomon's Request for Wisdom**

1. **1 Kings 3:5** (WEB, already-approved verse, new format): "In Gibeon, Yahweh appeared to
   Solomon in a dream by night; and God said, 'Ask for what I should give you.'" — MC: "What did God
   say to Solomon in the dream at Gibeon?" Options: that Solomon should ask for what he wanted God to
   give him / that Solomon should build him a house / that Solomon should choose a wife / that
   Solomon should fight his enemies. Correct: that Solomon should ask for what he wanted God to give
   him. *(Options unquoted per review Finding B — three were invented text inside quotation marks
   beside a verbatim one; all four are now unquoted descriptions, matching the pattern already used
   in items 5 and 9.)*
2. **1 Kings 3:9** (WEB, already-approved verse): "Give your servant therefore an understanding
   heart to judge your people, that I may discern between good and evil; for who is able to judge
   this great people of yours?" — recall: "Give your servant therefore an ___ heart to judge your
   people." → **understanding**.
3. **1 Kings 3:12** (WEB, already-approved verse, trimmed): "I have given you a wise and
   understanding heart, so that there has been no one like you before you, and after you none will
   arise like you." — MC: "What did God give Solomon because he asked for wisdom instead of long
   life or riches?" Options: a wise and understanding heart / great wealth / a large army / many
   sons. Correct: a wise and understanding heart.

**Scene 2 — The Two Women**

4. **1 Kings 3:25** (WEB, already-approved verse): "The king said, 'Divide the living child in two,
   and give half to the one, and half to the other.'" — MC: "What did the king order?" Options: Divide
   the living child in two / Ask them to swear an oath / Send for witnesses / Give the child to the
   older woman. Correct: Divide the living child in two. *(Stem tightened per review §7.8 — the
   original stem attributed settlement of the dispute to this order, but the text settles it at
   3:27.)*
5. **1 Kings 3:26** (WEB, already-approved verse, trimmed): "Then the woman whose the living child
   was spoke to the king, for her heart yearned over her son, and she said, 'Oh, my lord, give her
   the living child, and in no way kill him!'" — MC: "What did the child's true mother beg the king
   to do?" Options: Give the child to the other woman rather than kill him / Divide the child as
   ordered / Take the child herself / Punish the other woman. Correct: Give the child to the other
   woman rather than kill him.

**Scene 3 — The Temple's Construction and Dedication**

6. **1 Kings 8:10-11** (WEB, already-approved verse): "It came to pass, when the priests had come
   out of the holy place, that the cloud filled Yahweh's house, so that the priests could not stand
   to minister by reason of the cloud; for Yahweh's glory filled Yahweh's house." — MC: "What filled
   Yahweh's house so that the priests could not continue ministering?" Options: A cloud / Smoke from
   a fire / Music / A crowd of people. Correct: A cloud.
7. **1 Kings 6:7** (WEB, new): "The house, when it was under construction, was built of stone
   prepared at the quarry; and no hammer or ax or any tool of iron was heard in the house while it
   was under construction." — MC: **"According to 1 Kings 6:7, what sound was never heard in the
   house while the temple was under construction?"** Options: a hammer, ax, or iron tool /
   singing / trumpets / footsteps. Correct: a hammer, ax, or iron tool. *(Stem anchored to the
   specific verse, per the lesson from the Genesis 8:11 pilot review — see Contested Territory
   below. Stem corrected from "at the building site" to "in the house" per review §7.3/§5.1 — the
   original phrasing reached toward the one clause where traditions genuinely diverge on where tools
   were used.)*
8. **1 Kings 8:23** (WEB, trimmed): "Yahweh, the God of Israel, there is no God like you, in heaven

   before you with all their heart;" — MC: "In his temple prayer, what did Solomon say there is none
   like, in heaven or on earth?" Options: God / the ark / the temple / the priesthood. Correct: God.
   *(Re-tagged "(WEB, trimmed)" per review Finding A/§7.1 — full verse opens "and he said,".)*

## Contested Territory

Everything already flagged in `docs/content/1-kings.md` applies unchanged: the 12:15 kingdom-split
causation question (not engaged by any item in this tier — 12:15 itself is not selected), the 1
Kings 11 gap note, the exclusion of 18:40, and the 3:16 "prostitutes" detail (this tier keeps the
"two women" framing and does not select 3:16). The 8:27 transcendence question is **not** engaged in
this tier — 8:27 is not used here; this tier's item 8 uses 8:23 instead, bounded separately below.
*(Corrected per review §7.10 — the original line implied 8:27 was in play here.)*

**New for this tier — reviewed against live cross-tradition sources in
`docs/reviews/1-kings-easy-review.md` §5, bounding confirmed to hold in every case:**

- **1 Kings 6:7 (item 7)**: reviewed against Jewish tradition (Rashi, via Sefaria) and classic
  Christian commentary (Bible Hub aggregation): a real, old cross-tradition divergence exists over
  *how* and *where* the stones were prepared — Rashi's shamir tradition holds no metal was used
  anywhere, while several Christian commentators read iron tools as used at the quarry (for reasons
  ranging from Exodus/Deuteronomy's altar-tool prohibitions to symbolic readings of construction
  silence). The disagreement does not reach the item's graded fact: every source agrees no hammer,
  ax, or iron tool was heard **in the house** itself. **Structural rule: no challenge in any tier may
  grade how the stones were prepared, where the tools were used, or why iron was excluded — only
  that no such sound was heard in the house.** *(Replaces the original "no known cross-tradition
  dispute" claim per review §7.4 — that claim was not supported by the sources.)*
- **1 Kings 8:23 (item 8)**: sits in the same temple-prayer material as 8:27's transcendence
  question already bounded at `medium`. This item grades only what Solomon says ("there is no God
  like you"), never a position on divine uniqueness/monotheism as a doctrine — narrative
  quotation only, same bounding pattern as 8:27. **Structural rule: no challenge may grade whether
  other gods exist or how many gods there are; the plural/singular "servants" and capital/lowercase
  "God/god" renderings across translations are display-only and are never a graded token or
  distractor axis.**
- **1 Kings 18:22 (item 12)**: the "450" figure is plain narrative fact (the number the text states
  for Baal's prophets), not a claim requiring cross-tradition checking. **Structural rule: any item
  grading this number must name Baal explicitly and cite 18:22 — 1 Kings 18:19 separately lists 400
  prophets of the Asherah, so an unanchored question about "how many prophets Elijah faced" has a
  defensible answer of 850.**
- **Item 13's opening beat** ("The women are brought before the king") corresponds to 1 Kings 3:16,
  the one verse in Scene 2 deliberately excluded from challenge-ready selection. **Structural rule:
  this beat is narrative-summary only and never displays 3:16 text.**
- **Stem-anchoring lesson applied (item 7).** Per the Genesis pilot review's finding on Genesis
  8:11 (an unanchored MC stem produced a real answer-key ambiguity because the surrounding chapter
  had more than one textually-correct answer): item 7's stem is anchored to "According to 1 Kings
  6:7," and was checked against the surrounding verses (6:1-38) for any other passage describing
  temple-construction sounds — none was found, so the anchoring is a precaution, not a fix for a
  found ambiguity.
- **No new contested ground otherwise** — items 1-6, 9-11 reuse already-approved verse text under
  the same bounding already reviewed at `medium`; items 13-15 are unmodified reuses of already-
  reviewed `medium` content.
