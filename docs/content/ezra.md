## Setting / Era

Ezra opens where 2 Chronicles closes — Cyrus of Persia's decree releasing the Jewish exiles to
return to Judah and rebuild Yahweh's house in Jerusalem, after roughly 70 years of Babylonian
captivity. The book covers two waves of return separated by decades: first, the return under
Sheshbazzar/Zerubbabel and the high priest Jeshua, rebuilding the altar and (after opposition and
delay) the temple itself, completed and dedicated under Persian king Darius; second, decades later,
the return of Ezra himself — a priest and scribe "skilled in the law of Moses" — under King
Artaxerxes, bringing renewed devotion to the Law and provoking a national reckoning over
intermarriage with surrounding peoples. This is the first of the three post-exilic History-group
books added in the expansion (`docs/CANON_STRUCTURE.md` §6), following directly after World 14
(2 Chronicles) in the History grouping.

**A note on chronology, stated up front:** the relative dating and order of Ezra's and Nehemiah's
own careers (which Persian king "Artaxerxes" refers to, whether Ezra's return preceded or followed
Nehemiah's) is a genuine, live historical-critical debate among scholars — see Contested Territory
below. This brief does not assert a resolved chronology; it follows each book's own internal
narrative order for sequencing challenges, which is in-bounds per `docs/CONTENT_STYLE_GUIDE.md` §2
("the order events happen in the text").

## Pivotal Scenes (5)

### 1. Cyrus's Decree and the Return (Ezra 1)
Yahweh stirs the spirit of Cyrus, king of Persia, who issues a proclamation releasing the exiles to
return to Jerusalem and rebuild Yahweh's house, and returns the temple vessels Nebuchadnezzar had
taken.

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- Ezra 1:1 (WEB): "Now in the first year of Cyrus king of Persia, that Yahweh's word by Jeremiah's
  mouth might be accomplished, Yahweh stirred up the spirit of Cyrus king of Persia, so that he made
  a proclamation throughout all his kingdom, and put it also in writing, saying,"
- Ezra 1:3 (WEB): "Whoever there is among you of all his people, may his God be with him, and let
  him go up to Jerusalem, which is in Judah, and build the house of Yahweh, the God of Israel (he is
  God), which is in Jerusalem."
- Ezra 1:11 (WEB, trimmed): "All the vessels of gold and of silver were five thousand four hundred.
  Sheshbazzar brought all these up when the captives were brought up from Babylon to Jerusalem."

*Source: [ebible.org WEB, Ezra 1](https://ebible.org/web/EZR01.htm), cross-checked against
[biblegateway.com WEB, Ezra 1](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Ezra+1%2C+Ezra+3%2C+Ezra+7&version=WEB) — exact match, two independent sources.*

### 2. Rebuilding the Altar and the Temple Foundation (Ezra 3)
The returned exiles rebuild the altar first, in spite of fear of surrounding peoples, and resume
sacrifices. The following year they lay the temple's foundation with singing and trumpets; the
sound is a mix of loud joy from those who never saw the first temple and loud weeping from the old
men who remember it, so the two sounds could not be told apart.

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- Ezra 3:3 (WEB, trimmed): "In spite of their fear because of the peoples of the surrounding lands,
  they set the altar on its base; and they offered burnt offerings on it to Yahweh, even burnt
  offerings morning and evening."
- Ezra 3:11 (WEB): "They sang to one another in praising and giving thanks to Yahweh, 'For he is
  good, for his loving kindness endures forever toward Israel.' All the people shouted with a great
  shout, when they praised Yahweh, because the foundation of Yahweh's house had been laid."
- Ezra 3:13 (WEB): "...so that the people could not discern the noise of the shout of joy from the
  noise of the weeping of the people; for the people shouted with a loud shout, and the noise was
  heard far away."

*Source: [ebible.org WEB, Ezra 3](https://ebible.org/web/EZR03.htm), cross-checked against
[biblegateway.com WEB, Ezra 3](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Ezra+1%2C+Ezra+3%2C+Ezra+7&version=WEB) — exact match, two independent sources.*

### 3. Opposition, the Work Resumes, and the Temple Completed (Ezra 4-6)
Neighboring peoples oppose the rebuilding and the work halts for years, until the prophets Haggai
and Zechariah spur the people to resume. King Darius confirms Cyrus's original decree after a
records search, the temple is finished and dedicated with sacrifices, and the returned exiles keep
Passover together.

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- Ezra 6:14 (WEB, trimmed): "The elders of the Jews built and prospered, through the prophesying of
  Haggai the prophet and Zechariah the son of Iddo. They built and finished it, according to the
  commandment of the God of Israel, and according to the decree of Cyrus, Darius, and Artaxerxes
  king of Persia."
- Ezra 6:16 (WEB): "The children of Israel, the priests, the Levites, and the rest of the children
  of the captivity, kept the dedication of this house of God with joy."
- Ezra 6:22 (WEB, trimmed): "...and kept the feast of unleavened bread seven days with joy; because
  Yahweh had made them joyful, and had turned the heart of the king of Assyria to them, to
  strengthen their hands in the work of God, the God of Israel's house."

*Source: [ebible.org WEB, Ezra 6](https://ebible.org/web/EZR06.htm). Not yet independently
cross-checked against a second source — flag for `docs/reviews/ezra-review.md` §1 and QA §3.*

### 4. Ezra the Scribe: Devotion to the Law (Ezra 7)
Decades later, Ezra — a priest descended from Aaron and "a skilled scribe in the law of Moses" —
receives a sweeping letter of authorization from King Artaxerxes and leads a second group of exiles
back to Jerusalem, arriving after a four-month journey. The text summarizes his defining
characteristic in a single line: he had set his heart to seek, do, and teach Yahweh's law.

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- Ezra 7:6 (WEB, trimmed): "...this Ezra went up from Babylon. He was a skilled scribe in the law
  of Moses, which Yahweh, the God of Israel, had given; and the king granted him all his request,
  according to Yahweh his God's hand on him."
- Ezra 7:10 (WEB): "For Ezra had set his heart to seek Yahweh's law, and to do it, and to teach
  statutes and ordinances in Israel."
- Ezra 7:9 (WEB, trimmed): "...on the first day of the first month he began to go up from Babylon;
  and on the first day of the fifth month he came to Jerusalem, according to the good hand of his
  God on him."

*Source: [ebible.org WEB, Ezra 7](https://ebible.org/web/EZR07.htm), cross-checked against
[biblegateway.com WEB, Ezra 7](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Ezra+1%2C+Ezra+3%2C+Ezra+7&version=WEB) — exact match, two independent sources, for 7:9-10.*

### 5. National Repentance Over Intermarriage (Ezra 9-10) — see Contested Territory, escalated
Ezra learns that many returned exiles, including priests and leaders, have intermarried with
surrounding peoples in violation of the Law. He tears his clothes, pulls out his own hair in grief,
and prays a public prayer of confession on the people's behalf. A large assembly gathers, weeping;
one leader, Shecaniah, proposes that the people covenant to separate from their foreign wives, "and
let it be done according to the law." Ezra leads the assembly in swearing to this. The book closes
with a list of the men involved.

**Challenge-ready verses** (confession/repentance content only — see note below on what is
deliberately excluded from this scene):
- Ezra 9:3 (WEB, trimmed): "When I heard this thing, I tore my garment and my robe, and pulled the
  hair out of my head and of my beard, and sat down confounded."
- Ezra 9:6 (WEB, trimmed): "My God, I am ashamed and blush to lift up my face to you, my God, for
  our iniquities have increased over our head..."
- Ezra 10:1 (WEB, trimmed): "Now while Ezra prayed and made confession, weeping and casting himself
  down before God's house, there was gathered together to him out of Israel a very great assembly of
  men and women and children; for the people wept very bitterly."

**Deliberately excluded from challenge-ready content:** Ezra 9:1-2 (the description of specific
peoples the exiles intermarried with, listed as "abominations"), Ezra 10:3 (Shecaniah's proposal to
"put away all the wives and those who are born of them"), Ezra 10:10-12 (Ezra's directive and the
assembly's agreement to carry it out), and Ezra 10:44 (the closing statement that some of the men
"had wives by whom they had children") are all part of this chapter's text but are **not** selected
as challenge-ready verses. The scene description above states plainly what happened — that a
national covenant was made to separate from foreign wives — without inventing detail beyond what
the text narrates and without a recallable/gradable verse asking a player to process the fate of the
wives or the children specifically. This follows the same bounding pattern as
`docs/content/2-samuel.md`'s Scene 4 (Bathsheba/Uriah, excluding chapter 11 entirely from
challenge-ready text) and `docs/content/joshua.md`'s ḥerem-clause exclusions. **Unlike those two
precedents, this reviewer (self-run, see `docs/reviews/ezra-review.md`) is not confident this
exclusion-based bounding alone is sufficient here** — see that review's verdict and the escalation
noted in this brief's status header above.

## Contested Territory

Per `docs/CONTENT_STYLE_GUIDE.md` §4 — flagged for review, not resolved here:

- **Ezra 9-10, the mass divorce of foreign wives — the central, unresolved item in this brief.**
  See Scene 5 above and `docs/reviews/ezra-review.md` for the full cross-tradition sourcing.
  Summary: this is not a settled reading. Some Christian commentary (e.g. Ligonier) explicitly notes
  tension with later biblical teaching against divorcing an unbelieving spouse (1 Cor. 7:12-16) and
  states plainly "the community may have erred in these divorces" as a live possibility, not just a
  fringe view. Jewish and academic sources (Center for Online Judaic Studies) confirm the text
  itself, read plainly, describes both wives and children being separated from the community, and
  that the Mosaic Law itself does not explicitly demand this remedy — raising a real question about
  how the community's own action should be read. This brief does not attempt to resolve that
  question; it excludes the specific divorce/expulsion verses from challenge-ready content (see
  Scene 5) and escalates the scene as a whole given the child-safety dimension.
- **Ezra 1:1, prophecy fulfillment.** The verse's own text states the return fulfilled "Yahweh's
  word by Jeremiah's mouth" — this is direct verse content, in-bounds for recall per
  `docs/CONTENT_STYLE_GUIDE.md` §2. **Structural rule (prudence measure, same logic as Genesis
  1:27/3:15):** any challenge item built from this verse must stay in recall/fill-in-blank format —
  never a multiple-choice item asking a player to affirm that the prophecy was in fact fulfilled as
  a historical/theological claim beyond what the verse itself states.
- **Chronology of Ezra and Nehemiah.** Genuine, live historical-critical debate over which Persian
  king "Artaxerxes" refers to and whether Ezra's return preceded or followed Nehemiah's (see this
  brief's Setting/Era note). This brief takes no position — challenges sequence events only within
  each book's own narrative order, never across a reconstructed cross-book timeline.
- **Opposition from "the peoples of the land" (Ezra 4).** Not selected as challenge-ready content in
  this brief (Scene 3 summarizes only that opposition occurred and was later overcome) — the
  specific identity and later history of this group (sometimes connected to Samaritan origins in
  later tradition) is a separate historical question this brief does not need to take a position on
  since no verse from Ezra 4 is challenge-ready text here.
