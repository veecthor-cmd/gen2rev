## A note on this brief's scope and thinness

Haggai is two chapters. Per `docs/CANON_STRUCTURE.md` §2's guidance for the Minor Prophets ("expect
thin content briefs for several of these"), this brief selects **4 pivotal passages** that trace the
book's single, straightforward story — the temple rebuild is stalled, the prophet rebukes and
commands, the people obey, and Yahweh promises the finished house will be greater than before —
rather than padding out the ritual-purity dialogue or the closing Zerubbabel oracle, both left out
deliberately (see Contested Territory).

## Setting / Era

Haggai prophesies to the returned exiles in Jerusalem in 520 BC, in the second year of the Persian
king Darius — the same post-exilic setting, and largely the same cast (the governor Zerubbabel and
the high priest, spelled "Joshua" in Haggai's own Hebrew text vs. "Jeshua" in Ezra's, both English
renderings of the same historical figure), already introduced in World 15's Ezra content. Ezra
narrates the return and the temple project in outline; Haggai supplies the on-the-ground prophetic
push that gets the stalled rebuilding restarted. The book is entirely a straightforward exhortation
to finish the temple — genuinely low-risk narrative-adjacent material, with no significant contested
territory in its main sequence.

## Pivotal Passages (4)

### 1. The People's Excuse and the Lord's Rebuke (Haggai 1:2, 1:4)
Sixteen years after the return from exile, the temple rebuilding has stalled. The people say the
time hasn't yet come to build Yahweh's house — while living comfortably in their own finished,
paneled homes. Yahweh's rebuke through Haggai is direct: they have time for their own houses, not
his.

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- Haggai 1:2 (WEB): "This is what Yahweh of Armies says: These people say, 'The time hasn't yet
  come, the time for Yahweh's house to be built.'"
- Haggai 1:4 (WEB): "Is it a time for you yourselves to dwell in your paneled houses, while this
  house lies waste?"

### 2. The Command to Rebuild (Haggai 1:8)
Yahweh's answer to the stalled project: go up to the mountain, bring wood, and build the house —
with a direct promise attached.

**Challenge-ready verse:**
- Haggai 1:8 (WEB): "Go up to the mountain, bring wood, and build the house. I will take pleasure
  in it, and I will be glorified," says Yahweh.

### 3. The People Obey (Haggai 1:12-13)
Zerubbabel, Joshua the high priest, and the remnant of the people obey Yahweh's voice and the words
of Haggai. Yahweh's response through the prophet is immediate reassurance: "I am with you."

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- Haggai 1:12 (WEB): "Then Zerubbabel the son of Shealtiel and Joshua the son of Jehozadak, the
  high priest, with all the remnant of the people, obeyed Yahweh their God's voice, and the words
  of Haggai the prophet, as Yahweh their God had sent him; and the people feared Yahweh."
- Haggai 1:13 (WEB): "Then Haggai, Yahweh's messenger, spoke Yahweh's message to the people,
  saying, 'I am with you,' says Yahweh."

### 4. Be Strong, and the Latter Glory (Haggai 2:4, 2:9)
A month later, with the work underway but the new house looking modest next to memories of
Solomon's temple, Yahweh sends a second message of encouragement through Haggai: be strong and
work, because the latter glory of this house will be greater than the former, and Yahweh will give
peace in this place.

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- Haggai 2:4 (WEB, trimmed): "'Yet now be strong, Zerubbabel,' says Yahweh. 'Be strong, Joshua son
  of Jehozadak, the high priest. Be strong, all you people of the land,' says Yahweh, 'and work,
  for I am with you,' says Yahweh of Armies."
- Haggai 2:9 (WEB): "'The latter glory of this house will be greater than the former,' says Yahweh
  of Armies; 'and in this place I will give peace,' says Yahweh of Armies."

## Contested Territory

Per `docs/CONTENT_STYLE_GUIDE.md` §4 — flagged for the theological reviewer, not resolved here:

- **Deliberately excluded: the Zerubbabel "signet ring" oracle (Haggai 2:20-23).** The book's
  closing oracle tells Zerubbabel he will be made "like a signet ring," language that reverses the
  curse pronounced on his ancestor Jeconiah/Coniah in Jeremiah 22:24-30 (that no descendant of his
  would prosper sitting on David's throne). Some Christian traditions read this messianically or
  typologically (Zerubbabel restoring the Davidic line toward the Messiah — Zerubbabel appears in
  Matthew 1's genealogy); other readings treat it as a bounded, personal assurance of Zerubbabel's
  own significance and protection as Judah's governor, with no further messianic claim. This is the
  same category of prophecy/fulfillment dispute already flagged as out-of-bounds in Daniel's and
  Isaiah's briefs. **No verse from 2:20-23 is selected as challenge-ready content anywhere in this
  brief.**
- **Deliberately excluded: the ritual-purity dialogue (Haggai 2:10-19).** Haggai questions the
  priests about whether holiness and uncleanness transmit by contact, then applies the answer to the
  people's unclean offerings. This is real content but adds a priestly-law technicality that doesn't
  advance the book's narrative arc and would require unpacking Levitical purity concepts out of
  scope for a thin brief — left out for scope/thinness reasons, not sensitivity.
- **The people's excuse (1:2) and modern stewardship/tithing application.** Haggai 1 is a common
  proof-text in some traditions for teaching about giving priority to religious commitments over
  personal comfort or "give to the building fund" appeals. **No challenge item asks a player to
  apply the text to modern giving, stewardship, or church-building practice** — the challenge-ready
  verses (1:2, 1:4) test only what the text says the people said and what Yahweh's rebuke states,
  staying at the narrative-fact level per `docs/CONTENT_STYLE_GUIDE.md` §3's "moral judgment
  questions" and application boundaries.
- **The "shaking of the nations" (Haggai 2:6-7), quoted in Hebrews 12:26-27.** Not selected as
  challenge-ready text. The New Testament book of Hebrews applies this language typologically to an
  eschatological "shaking" of creation — the same kind of prophecy/fulfillment reading this brief
  avoids by simply not building a challenge from the passage.
