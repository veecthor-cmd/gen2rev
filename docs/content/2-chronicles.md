## Setting / Era

2 Chronicles opens with Solomon (having succeeded David, per 1 Chronicles 28-29) building and
dedicating the temple, then largely skips the material on the kingdom split and the northern
kingdom's kings that 1-2 Kings already cover (Chronicles, written for a post-exilic Judean audience,
has little interest in the northern kingdom's history once it splits off). It follows Judah's kings
through two notable reformers, Hezekiah and Josiah, then closes with Jerusalem's fall to Babylon —
but unlike 2 Kings, closes that fall with a forward-looking note: the Persian king Cyrus's decree
permitting the exiles to return and rebuild. As World 14, the MVP's final world, this book's ending
is this brief's own deliberate capstone choice, distinct from 2 Kings' capstone (World 12) — see
Scene 4 below for the confirmed textual basis for that distinction.

## Pivotal Scenes (4)

### 1. Solomon's Temple Dedication — Chronicles' Distinct Emphasis (2 Chronicles 5, 7)
As in 1 Kings 8 (World 11's material), the ark is brought into the newly built temple and a cloud —
Yahweh's glory — fills the house so that the priests cannot continue ministering. Chronicles then
adds a detail 1 Kings' dedication account does not include: after Solomon finishes his prayer, fire
comes down from heaven and consumes the sacrifice on the altar, in full view of the assembled
people, who fall on their faces and worship.

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- 2 Chronicles 5:13-14 (WEB, trimmed — **corrected 2026-08-19**, see `docs/QA_SIGNOFF.md`): "when the
  trumpeters and singers were as one, to make one sound to be heard in praising and thanking
  Yahweh... then the house was filled with a cloud, even Yahweh's house, so that the priests could
  not stand to minister by reason of the cloud; for Yahweh's glory filled God's house."
- 2 Chronicles 7:1 (WEB): "Now when Solomon had finished praying, fire came down from heaven and
  consumed the burnt offering and the sacrifices; and Yahweh's glory filled the house."
- 2 Chronicles 7:3 (WEB, trimmed): "All the children of Israel looked on, when the fire came down,
  and Yahweh's glory was on the house. They bowed themselves with their faces to the ground on the
  pavement, worshiped, and gave thanks to Yahweh, saying, 'For he is good, for his loving kindness
  endures forever!'"

### 2. Hezekiah's Reforms: Cleansing and Reopening the Temple (2 Chronicles 29)
In his first year as king, Hezekiah reopens and repairs the temple doors, which had fallen into
disuse, and directs the priests and Levites to cleanse the temple of accumulated defilement. This
scene emphasizes the worship-restoration material 2 Kings' Hezekiah account (the Assyrian siege,
World 12) does not cover in depth.

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- 2 Chronicles 29:1-2 (WEB, trimmed): "Hezekiah began to reign when he was twenty-five years old,
  and he reigned twenty-nine years in Jerusalem... He did that which was right in Yahweh's eyes,
  according to all that David his father had done."
- 2 Chronicles 29:3 (WEB): "In the first year of his reign, in the first month, he opened the doors
  of Yahweh's house and repaired them."
- 2 Chronicles 29:18 (WEB, trimmed): "We have cleansed all Yahweh's house, including the altar of
  burnt offering with all its vessels, and the table of show bread with all its vessels."

### 3. Josiah's Reforms: The Great Passover (2 Chronicles 34-35)
As in 2 Kings 22-23 (World 12's material), Josiah's workers find the book of the Law during temple
repairs, and Josiah tears his clothes and renews the covenant on hearing it. This brief emphasizes
what 2 Chronicles gives fuller treatment than 2 Kings does: the Passover celebration that follows,
described as unmatched since the days of the prophet Samuel.

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- 2 Chronicles 34:15 (WEB): "Hilkiah answered Shaphan the scribe, 'I have found the book of the law
  in Yahweh's house.' So Hilkiah delivered the book to Shaphan."
- 2 Chronicles 35:1 (WEB): "Josiah kept a Passover to Yahweh in Jerusalem. They killed the Passover
  on the fourteenth day of the first month."
- 2 Chronicles 35:18 (WEB, trimmed): "There was no Passover like that kept in Israel from the days
  of Samuel the prophet, nor did any of the kings of Israel keep such a Passover as Josiah kept."

### 4. The Fall of Judah and Cyrus's Decree — an Ending of Hope (2 Chronicles 36) — capstone scene
Judah's later kings ignore repeated prophetic warning, and Babylon eventually conquers Jerusalem,
burning the temple and the city and carrying the survivors into exile. Unlike 2 Kings' ending
(World 12's capstone, which closes on a released-but-still-captive king), 2 Chronicles closes with
Persia's King Cyrus, decades later, decreeing that the exiles may return to Jerusalem and rebuild
the temple — the last words of the Hebrew canon in the Jewish ordering of the Tanakh, and,
correspondingly, the last scene of this MVP's 14-world arc.

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- 2 Chronicles 36:19-20 (WEB, trimmed): "They burned God's house, broke down the wall of Jerusalem,
  burned all its palaces with fire, and destroyed all of its valuable vessels. He carried those who
  had escaped from the sword away to Babylon."
- 2 Chronicles 36:22 (WEB, trimmed): "In the first year of Cyrus king of Persia... Yahweh stirred up
  the spirit of Cyrus king of Persia, so that he made a proclamation throughout all his kingdom, and
  put it also in writing."
- 2 Chronicles 36:23 (WEB, trimmed): "Cyrus king of Persia says, 'Yahweh, the God of heaven, has
  given all the kingdoms of the earth to me; and he has commanded me to build him a house in
  Jerusalem, which is in Judah... let him go up.'"

## Contested Territory

Per `docs/CONTENT_STYLE_GUIDE.md` §4 — flagged for the theological reviewer, not resolved here:

- **2 Chronicles 7:14, "if my people who are called by my name..."** Not selected as a
  challenge-ready verse in Scene 1 above by design — this verse (spoken to Solomon immediately after
  7:1-3) is one of the most frequently invoked verses in modern political and civil-religion contexts,
  commonly applied to nations generally (especially the United States in American evangelical
  discourse) despite being addressed in-text to Israel specifically under its own covenant. **Per
  theological review (`docs/reviews/2-chronicles-review.md`): this verse is excluded from
  challenge-ready content entirely, not merely restricted to recall-only format.** The
  modern-application debate around this verse is live and largely internal to one tradition's
  present-day usage (chiefly American evangelical civil religion) rather than a stable, bounded
  cross-tradition disagreement of the kind Genesis 1:27/3:15 or 1 Chronicles 21:1 present — the
  review judged full exclusion the more conservative and appropriate call, rather than the
  recall-only restriction used for those other items.
- **2 Chronicles 36:22-23 vs. 2 Kings 25:27-30 — the two different endings.** See the parallel note
  in `docs/content/2-kings.md`'s Contested Territory section. Confirmed by direct text comparison
  (not an interpretive claim): 2 Kings ends on survival without restoration; 2 Chronicles ends on a
  decree of return. This brief's Scene 4 description states this explicitly as the reason for this
  book's distinct capstone framing.
- **2 Chronicles 36:23, Cyrus as an instrument of Yahweh.** Cyrus was not an Israelite and not a
  worshiper of Yahweh in any exclusive sense; the text nonetheless says Yahweh "stirred up" his
  spirit to issue the decree. Some traditions read this typologically or providentially in ways that
  go beyond the text's own claim (e.g., connecting it to Isaiah's "Cyrus my anointed," a different
  book outside this MVP's scope). This brief's challenge item tests only what 2 Chronicles 36:22-23
  itself states, not any cross-book typological reading.
- **2 Chronicles 36:17, violence in the fall of Jerusalem.** "He had no compassion on young man or
  virgin, old man or infirm" (not selected as a challenge-ready verse above) describes the conquest
  in general terms; per the assignment's sensitivity instruction, this brief does not select this
  verse for challenge content and keeps Scene 4's challenge verses (36:19-20, 36:22, 36:23) to the
  factual outcome (temple/city burned, exile, later decree) rather than the conquest's violence
  itself.
- **Hezekiah's and Josiah's reforms — destruction of idolatrous worship sites.** Both reform
  narratives (not deeply detailed in this brief's chosen verses, which focus on cleansing/celebration
  rather than the accompanying destruction of high places and idols found elsewhere in chapters
  29-31 and 34) touch the same just-war/iconoclasm-adjacent territory the style guide flags for
  conquest narratives generally. This brief's chosen verses describe positive restoration acts
  (reopening the temple, keeping the Passover) rather than the destruction acts, which is a
  deliberate scene-selection choice, not an oversight.
