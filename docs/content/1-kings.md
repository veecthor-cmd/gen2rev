## Setting / Era

1 Kings opens where 2 Samuel (World 10) left off: David is old, and the book's first two chapters
resolve his succession, with Solomon secured on the throne. The book then follows Solomon's reign —
his request for wisdom, the building and dedication of the temple in Jerusalem, his wealth and
fame — before his death opens a fracture: the kingdom splits into a northern kingdom (Israel, under
Jeroboam) and a southern kingdom (Judah, under Rehoboam, David's line). The book's second half
follows the divided kingdom's early kings in parallel, interwoven with the prophetic ministry of
Elijah, who confronts the northern kingdom's turn to Baal worship under King Ahab. This brief picks
up the national-institutions thread (temple, monarchy, prophetic office) that Samuel's more
personal, David-centered narrative (Worlds 9–10) had not yet centered.

## Pivotal Scenes (5)

### 1. Solomon's Request for Wisdom (1 Kings 3:5-14)
Early in his reign, Yahweh appears to Solomon in a dream at Gibeon and offers to give him anything
he asks. Solomon asks not for long life, riches, or victory over enemies, but for an understanding
heart to govern well. God grants the request, and promises riches and honor besides.

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- 1 Kings 3:5 (WEB): "In Gibeon, Yahweh appeared to Solomon in a dream by night; and God said, 'Ask
  for what I should give you.'"
- 1 Kings 3:9 (WEB): "Give your servant therefore an understanding heart to judge your people, that
  I may discern between good and evil; for who is able to judge this great people of yours?"
- 1 Kings 3:12 (WEB, trimmed): "I have given you a wise and understanding heart, so that there has
  been no one like you before you, and after you none will arise like you."

### 2. Solomon's Judgment: The Two Mothers (1 Kings 3:16-28)
Two women, both claiming to be the mother of the same living infant, bring their dispute before
Solomon. He orders the child cut in two and divided between them; the true mother begs him to give
the baby to the other woman rather than see it killed, and by that response Solomon identifies her
as the real mother. The account closes on Israel's reaction: the people recognize God's wisdom at
work in the king.

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- 1 Kings 3:25 (WEB): "The king said, 'Divide the living child in two, and give half to the one, and
  half to the other.'"
- 1 Kings 3:26 (WEB, trimmed): "Then the woman whose the living child was spoke to the king, for
  her heart yearned over her son, and she said, 'Oh, my lord, give her the living child, and in no
  way kill him!'"
- 1 Kings 3:27-28 (WEB): "Then the king answered, 'Give the first woman the living child, and
  definitely do not kill him. She is his mother.' All Israel heard of the judgment which the king
  had judged; and they feared the king, for they saw that the wisdom of God was in him to do
  justice."

### 3. The Temple's Construction and Dedication (1 Kings 6, 8)
Solomon builds a permanent temple for Yahweh in Jerusalem. At its dedication, the ark of the
covenant is brought in, and a cloud — identified as Yahweh's glory — fills the house so thickly the
priests cannot continue ministering. Solomon then prays a public dedication prayer, asking that
Yahweh hear prayers made toward this place.

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- 1 Kings 8:10-11 (WEB): "It came to pass, when the priests had come out of the holy place, that the
  cloud filled Yahweh's house, so that the priests could not stand to minister by reason of the
  cloud; for Yahweh's glory filled Yahweh's house."
- 1 Kings 8:27 (WEB): "But will God in very deed dwell on the earth? Behold, heaven and the heaven
  of heavens can't contain you; how much less this house that I have built!"
- 1 Kings 8:29 (WEB, trimmed): "That your eyes may be open toward this house night and day, even
  toward the place of which you have said, 'My name shall be there.'"

### 4. The Kingdom Splits (1 Kings 12)
After Solomon's death, the northern tribes ask his son Rehoboam to lighten the labor burden Solomon
had placed on them. Rehoboam, rejecting his elder advisors' counsel in favor of his young peers',
answers harshly. The northern tribes reject Rehoboam's rule, and the kingdom divides in two:
Jeroboam becomes king over the northern tribes (Israel); Rehoboam retains only Judah in the south.

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- 1 Kings 12:4 (WEB): "Your father made our yoke difficult. Now therefore make the hard service of
  your father, and his heavy yoke which he put on us, lighter, and we will serve you."
- 1 Kings 12:14 (WEB, trimmed): "My father made your yoke heavy, but I will add to your yoke."
- 1 Kings 12:16 (WEB, trimmed): "When all Israel saw that the king didn't listen to them, the people
  answered the king, saying, 'What portion have we in David? ... To your tents, Israel!'"

### 5. Elijah vs. the Prophets of Baal on Mount Carmel (1 Kings 18:20-39)
Elijah challenges 450 prophets of Baal to a public contest on Mount Carmel: each side prepares a
sacrifice and calls on their god to send fire to consume it, without lighting the fire themselves.
Baal's prophets call out for hours with no answer. Elijah, after having his own altar and offering
drenched in water, prays a short prayer; fire falls from Yahweh, consuming the offering entirely.
The watching people fall on their faces and declare, "Yahweh, he is God!"

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- 1 Kings 18:21 (WEB): "How long will you waver between the two sides? If Yahweh is God, follow him;
  but if Baal, then follow him."
- 1 Kings 18:24 (WEB, trimmed): "You call on the name of your god, and I will call on Yahweh's name.
  The God who answers by fire, let him be God."
- 1 Kings 18:38-39 (WEB): "Then Yahweh's fire fell and consumed the burnt offering, the wood, the
  stones, and the dust; and it licked up the water that was in the trench. When all the people saw
  it, they fell on their faces. They said, 'Yahweh, he is God! Yahweh, he is God!'"

**Not selected as challenge-ready:** 1 Kings 18:40, where Elijah has the defeated prophets of Baal
seized and killed at the Kishon valley immediately after the contest. See Contested Territory below
for why this is excluded.

## Contested Territory

Per `docs/CONTENT_STYLE_GUIDE.md` §4 — flagged for the theological reviewer, not resolved here:

- **1 Kings 12:15, the kingdom split's causation.** The text itself states the split "was a thing
  brought about from Yahweh" fulfilling a prophecy given earlier (1 Kings 11, not a pivotal scene in
  this brief — see gap note below), while also narrating Rehoboam's own harsh political choice as
  the proximate trigger. Both are stated by the text; this brief's challenge items (12:4, 12:14,
  12:16) test only the narrated negotiation and the people's response, never a claim about divine
  determinism vs. human responsibility as the "real" cause.
- **Gap note: 1 Kings 11 (Solomon's turn to idolatry) is not a pivotal scene here.** The assignment
  scoped this brief's Solomon material to his wisdom and temple-building; 1 Kings 11 — where Solomon's
  foreign wives lead him into idolatry, prompting Yahweh's prophecy to Jeroboam through Ahijah — is
  the narrative cause behind the split and behind 12:15's "brought about from Yahweh" line, but isn't
  included as its own scene. This means the split scene's causation will read as somewhat
  unexplained without that backstory. Flagging this explicitly for the content team's awareness
  rather than unilaterally expanding past the assigned scope.
- **1 Kings 18:40, the killing of Baal's prophets, excluded from challenge-ready content.** This
  scene of religious violence immediately follows the contest this brief does include. Given the
  project's child-directed default and `CLAUDE.md`'s content-integrity guardrails, this brief bounds
  Scene 5 to the contest itself (18:20-39) and does not select any verse from 18:40, nor does the
  scene description above narrate it. Flagging the exclusion explicitly so it isn't silently added
  by a future challenge-authoring pass without review. **Per theological review
  (`docs/reviews/1-kings-review.md`): Christian commentary on this verse shows genuine internal
  tension even within a single tradition (justified within OT law by most classic commentators, but
  explicitly cautioned against as a model for later religious violence by others); a
  Jewish-tradition-specific source check on this exact verse was attempted but not obtained, so this
  cross-tradition check should be treated as incomplete, not fully resolved, if this verse is ever
  reconsidered for inclusion.**
- **1 Kings 3:16, the two women identified as prostitutes.** The text identifies the two women in
  the judgment scene as prostitutes (a detail present in the base text, not invented). This brief's
  challenge items and scene description refer to them only as "two women," since that identity
  detail is incidental to the judgment itself and isn't needed for any challenge item; the fuller
  identification is preserved in the verse text where it is quoted (3:16, not itself selected as
  challenge-ready) but not foregrounded. Flagging as a content-appropriateness judgment call for a
  child-directed game, not a doctrinal one.
- **1 Kings 8:27, the temple/transcendence question.** "Will God in very deed dwell on the earth?"
  touches on divine transcendence vs. immanence, a live theological topic in the abstract, but the
  verse itself is Solomon voicing the question rhetorically within his prayer — the challenge item
  tests recall of the line as spoken, not a position on the underlying question.
