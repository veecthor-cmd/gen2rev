## Design intent for this tier

Per Kachi: "easy... real simple," following the Genesis pilot's design intent
(`docs/content/genesis-easy.md`). This tier draws the most iconic, broadly-recognizable facts from
each of the book's 3 passages and favors multiple-choice recognition over typed recall. All items
in this brief sit at **`difficulty_rank` 1**. It does **not** simplify or paraphrase verse text —
verbatim WEB text only, identical rule to every other tier. This brief stays deliberately thin (9
items total against Lamentations' 5-chapter, heavily-excluded source material), the same
proportionality the medium brief itself already established.

## Pivotal Passages — reused from `docs/content/lamentations.md`, no new passages added

Same 3 passages: (1) The Opening Lament (Lam 1:1-2, 1:12); (2) The Turn to Hope, at the Book's
Darkest Point (Lam 3:18-24); (3) The Book's Closing Plea (Lam 5:19-21).

## Challenge Items (9: 6 regular + 3 boss)

### Regular (6)

1. **Lam 1:1** (WEB, already-approved verse, new question angle): "How the city sits solitary,
   that was full of people! She has become as a widow, who was great among the nations! She who
   was a princess among the provinces has become a slave!" — MC: "According to Lamentations 1:1,
   what has the city become, having once been great among the nations?" Options: a widow / a queen
   / a prisoner / a beggar. Correct: a widow.
2. **Lam 1:2** (WEB, already-approved verse, trimmed, new question angle): "She weeps bitterly in
   the night. Her tears are on her cheeks." — MC: "According to Lamentations 1:2, what does the
   city do in the night?" Options: weeps bitterly / sings mournfully / prays silently / sleeps
   peacefully. Correct: weeps bitterly.
3. **Lam 1:12** (WEB, already-approved verse, new question angle): "'Is it nothing to you, all you
   who pass by? Look, and see if there is any sorrow like my sorrow, which is brought on me, with
   which Yahweh has afflicted me in the day of his fierce anger.'" — MC: "What does the city ask of
   everyone who passes by, in Lamentations 1:12?" Options: whether any sorrow compares to hers /
   for food / for forgiveness / for shelter. Correct: whether any sorrow compares to hers.
4. **Lam 3:18** (WEB, already-approved verse, new question angle): "I said, 'My strength has
   perished, along with my expectation from Yahweh.'" — MC: "According to Lamentations 3:18, what
   does the speaker say had perished, at his lowest point?" Options: his strength and his hope in
   Yahweh / his family / his wealth / his health. Correct: his strength and his hope in Yahweh.
5. **Lam 3:21-23** (WEB, already-approved verse, new format, same as `medium`'s own Level-1 ladder
   example): "This I recall to my mind; therefore I have hope. It is because of Yahweh's loving
   kindnesses that we are not consumed, because his mercies don't fail. They are new every ___.
   Great is your faithfulness." → **morning**.
6. **Lam 5:19** (WEB, already-approved verse, new question angle): "You, Yahweh, remain forever.
   Your throne is from generation to generation." — MC: "According to Lamentations 5:19, how long
   does Yahweh's throne last?" Options: from generation to generation / for one thousand years /
   until the exile ends / it is not stated in this verse. Correct: from generation to generation.

### Boss (3)

7. **Boss sequence — reused verbatim from `medium`'s own Level-3 ladder example**: order the three
   pivotal passages as they appear in the book — the opening lament (ch. 1) → the turn to hope
   (ch. 3) → the closing plea (ch. 5).
8. **Boss quotation-order — reused verbatim from `medium`'s own Level-4 ladder example**: given
   three quotations (Lam 1:12, Lam 3:22-23, Lam 5:21), place them in the order they occur and match
   each to its reference.
9. **Lam 5:21** (WEB, already-approved verse, new question angle): "'Turn us to yourself, Yahweh,
   and we will be turned. Renew our days as of old.'" — MC: "What does the book's closing plea ask
   Yahweh to do?" Options: turn us back to you, and renew our days as of old / rebuild the temple
   immediately / send a new prophet / destroy Babylon. Correct: turn us back to you, and renew our
   days as of old.

## Contested Territory

Everything already flagged in `docs/content/lamentations.md` applies unchanged, and every
structural rule and exclusion below is carried forward verbatim from that brief — **no new
contested ground was introduced by this tier's new question angles**, since every item above draws
on verse text already reviewed at `medium` and no item extends any passage's boundary.

- **Authorship (traditional attribution to Jeremiah).** No challenge item in this brief asserts
  authorship as a testable claim, matching the medium brief's bounding — the book is presented only
  as "Lamentations" in every item above.
- **Lamentations 3:1-17, excluded affliction imagery.** No challenge-ready verse in this brief
  draws on this range — item 4-5 open at 3:18, exactly matching the medium brief's passage
  boundary. **Exclusion holds, unchanged.**
- **Lamentations 2:20 and 4:10, cannibalism during the siege.** Not selected as a pivotal passage
  and no verse from either is challenge-ready text anywhere in this brief. **Exclusion holds,
  unchanged.**
- **Lamentations 5:22, the book's actual final verse.** The closing-plea passage stops at 5:21 in
  this brief (item 9), exactly matching the medium brief's deliberate boundary — 5:22 is not used
  as challenge-ready text, for the same live-translation-ambiguity reason the medium review
  documented. **Exclusion holds, unchanged.**
- **The book's function in Jewish (Tisha B'Av) and Christian (Tenebrae) liturgy.** No challenge
  item or scene description makes a liturgical or typological claim in either direction; every item
  tests only the book's own text, matching the medium brief's bounding.
- **No new contested ground otherwise** — items 1-4, 6, and 9 are plain narrative/textual-detail
  multiple choice with no live cross-tradition disagreement bearing on what's graded.
