## Design intent for this tier

Per Kachi: "hard... needs very deep scripture knowledge." This tier draws on verses within the same
6 approved passages that are theologically or narratively significant but less likely to be
immediately recognized — precise closing clauses, secondary phrasing, less-quoted verses — and
requires typed recall (no multiple-choice scaffolding) wherever the format allows. It stays inside
the same guardrails as every other tier: verbatim WEB text only, narrative fact only, no doctrinal
position required. All items are `difficulty_rank` 5.

## Pivotal Passages — reused from `docs/content/malachi.md`, no new passages added

Same 6 passages as `medium` and `easy`: Malachi 1:6-11, 3:1-3, 3:6-10, 3:16-18, 4:1-3, 4:4-6.
**Malachi 1:2-3 and 2:10-16 remain entirely out of scope at this tier, same as every other tier** —
see Contested Territory below.

## Contested Territory

Everything already flagged in `docs/content/malachi.md` applies unchanged and is **re-confirmed to
hold for this tier**:

- **No verse from Malachi 1:2-3 ("Jacob I loved, but Esau I hated") appears anywhere in this brief.**
- **No verse from Malachi 2:10-16 (unfaithfulness and divorce) appears anywhere in this brief.**
- **Malachi 3:1 (item 3) and 3:3 (item 4)** stay strictly on identity-neutral action content — a
  messenger will come, the coming refiner purifies the sons of Levi — never on who "my messenger,"
  "the Lord," or "the messenger of the covenant" are, per the existing bounding in
  `docs/reviews/malachi-review.md` §3.3. Item 3's blank ("desire") is a verb describing the people's
  own longing, not an identity claim about any of the three figures named in the verse.
- **Malachi 3:6 (item 8) and 3:9 (item 5)** stay on the plain historical charge and Yahweh's stated
  unchangingness, with no assertion about present-day applicability, per the existing recall-only/
  no-modern-application bounding for 3:6-12 in `docs/reviews/malachi-review.md` §3.4. **Structural
  rule carried forward: the divine name "Yahweh" is never itself the blanked token in item 8 or
  anywhere else in this brief** — the blank falls on "change," not the name, consistent with this
  project's general practice of never blanking a name/pronoun that could turn a recall item into an
  identity or Trinity-adjacent question.
- **Malachi 4:5 (item 9) and 4:6 (item 11)** name "Elijah the prophet" exactly as the text does and
  assert nothing about fulfillment, timing, or identity beyond what the text states — per the
  existing bounding for 4:4-6 in `docs/reviews/malachi-review.md` §3.6. Item 11's blank ("curse")
  falls on the verse's own stated consequence, not on any disputed clause.
- **No new contested ground otherwise.** Malachi 1:6's "name" clause, 1:7, 3:17, and 4:3 are plain
  narrative description or direct quotation with no live cross-tradition disagreement identified.
