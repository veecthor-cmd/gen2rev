## Design intent for this tier

Per Kachi: "easy... real simple." This tier draws the most iconic, broadly-recognizable facts from
Ecclesiastes's 4 pivotal passages and favors multiple-choice recognition over typed recall, so a
first-time or younger player can succeed without prior Bible knowledge. It does **not** simplify or
paraphrase verse text — the style guide's "verbatim WEB text only" rule applies identically at every
tier. Like the `medium` brief, "sequence" items order *passages as they appear in the book* or *lines
within a listed passage*, not plot events — Ecclesiastes has no narrative event-sequence.

## Pivotal Passages — reused from `docs/content/ecclesiastes.md`, no new passages added

Same 4 passages: Vanity of Vanities (Ecclesiastes 1), A Time for Everything (Ecclesiastes 3), Two Are
Better Than One (Ecclesiastes 4), Remember Your Creator; Fear God and Keep His Commandments
(Ecclesiastes 12).

## Challenge Items (10: 7 regular + 3 boss)

### Regular (7)

1. **Eccl 1:2** (WEB, already-approved verse, new question angle): "'Vanity of vanities,' says the
   Preacher; 'Vanity of vanities, all is vanity.'" — MC: "According to Ecclesiastes 1:2, what does
   the Preacher say 'all' is?" Options: vanity / wisdom / gold / dust. Correct: vanity.
2. **Eccl 1:9** (WEB, already-approved verse, new question angle): "That which has been is that
   which shall be, and that which has been done is that which shall be done; and there is no new
   thing under the sun." — MC: "According to Ecclesiastes 1:9, what does the text say there is no
   new thing under?" Options: the sun / the moon / heaven / the stars. Correct: the sun.
3. **Eccl 3:1** (WEB, new): "For everything there is a season, and a time for every purpose under
   heaven:" — MC: "According to Ecclesiastes 3:1, what does the text say there is a time for, under
   heaven?" Options: every purpose / every nation / every king / every harvest. Correct: every
   purpose.
4. **Eccl 3:4** (WEB, new): "a time to weep, and a time to laugh; a time to mourn, and a time to
   dance;" — MC: "According to Ecclesiastes 3:4, 'a time to weep' is paired with a time to do what?"
   Options: laugh / sleep / rest / sing. Correct: laugh.
5. **Eccl 4:9** (WEB, already-approved verse, new question angle): "Two are better than one, because
   they have a good reward for their labor." — MC: "According to Ecclesiastes 4:9, why are two
   better than one?" Options: they have a good reward for their labor / they travel faster / they
   eat less / they are stronger alone. Correct: they have a good reward for their labor.
6. **Eccl 4:12** (WEB, already-approved verse, new question angle): "If a man prevails against one
   who is alone, two shall withstand him; and a threefold cord is not quickly broken." — MC:
   "According to Ecclesiastes 4:12, what is 'not quickly broken'?" Options: a threefold cord / a
   single thread / an iron chain / a wooden staff. Correct: a threefold cord.
7. **Eccl 12:1** (WEB, already-approved verse, new question angle): "Remember also your Creator in
   the days of your youth, before the evil days come, and the years draw near, when you will say, 'I
   have no pleasure in them;'" — MC: "According to Ecclesiastes 12:1, who should you remember 'in the
   days of your youth'?" Options: your Creator / your teacher / your king / your ancestors. Correct:
   your Creator.

## Contested Territory

Everything already flagged in `docs/content/ecclesiastes.md` applies unchanged: the book's
resolved-hope-vs-bleak interpretive fork (with its structural rule that no item may combine Passage 1
and Passage 4 material except in a pure ordering item — item 9 above is exactly that permitted form,
not an exception to it), the "vanity"/hevel translation choice, Solomonic authorship, and the
unselected chapters (2, 5-11).

**New for this tier — reviewed in `docs/reviews/ecclesiastes-easy-review.md` §3, bounding confirmed
to hold:**

- **Ecclesiastes 3:1, 3:4** (items 3-4) are plain narrative description of the poem's structure —
  no new contested ground found.
- **Ecclesiastes 3:11** (item 8) displays "He has also set eternity in their hearts" as part of the
  quoted verse text, but the graded token in this tier's item is "beautiful," from the verse's first
  sentence only — the "eternity" clause is display-only here. The "eternity"/*olam* clause is a live
  translation-choice question in its own right (see review) — not yet flagged in the `medium` brief
  because `medium` never built a graded item from 3:11's second sentence. **Structural rule (revised
  in theological review — see `docs/reviews/ecclesiastes-easy-review.md` §3 — to be consistent with
  `docs/content/ecclesiastes-hard.md` item 4, which does grade this clause): the "eternity"/*olam*
  clause of 3:11 may be graded only via verbatim recall/fill-in-the-blank, never multiple-choice, and
  no item at any tier may ask what the clause means or whether "eternity" is the correct rendering —
  the same bounding already applied throughout this book to the "vanity"/hevel translation choice.
  In this tier's item 8, the clause is displayed but not graded at all, which trivially satisfies
  this rule.**
