## Design intent for this tier

Per Kachi: "easy... real simple," following the Genesis pilot's design intent
(`docs/content/genesis-easy.md`). This tier draws the most iconic, broadly-recognizable facts from
each of Ezekiel's 6 pivotal passages and favors multiple-choice recognition over typed recall. All
items in this brief sit at **`difficulty_rank` 1**. It does **not** simplify or paraphrase verse
text — verbatim WEB text only, identical rule to every other tier. Where the medium brief restricts
Ezekiel 34:23-24 to recall-only format, that restriction is honored here even though it means this
one item doesn't follow the tier's general multiple-choice preference.

## Pivotal Passages — reused from `docs/content/ezekiel.md`, no new passages added

Same 6 passages, same verse ranges medium already scoped: (1) The Call Vision and Commissioning
(Ezek 1:1-3, 1:26-28, 2:1-10, 3:1-4); (2) The Sign-Act: Bearing Israel's and Judah's Iniquity
(Ezek 4:1-8); (3) The Watchman (Ezek 3:16-21; 33:1-11); (4) Individual Responsibility (Ezek 18:1-4,
20, 23); (5) The Shepherd of Israel (Ezek 34:1-6, 11-16, 23-24); (6) The Valley of Dry Bones
(Ezek 37:1-14).

Every verse below is drawn from inside these same six already-approved ranges. **Ezekiel 16, 23,
and 38-39 remain entirely excluded — no verse, scene description, or reference to their content
anywhere in this brief**, and the temple vision (Ezek 40-48) remains out of scope for the same
passage-vs-narrative reason it was excluded at medium.

## Challenge Items (10: 7 regular + 3 boss)

### Regular (7)

1. **Ezek 1:1** (WEB, already-approved verse, new question angle): "Now in the thirtieth year, in
   the fourth month, in the fifth day of the month, as I was among the captives by the river
   Chebar, the heavens were opened, and I saw visions of God." — MC: "According to Ezekiel 1:1,
   where was Ezekiel when the heavens opened and he saw visions of God?" Options: by the river
   Chebar / on Mount Sinai / in the Temple in Jerusalem / in the palace of Babylon. Correct: by the
   river Chebar.
2. **Ezek 2:3** (WEB, already-approved verse, new question angle): "He said to me, 'Son of man, I
   send you to the children of Israel, to a nation of rebels who have rebelled against me. They and
   their fathers have transgressed against me even to this very day.'" — MC: "According to
   Ezekiel 2:3, who did Yahweh send Ezekiel to?" Options: a nation of rebels who have rebelled
   against him / the king of Babylon / the priests of Egypt / a foreign nation with a strange
   language. Correct: a nation of rebels who have rebelled against him.
3. **Ezek 3:3** (WEB, already-approved verse, new question angle): "He said to me, 'Son of man, eat
   this scroll that I give you and fill your belly and your bowels with it.' Then I ate it. It was
   as sweet as honey in my mouth." — MC: "According to Ezekiel 3:3, what did the scroll taste like
   in Ezekiel's mouth?" Options: as sweet as honey / bitter as gall / like ash / like bread.
   Correct: as sweet as honey.
4. **Ezek 4:1** (WEB, already-approved verse, new question angle): "You also, son of man, take a
   tile, and lay it before yourself, and portray on it a city, even Jerusalem." — MC: "According to
   Ezekiel 4:1, what city did Ezekiel portray on the tile?" Options: Jerusalem / Babylon / Samaria /
   Nineveh. Correct: Jerusalem.
5. **Ezek 3:17** (WEB, already-approved verse, new question angle): "'Son of man, I have made you a
   watchman to the house of Israel. Therefore hear the word from my mouth, and warn them from
   me...'" — MC: "According to Ezekiel 3:17, what role did Yahweh give Ezekiel over the house of
   Israel?" Options: a watchman / a king / a high priest / a scribe. Correct: a watchman.
6. **Ezek 18:20** (WEB, already-approved verse, new format; trimmed for challenge use, full verse
   continues "The righteousness of the righteous shall be on him, and the wickedness of the wicked
   shall be on him."): "The soul who sins, he shall die. The son shall not bear the iniquity of the
   father, neither shall the father bear the iniquity of the son." — recall: "The soul who ___, he
   shall die." → **sins**.
7. **Ezek 34:2-3** (WEB, already-approved verse, new question angle): "Son of man, prophesy against
   the shepherds of Israel. Prophesy, and tell them, even the shepherds, 'The Lord Yahweh says: Woe
   to the shepherds of Israel who feed themselves! Shouldn't the shepherds feed the sheep? You eat
   the fat. You clothe yourself with the wool. You kill the fatlings, but you don't feed the
   sheep.'" — MC: "According to Ezekiel 34:2-3, what were Israel's shepherds condemned for?"
   Options: feeding themselves instead of the sheep / being too young to lead / never speaking to
   the people / living outside the land. Correct: feeding themselves instead of the sheep.

### Boss (3)

8. **Ezek 34:23** (WEB, already-approved verse — **recall/fill-in-blank only, per the medium
   brief's structural rule, never multiple-choice**): "I will set up one shepherd over them, and he
   will feed them, even my servant ___." → **David**. *(Format restriction carried forward — see
   Contested Territory below.)*
9. **Ezek 37:3** (WEB, already-approved verse, new question angle): "He said to me, 'Son of man,
   can these bones live?' I answered, 'Lord Yahweh, you know.'" — MC: "According to Ezekiel 37:3,
   what did Yahweh ask Ezekiel about the bones in the valley?" Options: "Can these bones live?" /
   "Whose bones are these?" / "How did they die?" / "Where did they come from?" Correct: "Can these
   bones live?"
10. **Boss sequence — reused from `medium`'s own Level 3 example**: Order the pivotal passages as
    they appear in the book: call vision → sign-act → watchman → individual responsibility →
    shepherd → dry bones.

## Contested Territory

Everything already flagged in `docs/content/ezekiel.md` applies unchanged. **No new contested
ground for this tier** — every item above is either an exact reuse of an already-approved,
already-bounded verse (new question angle or format only) or a plain narrative-fact MC drawn from
inside an already-scoped verse range.

**Structural rules carried forward, confirmed to hold for this tier:**
- **Ezekiel 16, 23, and 38-39 remain entirely excluded** — no verse, scene description, or content
  reference of any kind appears anywhere in this brief.
- **Ezekiel 34:23-24, "my servant David": recall/fill-in-blank format only, never multiple-choice.**
  Item 8 honors this exactly — it asks the player to complete the verse's own wording, never who
  "my servant David" is or what the figure represents.
- **Ezekiel 37 (dry bones):** item 9 tests only the text's own stated question ("Can these bones
  live?"), not the rabbinic literal-vs-allegory dispute or the vision's meaning — consistent with
  the medium brief's narrative-only bounding, confirmed sufficient in `docs/reviews/ezekiel-review.md`
  §3.4.
- **Ezekiel 18 vs. Exodus 20:5:** item 6 tests only Ezekiel 18:20's own stated content, not the
  harmonization question, consistent with the medium brief's bounding.
- **The sign-act (Ezekiel 4):** item 4 draws only from 4:1, the mild tile-portrait detail — no item
  in this brief touches the 390/40-day symbolic counts or any more intense sign-act material from
  chapters 5, 12, or 24.
- **The temple vision (Ezekiel 40-48) remains out of scope** — no verse from these chapters appears.
