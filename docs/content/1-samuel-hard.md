## Design intent for this tier

Per Kachi's "hard... needs very deep scripture knowledge" framing (see
`docs/content/genesis-hard.md`). This tier draws on verses that are narratively or theologically
significant but less likely to be immediately recognized — exact phrasing, secondary detail, and
precise numbers — and requires typed recall (no multiple-choice scaffolding) wherever the format
allows. It stays inside the same guardrails as every other tier: verbatim WEB text only, narrative
fact only, no doctrinal position required.

## Pivotal Scenes — reused from `docs/content/1-samuel.md`, no new scenes added

Same 5 scenes as `medium` and `easy`: Samuel's Call (1 Sam 3), Israel Demands a King; Saul Anointed
(1 Sam 8, 10), Saul's Disobedience and Rejection; David Anointed (1 Sam 15, 16), David and Goliath
(1 Sam 17), David and Jonathan's Covenant; Saul's Pursuit of David (1 Sam 18, 24). **No item below
draws on 1 Samuel 15:2-3 (the Amalek/ḥerem command) or any other verse from chapter 15** — same
zero-footprint exclusion as the `easy` tier.

## Contested Territory

Everything already flagged in `docs/content/1-samuel.md` applies unchanged, including the exclusion
of 1 Samuel 15:2-3 (not touched anywhere in this tier), the 1 Samuel 8 kingship-request bounding,
the 1 Samuel 16:7 election/foreknowledge bounding, the 1 Samuel 18:1/18:3 recall-only structural
rule (not engaged — this tier doesn't use 18:1 or 18:3), and the 1 Samuel 24 moral-judgment
bounding.

**New for this tier — flagged explicitly, best-effort proposed bounding for the theological
reviewer to check, not silently assumed safe:**

- **1 Sam 10:24, "Long live the king!"** (item 3) sits inside the same kingship-institution ground
  flagged for 8:5/8:7 (`docs/content/1-samuel.md`, `docs/reviews/1-samuel-review.md` §3: kingship
  read by some traditions as a sinful concession, by others as Yahweh's foreknown accommodation).
  The item grades only the crowd's narrated acclamation — never whether their enthusiasm, or
  kingship itself, was rightly placed. **Proposed structural rule: this item stays recall-only,
  never reframed as a multiple-choice question implying the crowd's response was correct or
  incorrect.**
- **1 Sam 16:1 and 16:12** (items 4-5) sit inside the same scene as 16:7, which
  `docs/content/1-samuel.md` already flags for touching divine election/foreknowledge (why David
  was chosen over his older brothers — live Reformed vs. Arminian/other ground) and which
  `docs/reviews/1-samuel-review.md` §3 left as "not independently re-searched in depth... recommend
  a dedicated source check only if a future challenge-authoring pass tries to build an item that
  tests *why* God chose David specifically." This tier is exactly that future pass, so the check is
  due now rather than deferred again. Both items as written grade only narrated action (Yahweh's
  statement about mourning Saul; David's physical appearance and the anointing itself) — neither
  asks *why* David specifically was chosen over his brothers or invites a doctrine of election.
  **Structural rule (confirmed sound and adopted as drafted, per review §3.2): 16:1 and 16:12 stay
  recall-only, and no challenge built from this scene (16:1-13) may ask what criteria God used to
  choose David, or characterize the choice as predestined vs. contingent** — extending the same
  caution 16:7 already carries to its immediate narrative neighbors. **Extended per review §3.2,
  Recommendation 1 [recommended]:** no item in any tier may grade the "handsome face"/"good
  appearance" wording immediately after 16:12's graded word — Revised JPS renders that clause
  "bright-eyed, and handsome" where WEB reads "with a handsome face and good appearance," a genuine
  translation divergence. "Ruddy" (the graded token) is safe; what follows it is not uniformly
  rendered, so it must never itself become a challenge subject.
- **1 Sam 17:4, Goliath's height ("six cubits and a span").** Not a doctrinal or denominational
  dispute, but a genuine, documented textual-tradition variant: the Masoretic Text (which WEB and
  most modern translations follow) reads "six cubits and a span" (roughly 9'9"), while the Dead Sea
  Scrolls manuscript 4QSamuel^a and the Septuagint's Codex Vaticanus read "four cubits and a span"
  (roughly 6'9") — a variant widely discussed in mainstream Old Testament textual criticism, not a
  fringe reading. This is the same category of issue as the אַחַר/אֶחָד manuscript variant flagged
  at Genesis 22:13 in `docs/reviews/genesis-easy-review.md` §4.1. **Structural rule (confirmed sound
  and adopted as drafted, per review §3.3): this item grades only what number WEB's own text states
  (a direct-verse-recall task, in-bounds per `CONTENT_STYLE_GUIDE.md` §2) and must never be framed
  as asserting Goliath's historically certain height, and no future item may ask a player to choose
  between "four cubits" and "six cubits" as if one were simply wrong.** **Extended per review §3.3,
  Recommendation 2 [recommended]:** even within the MT's "six cubits and a span," classic
  commentators disagree on the modern-unit conversion (estimates range from roughly 8'5" to over
  11'), so **no item may grade Goliath's height in feet, metres, or any converted unit — only the
  text's own "six cubits and a span."**
- **1 Sam 17:47 (item 11) — the divine name as a typed graded answer.** See item 11's own note above:
  the original draft made "Yahweh" the typed graded answer, which the review flagged (§6.2) as new
  territory for this project (every prior item only *displays* the name; none had required typing
  it) and offered a clean in-brief fix for rather than settling on its own authority. That fix — move
  the blank to "hand" within the same verse — is applied above. If a future authoring pass ever
  wants to reintroduce "Yahweh" as a typed graded token anywhere in the project, that specific
  decision should go to a human reviewer, per the review's own scoping.
- **No new contested ground otherwise** — 3:1, 3:19, 17:40, and 24:11 are plain narrative
  description or in-text direct quotation with no live cross-tradition disagreement found.
