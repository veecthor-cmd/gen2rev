## Setting / Era

1 Samuel covers Israel's transition from the period of the judges to the united monarchy: the end
of Eli's priestly line at Shiloh, the prophet/judge Samuel's rise, Israel's demand for a king "like
all the nations," Saul's anointing, reign, and disobedience-driven rejection by God, and the
parallel rise of David — anointed in secret while still a shepherd, made famous by defeating
Goliath, bound to Saul's son Jonathan in covenant friendship, and then hunted by an increasingly
paranoid Saul across the Judean wilderness. The book ends with Saul's death in battle against the
Philistines (1 Samuel 31), setting up 2 Samuel's opening: David's kingship.

## Pivotal Scenes (5)

### 1. Samuel's Call (1 Samuel 3)
The boy Samuel, serving in the tabernacle under the aging priest Eli, hears Yahweh calling his
name at night and, after Eli recognizes what is happening, responds.

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- 1 Samuel 3:4-5 (WEB): "Yahweh called Samuel. He said, 'Here I am.' He ran to Eli and said, 'Here
  I am; for you called me.' He said, 'I didn't call. Lie down again.' He went and lay down."
- 1 Samuel 3:9 (WEB): "Therefore Eli said to Samuel, 'Go, lie down. It shall be, if he calls you,
  that you shall say, Speak, Yahweh; for your servant hears.'"
- 1 Samuel 3:10 (WEB): "Yahweh came, and stood, and called as at other times, 'Samuel! Samuel!'
  Then Samuel said, 'Speak; for your servant hears.'"

### 2. Israel Demands a King; Saul Anointed (1 Samuel 8, 10)
The elders of Israel ask Samuel for a king "to judge us like all the nations." Yahweh tells Samuel
the people have not rejected Samuel but have rejected Yahweh as their king; Samuel anoints Saul.

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- 1 Samuel 8:5 (WEB): "They said to him, 'Behold, you are old, and your sons don't walk in your
  ways. Now make us a king to judge us like all the nations.'"
- 1 Samuel 8:7 (WEB): "Yahweh said to Samuel, 'Listen to the voice of the people in all that they
  tell you; for they have not rejected you, but they have rejected me as the king over them.'"
- 1 Samuel 10:1 (WEB): "Then Samuel took the vial of oil and poured it on his head, then kissed him
  and said, 'Hasn't Yahweh anointed you to be prince over his inheritance?'"

### 3. Saul's Disobedience and Rejection; David Anointed (1 Samuel 15, 16)
Saul disobeys Yahweh's command through Samuel and is told obedience matters more than sacrifice;
Yahweh rejects him as king. Samuel is sent to Jesse's household in Bethlehem, where Yahweh chooses
the youngest son, David, to be anointed as Saul's eventual successor.

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- 1 Samuel 15:22 (WEB): "Has Yahweh as great delight in burnt offerings and sacrifices, as in
  obeying Yahweh's voice? Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice, and to listen than the fat of
  rams."
- 1 Samuel 16:7 (WEB): "Don't look on his face, or on the height of his stature, because I have
  rejected him; for I don't see as man sees. For man looks at the outward appearance, but Yahweh
  looks at the heart."
- 1 Samuel 16:13 (WEB): "Then Samuel took the horn of oil and anointed him in the middle of his
  brothers. Then Yahweh's Spirit came mightily on David from that day forward. So Samuel rose up
  and went to Ramah."

### 4. David and Goliath (1 Samuel 17)
The Philistine champion Goliath defies the armies of Israel; the shepherd boy David, sent by his
father to bring food to his brothers at the battle line, volunteers to fight him, refuses Saul's
armor, and kills Goliath with a sling and stone.

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- 1 Samuel 17:45 (WEB): "Then David said to the Philistine, 'You come to me with a sword, with a
  spear, and with a javelin; but I come to you in the name of Yahweh of Armies, the God of the
  armies of Israel, whom you have defied.'"
- 1 Samuel 17:49 (WEB): "David put his hand in his bag, took a stone and slung it, and struck the
  Philistine in his forehead. The stone sank into his forehead, and he fell on his face to the
  earth."
- 1 Samuel 17:50 (WEB): "So David prevailed over the Philistine with a sling and with a stone, and
  struck the Philistine and killed him; but there was no sword in David's hand."

### 5. David and Jonathan's Covenant; Saul's Pursuit of David (1 Samuel 18, 24)
Jonathan, Saul's son, forms a covenant friendship with David after the Goliath victory. As Saul
grows jealous and paranoid, he repeatedly pursues David to kill him; in one encounter, David has
the chance to kill Saul in a cave but spares him instead, and Saul openly acknowledges David's
mercy.

**Challenge-ready verses:**
- 1 Samuel 18:1 (WEB): "When he had finished speaking to Saul, the soul of Jonathan was knit with
  the soul of David, and Jonathan loved him as his own soul."
- 1 Samuel 18:3 (WEB): "Then Jonathan and David made a covenant, because he loved him as his own
  soul."
- 1 Samuel 24:6 (WEB): "He said to his men, 'Yahweh forbid that I should do this thing to my lord,
  Yahweh's anointed, to stretch out my hand against him, since he is Yahweh's anointed.'"
- 1 Samuel 24:17 (WEB): "He said to David, 'You are more righteous than I; for you have done good
  to me, whereas I have done evil to you.'"

## Contested Territory

Per `docs/CONTENT_STYLE_GUIDE.md` §4 — flagged for the theological reviewer, not resolved here:

- **1 Samuel 8, Israel's request for a king.** Some traditions read the text as fundamentally
  anti-monarchic (kingship itself as a concession to Israel's rejection of Yahweh); others read it
  as Yahweh's foreknown accommodation, compatible with the later Davidic covenant (2 Sam. 7)
  affirming kingship as part of the plan. The brief's challenge items test only the narrated
  exchange (the elders' request, Yahweh's statement to Samuel) — never which of these readings of
  "why" is correct.
- **1 Samuel 15, the rejection of Saul and the underlying Amalekite command.** The scene's
  challenge-ready verse (15:22, "to obey is better than sacrifice") is the *consequence* language,
  not the command itself — but the command Saul disobeyed (1 Sam 15:2-3: "utterly destroy all that
  they have... kill both man and woman, infant and nursing baby") is a ḥerem/conquest-command
  passage of the same contested type flagged for Joshua-era material in
  `docs/CONTENT_STYLE_GUIDE.md` §4. **This brief does not select 15:2-3 as a challenge-ready
  verse and does not test any content from it** — the pivotal scene is scoped to Saul's rejection
  and the obedience-over-sacrifice principle only, deliberately avoiding the herem command's
  content. Flagging this exclusion explicitly so the reviewer evaluates it as a deliberate choice.
- **1 Samuel 16:7, "Yahweh looks at the heart."** Touches on divine election/foreknowledge
  (why David was chosen over his older brothers) — a live doctrinal topic (Reformed vs.
  Arminian/other framings of election) in some theological traditions. The challenge item tests
  only the narrated statement and event (Samuel was told not to judge by appearance; David was
  anointed), never a doctrine of election or predestination.
- **1 Samuel 18, David and Jonathan's covenant friendship.** In addition to the traditional reading
  (an ancient Near Eastern political/personal covenant of loyalty between men, common in the text's
  era), some modern scholarship and popular commentary read the relationship's language ("loved
  him as his own soul," Jonathan's stripping of his robe and armor to give to David) as carrying
  romantic or erotic significance. This is a genuine, live interpretive divergence, not a
  fringe reading. **This brief's challenge items describe the relationship only in the text's own
  terms — "covenant," "loved as his own soul" — and never characterize the nature of the
  relationship beyond what is stated**, so no challenge item requires a player to affirm either
  reading. **Structural rule (added per theological review, `docs/reviews/1-samuel-review.md`):
  1 Samuel 18:1 and 18:3 may only be used in recall/fill-in-blank format ("the soul of ___ was
  knit with the soul of David") — never as a multiple-choice item asking players to characterize
  what kind of relationship or love this was** — so the boundary can't be crossed by a future
  challenge-authoring pass that doesn't re-read this note.
- **1 Samuel 24, David sparing Saul's life.** Saul's own statement (24:17) that David was "more
  righteous" than he is used as a challenge-ready verse — this is the text's own narrated
  quotation, not the game asking the player to render a moral verdict. No challenge item asks
  whether David was right to spare Saul or whether Saul deserved judgment; it tests only what Saul
  is recorded as having said.
