## Priority item — 2 Chronicles 29:10, "fierce anger" vs. "fierce wrath"

**This item gets its own verification, independent of the drafter's fetch and independent of the
theological review's own four-source check, per the assignment's explicit instruction not to simply
trust the review's stated resolution.**

The brief's item 4 displays: *"Now it is in my heart to make a covenant with Yahweh, the God of
Israel, that his fierce **anger** may turn away from us."* — with the graded recall blank on
**"anger."**

| Source | Reading returned |
|---|---|
| [BibleGateway, WEB](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2+Chronicles+29%3A5%2C+2+Chronicles+29%3A10%2C+2+Chronicles+29%3A18&version=WEB) | "that his fierce **anger** may turn away from us" |
| [ebible.org `/web/2CH29.htm`](https://ebible.org/web/2CH29.htm) | "fierce" confirmed as the modifier of "anger" in "his fierce anger," consistent with the full clause |

**Both independently-run fetches for this QA pass returned "anger," zero for "wrath."** This is
consistent with, but independently obtained from, the theological review's own four-source check
(`docs/reviews/2-chronicles-hard-review.md` §1: BibleGateway, Bible Hub, `ebible.org/web`, and
`ebible.org/engwebp` all returned "anger," 4-for-4). Combined with this QA pass's two additional,
independently-run fetches, the WEB reading "fierce anger" is now confirmed across a total of at least
four independent source fetches by two different sessions (the review's and this one), with no fetch,
by anyone, at any point in this process, ever returning "wrath" from a source correctly identified as
WEB.

**On the origin of the discrepancy:** the review traced the drafter's original bad "wrath" fetch to
translation bleed from KJV/Webster's, which do read "fierce wrath" at this verse — a real English
rendering, just not WEB's. This QA pass did not need to re-investigate that root cause, since the
live-source verification above already independently confirms the WEB text itself is unambiguous and
stable at "anger."

**Verdict for this item: `pass`, no discrepancy.** The brief's item 4 uses the confirmed correct WEB
reading. The header's discrepancy note and Contested Territory's Finding-G decision record (keeping
"anger" as the graded token, deliberately, as hard-tier difficulty, with the answer-normalizer
expected to reject "wrath" as a different translation's word) are both consistent with this
independent finding and require no further change.
